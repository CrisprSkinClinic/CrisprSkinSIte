Parents often notice a dark band at the back of a child's neck and assume it is dirt. In many children it is acanthosis nigricans.

## Why It Happens

As in adults, it reflects high insulin levels. It is more common in children who are above their ideal weight, have a family history of diabetes, or are entering puberty, when insulin resistance naturally rises.

## What Parents Should Do

Do not scrub. Ask your paediatrician or dermatologist to check blood sugar, and consider cholesterol and thyroid. Early identification of prediabetes in childhood lets you turn it around.

## What Helps

- Regular physical activity, ideally daily play or sport.
- Fewer sugary drinks and packaged snacks, and balanced meals the whole family eats.
- Good sleep and limited screen time.
- Gentle moisturiser and, if advised, a mild softening cream.

## Be Sensitive

Children may feel self-conscious. Reassure them it is a body signal, not a hygiene issue, and focus on healthy habits for everyone, not on weight.
