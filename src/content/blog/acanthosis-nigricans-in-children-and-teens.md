Parents often notice a dark band at the back of a child's neck and assume it is dirt. In many children it is acanthosis nigricans.

## Why It Happens

As in adults, it reflects high insulin levels. It is more common in children who are above their ideal weight, have a family history of diabetes, or are entering puberty, when insulin resistance naturally rises.

## What Parents Should Do

Do not scrub. Ask your paediatrician or dermatologist to check blood sugar, and consider cholesterol and thyroid. Early identification of prediabetes in childhood lets you turn it around.

## What Helps

- Regular physical activity, ideally daily play or sport.
- Fewer sugary drinks and packaged snacks, and balanced meals the whole family eats.
- Good sleep and limited screen time.
- Gentle moisturiser and, if advised, a mild softening cream.

## Be Sensitive

Children may feel self-conscious. Reassure them it is a body signal, not a hygiene issue, and focus on healthy habits for everyone, not on weight.

## Why It Is Increasing

Childhood obesity, less physical activity and diets high in sugary drinks and processed foods have raised insulin resistance in children and adolescents. Puberty itself makes cells slightly more insulin-resistant, so AN often appears in the early teens. A family history of type 2 diabetes increases the risk, and in South Asian children, insulin resistance occurs at lower body weights than in some other groups.

## What Doctors Check

- Height, weight and body mass index plotted on growth charts.
- Blood pressure.
- Waist circumference.
- Fasting glucose or HbA1c, and a lipid profile.
- In girls: periods, acne and hair growth, to look for PCOS.
- Sleep, activity and diet history.

## Practical Family-Based Strategies

Studies find that changes work best when the whole household participates.

- **Drinks:** water or milk instead of sweetened beverages and packaged juice.
- **Snacks:** fruit, nuts or roasted chickpeas instead of chips and biscuits.
- **Meals:** regular times, plenty of vegetables and pulses, smaller portions of fried foods.
- **Activity:** at least an hour a day of active play or sport. Limit screen time and sit less.
- **Sleep:** adequate sleep supports healthy hormones. Aim for eight to ten hours for teenagers.

## Treating the Skin

Gentle softening creams, such as urea, and sun protection can help. Avoid harsh scrubbing or adult bleaching creams. Retinoids and peels are used cautiously in older teenagers with supervision.

## Emotional Wellbeing

Children and teens with AN may be teased about a "dirty" neck. Explaining that it reflects hormones and insulin, not cleanliness, and that it can improve, makes a big difference. Avoid weight-based criticism, and focus on healthy habits.

## When to Seek Early Help

Rapid weight gain, increased thirst or urination, tiredness, very irregular periods, or severe acne and excess hair in a teenage girl should prompt a check.
