Acanthosis nigricans is often the skin's early signal that the body is making a lot of insulin to keep blood sugar normal. Testing early lets you act before diabetes develops.

## Tests Doctors Commonly Use

- **Fasting blood glucose and HbA1c**, to look for prediabetes or diabetes.
- **Lipid profile**, since cholesterol and triglycerides are often raised with insulin resistance.
- **Fasting insulin**, which may help in some situations, though results vary.
- **Thyroid tests**, which can contribute to weight gain and skin changes.
- **A PCOS work-up** in women with irregular periods, acne or excess hair.

Not everyone needs every test, and your doctor will choose what suits you.

## What the Results Mean

Normal sugar with acanthosis nigricans still suggests insulin resistance in many people, so repeat testing once a year makes sense if the patches persist or your weight rises.

## What You Can Do

Gradual weight loss, regular activity, less sugar and refined carbohydrate, and a balanced diet improve insulin sensitivity and often lighten the skin. Medicines such as metformin may be advised by your physician.

## Interpreting Test Results

**Fasting blood glucose:** below 100 mg/dL is normal. 100 to 125 indicates impaired fasting glucose. 126 or higher on repeat testing suggests diabetes.

**HbA1c:** below 5.7 percent is normal, 5.7 to 6.4 percent suggests prediabetes, and 6.5 or above suggests diabetes. Indian guidelines sometimes use slightly different cut-offs, and your doctor will apply what suits you.

**Oral glucose tolerance test:** useful when fasting tests are borderline.

**Fasting insulin and HOMA-IR:** these estimate insulin resistance, but cut-offs vary by laboratory and population, so we interpret them with caution.

**Lipid profile:** high triglycerides and low HDL cholesterol commonly accompany insulin resistance.

**Thyroid tests (TSH):** an underactive thyroid can contribute to weight gain and AN.

**Liver tests and ultrasound:** fatty liver is common with insulin resistance. A physician may advise further assessment.

## For Women With PCOS Features

Tests may include hormone levels, such as testosterone and DHEAS, and a pelvic ultrasound, coordinated with your gynaecologist. See [PCOS and your skin](/blog/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs-checklist).

## What to Do With the Results

If results are normal, repeat annually, since insulin resistance can precede abnormal sugar by years. If you have prediabetes, a structured lifestyle programme can reduce progression to diabetes by a large margin. Your physician may suggest metformin in selected cases. If diabetes is found, early treatment protects your eyes, kidneys and nerves.

## Lifestyle Changes With Evidence

- Losing five to ten percent of body weight when overweight.
- At least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week, plus strength training.
- Fewer sugary drinks and refined carbohydrates, more pulses, vegetables and whole grains.
- Adequate sleep and stress management.
- Quitting smoking.

## When Not to Wait

See a doctor urgently for rapidly spreading AN, patches in the mouth or palms, unexplained weight loss, persistent stomach symptoms or loss of appetite, as these can signal an underlying cause other than insulin resistance.
