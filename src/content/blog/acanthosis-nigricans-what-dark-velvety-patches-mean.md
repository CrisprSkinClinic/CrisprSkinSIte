If the back of your neck or your armpits have become dark, thick and velvety, and no amount of scrubbing helps, you are very likely looking at acanthosis nigricans.

## What It Is

High insulin levels act on skin cells and make them multiply and thicken, producing a dark, velvety patch. It tends to appear on the neck, armpits, groin, knuckles and under the breasts. Some people also have small skin tags in the same areas.

## It Is Not Dirt

Rubbing and scrubbing irritate the skin and can darken it further. The colour is within the skin, and it is a sign from inside the body rather than a hygiene problem.

## What It Often Points To

Insulin resistance is the main link, which may come with excess weight, PCOS or prediabetes. Many people have it for years before blood sugar rises, so it is a useful early warning. Read [which tests we suggest](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-insulin-resistance-and-tests).

## When It Is Something Else

Rarely, patches that appear suddenly, spread fast, involve the lips or palms, or come with weight loss need prompt evaluation for another cause.

## What Helps

Treating the cause comes first, with creams and procedures to lighten the skin. ## How Doctors Recognise It

Acanthosis nigricans (AN) has a distinctive look: skin that is thickened, darker and velvety, often with a slightly warty or "dirty" appearance, typically in the neck, armpits and groin. The dark band across the back of the neck is the most familiar. Other sites include the knuckles, elbows, under the breasts and around the navel. Patches develop slowly, over months or years, and are rarely itchy.

## Causes in More Detail

**Insulin resistance** is by far the commonest cause. When cells respond poorly to insulin, the pancreas makes more of it. High insulin levels activate receptors on skin cells that stimulate growth.

**Conditions linked to insulin resistance:** obesity, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, PCOS and metabolic syndrome.

**Hormonal conditions:** underactive thyroid, Cushing syndrome, and growth hormone excess.

**Medicines:** nicotinic acid in high doses, some hormonal treatments including certain contraceptives, systemic steroids and some others.

**Inherited forms:** rare families have AN without an obvious cause.

**Malignancy-associated AN:** very rarely, a cancer, usually of the stomach or another internal organ, triggers a sudden, extensive form, often involving the palms, lips and mouth, and accompanied by weight loss.

## Look-Alikes

Not every dark neck is AN. Other possibilities include friction darkening, tinea versicolor, post-inflammatory marks from irritation, and a condition called confluent and reticulated papillomatosis. A dermatologist can usually tell by examination. See [post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation](/conditions/post-inflammatory-hyperpigmentation).

## What We Ask and Check

We ask about weight changes, periods, family history of diabetes and medicines. We examine the skin, measure waist and blood pressure where relevant, and suggest blood tests when appropriate. Early detection of prediabetes lets you reverse it through lifestyle change.

## What Not to Do

Do not scrub, use pumice stones or use harsh bleaching creams, which irritate and darken skin. Avoid unsupervised steroid creams. Skip fad "detox" products, which do not address the cause.

## Is It Dangerous?

AN itself is harmless. Its importance lies in what it tells you about your metabolism, and a small chance of rare underlying causes. Treat it as a useful early warning rather than a cosmetic problem alone.
