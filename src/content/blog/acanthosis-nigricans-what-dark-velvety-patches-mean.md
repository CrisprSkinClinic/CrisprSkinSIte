If the back of your neck or your armpits have become dark, thick and velvety, and no amount of scrubbing helps, you are very likely looking at acanthosis nigricans.

## What It Is

High insulin levels act on skin cells and make them multiply and thicken, producing a dark, velvety patch. It tends to appear on the neck, armpits, groin, knuckles and under the breasts. Some people also have small skin tags in the same areas.

## It Is Not Dirt

Rubbing and scrubbing irritate the skin and can darken it further. The colour is within the skin, and it is a sign from inside the body rather than a hygiene problem.

## What It Often Points To

Insulin resistance is the main link, which may come with excess weight, PCOS or prediabetes. Many people have it for years before blood sugar rises, so it is a useful early warning. Read [which tests we suggest](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-insulin-resistance-and-tests).

## When It Is Something Else

Rarely, patches that appear suddenly, spread fast, involve the lips or palms, or come with weight loss need prompt evaluation for another cause.

## What Helps

Treating the cause comes first, with creams and procedures to lighten the skin.
