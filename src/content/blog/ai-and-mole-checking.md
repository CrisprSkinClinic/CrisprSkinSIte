Apps that claim to assess a mole from a photograph are increasingly common, and artificial intelligence is being used in dermatology research and in clinics. It is worth understanding both the promise and the limits.

## What the Technology Can Do

In studies, some AI systems can help flag suspicious lesions, particularly when used alongside dermoscopy, the magnified examination doctors use. They may help clinicians as a second opinion.

## The Limits for Consumers

A phone photo is a poor substitute for examination. Lighting, image quality, skin tone, and body location all affect accuracy, and many tools have been trained on limited types of skin. A reassuring result from an app can lead to dangerous delay.

## What Matters More

Knowing the warning signs, checking your own skin regularly, and having a doctor examine anything that is changing, new, or different from your other moles matters more than any app.

## When to Get a Mole Checked

Any mole that is changing in size, shape, or colour, bleeds, itches persistently, or looks unlike the others deserves an examination. See our ABCDE guide for the warning signs.

## A Sensible Use

Photos taken at regular intervals can help you notice change over time, and you can bring them to your visit. Treat any app as a prompt to see a doctor, not a replacement.
