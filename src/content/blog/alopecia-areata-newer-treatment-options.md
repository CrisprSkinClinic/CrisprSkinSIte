For a long time, treatment for alopecia areata relied on a small set of options with variable results. Recent years have brought more choices, particularly for severe disease.

## What Has Changed

Medicines that block specific signals inside immune cells, known as [JAK inhibitors](/blog/jak-inhibitors-safety-and-monitoring), have shown meaningful hair regrowth in some people with extensive alopecia areata in clinical studies. They are used for selected patients, and which medicines are approved or available differs between countries.

## What Has Not Changed

Many people with patchy [alopecia areata](/services/alopecia-areata) still do well with established treatments such as topical and injected corticosteroids and other topical therapy. Hair regrowth can also happen on its own. Newer medicines are generally reserved for more extensive or persistent disease.

## Things to Weigh Up

These medicines need pre-treatment assessment, monitoring, and a realistic conversation about risks. Hair loss may return if treatment is stopped, so the plan is a long-term one.

## Support Matters Too

[Alopecia areata can be distressing](/blog/living-with-alopecia-areata), and emotional support, honest expectations, and practical options such as camouflage are part of good care.

## What to Do

If your hair loss is extensive, recurring, or not responding to treatment, come in for an evaluation. We can discuss which options are appropriate for you and which are not.

## What Treatment Choice Depends On

Treatment is shaped by how much hair is lost, how long it has been going on, your age and your general health. In a child with one small patch, we may simply watch or use a mild treatment. In an adult with extensive loss, we discuss stronger options. Disease can also be unpredictable, with regrowth and new patches both possible.

## Regrowth Takes Time

Even when treatment works, new hair takes months to appear, and early regrowth may be fine and pale before it darkens. Judge response after several months rather than weeks. Not every treatment works for every person, and if one fails, others may be tried.

## Looking After Your Scalp and Mind

Gentle scalp care, sun protection on exposed patches and wigs or hair fibres, if wanted, can make daily life easier. Sleep, stress and other autoimmune conditions such as thyroid problems can matter, so we ask about them. Support from family, friends or others with the condition helps many people.

## Children and Teenagers

Alopecia areata in young people calls for a gentle, supportive approach that considers school life and confidence. We choose treatments with care and involve parents in decisions.
