Athlete's foot, or tinea pedis, is a fungal infection of the feet. It affects people who never play sport, and is one of the commonest reasons for itchy, peeling or smelly feet in a humid climate.

## Three Common Patterns

**Between the toes (interdigital).** Peeling, soggy, white or cracked skin, mostly between the fourth and fifth toes, with itching and sometimes odour. This type can become infected with bacteria.

**Moccasin type.** Dry, scaly, thickened skin covering the sole and sides of the foot like a slipper, often mistaken for ordinary dry skin or cracked heels. Often one hand is also involved. See [cracked heels](/conditions/cracked-heels).

**Blistering (vesicular).** Itchy small blisters on the arch and sole that burst and peel.

## What Else Can Look Like This

Eczema, psoriasis, contact allergy from footwear and bacterial infection can all mimic it. If creams have not worked, or both feet are red and itchy, ask for an examination and a scraping test.

## Treatment

- **Antifungal cream or powder,** applied to the whole foot and between the toes as directed, usually for several weeks, even after the skin looks normal.
- **Tablets** for the moccasin type, for severe or widespread disease, and for infections that keep returning, chosen by your doctor.
- **Care of the skin,** such as drying well between the toes.
- **Treating nails.** Infected toenails are a reservoir that reinfects the feet. See [nail fungus](/conditions/onychomycosis).

Steroid or combination creams should be avoided unless prescribed for a particular reason. See [steroid creams and tinea](/blog/steroid-creams-and-tinea-why-they-make-it-worse).

## Prevention

- Wash feet daily and dry them thoroughly, especially between the toes.
- Change socks daily, and more often if sweaty. Cotton or moisture-wicking socks are better than nylon.
- Alternate shoes so each pair dries completely for 24 hours, and dry them in sunlight.
- Wear sandals in shared bathrooms, gyms and pools.
- Do not share towels, socks or footwear.
- Put on socks before underwear to avoid spreading the fungus to the groin. See [jock itch](/blog/jock-itch-tinea-cruris-treatment-and-prevention).
- Use an antifungal powder in shoes if advised.

## People Who Need Extra Care

If you have diabetes, poor circulation or reduced feeling in your feet, even a small crack between the toes can lead to a serious infection. Check your feet daily and see a doctor early for any crack, blister or redness. People on immune-lowering medicines should also seek advice promptly.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if there is swelling, pus, spreading redness, fever, a foul smell, or if the problem returns after treatment. Read more on [tinea infections](/conditions/tinea-infections) and our [fungal infection service](/services/fungal-infections).
