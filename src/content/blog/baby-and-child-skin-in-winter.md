Children's skin has a thinner barrier than adults', so it loses moisture quickly and reacts faster in dry weather.

## Bathing

Short, lukewarm baths, once a day or less if the skin is dry. Use a mild, fragrance-free wash only where needed, and pat dry gently.

## Moisturise After Every Bath

Apply a plain, thick moisturiser within three minutes of the bath, and again later in the day on dry areas. Ointments and creams work better than thin lotions.

## Dress Comfortably

Layers of soft cotton are better than a single thick woollen layer next to the skin. Wool and synthetic fibres can irritate. Do not overdress babies, as sweating also irritates.

## Lips and Cheeks

Dry cheeks and lips are common. A plain petrolatum-based balm helps. Avoid licking the lips, which makes them worse.

## Watch for Eczema

Itchy, red, dry patches in the creases of elbows and knees, on cheeks or behind the ears may be eczema. See our guide to [managing childhood eczema](/blog/managing-childhood-eczema) and [common childhood skin problems](/blog/common-childhood-skin-conditions).

## When to Book a Visit

If skin is weeping, crusted or your child is scratching at night, our [paediatric dermatology](/services/pediatric-dermatology) service can help. See also our [winter skin care](/services/winter-skin-care) page.

## Why Babies Are More Prone

A baby's skin is thinner and loses water faster. The oil glands are still maturing, and the skin barrier is not fully developed. This makes babies and toddlers susceptible to dryness, cradle cap, and eczema flares when humidity drops or when they are bathed too often.

## Bathing Guide

- **Frequency:** every day or every second day is fine. More often is not necessary in cool weather.
- **Duration:** five to ten minutes, lukewarm water.
- **Cleansers:** soap-free, fragrance-free, and only where needed.
- **After the bath:** pat dry and moisturise within three minutes.

## Oils, Massage and Home Remedies

Massage with a plain oil, such as coconut or a refined vegetable oil, is traditional and generally fine for most babies. Do not use strong-smelling, medicated or mustard oil on newborn skin, which can irritate it. Avoid applying kajal, powders or turmeric pastes on broken skin. If your baby has eczema, ask which oils your doctor prefers, since some can worsen it.

## Clothing and Bedding

Cotton next to the skin, soft layers that can be removed, and avoiding overheating. Wool and synthetic blankets should not touch the baby's skin directly. Wash clothes in a mild detergent and rinse well.

## Common Winter Skin Problems

**Dry patches and cracked cheeks:** use a thick moisturiser or ointment, especially before going outside.

**Cradle cap:** yellow scaly patches on the scalp. Soften with oil, then gentle shampoo. It usually settles by itself.

**Chapped lips and mouth corners:** petroleum jelly. Drooling and teething can add to it.

**Diaper area dryness:** change often, use a barrier cream. See [diaper dermatitis](/conditions/diaper-dermatitis).

**Eczema:** itchy, red patches. See [managing childhood eczema](/blog/managing-childhood-eczema).

## Older Children

School-age children get dry hands, lips and shins. A small moisturiser tube in the school bag helps. Sun protection is still needed on sunny days.

## Warning Signs

Fever with a rash, blisters, pus-like discharge, rapid spreading, or a baby who feeds poorly should be seen urgently. Itch that disturbs sleep for more than a week deserves an evaluation. See our [paediatric dermatology](/services/pediatric-dermatology) service.
