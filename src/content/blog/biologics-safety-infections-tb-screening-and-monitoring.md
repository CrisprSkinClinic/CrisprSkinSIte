Biologics are targeted medicines, and most people tolerate them well. Because they affect the immune system, screening and monitoring are part of safe use.

## Why Screening Matters

Some biologics can allow dormant infections to flare. Before starting we check for:

- **Tuberculosis (TB),** with a blood test (and sometimes a skin test) and a chest X-ray. If you have latent TB, treatment is given first or alongside.
- **Hepatitis B and C,** which can reactivate, with blood tests and, if needed, antiviral cover.
- **HIV,** where relevant.
- **Other active infections,** which should be treated before starting.

## Infections to Watch For

- Colds and sore throats may be more frequent.
- Skin infections, urinary infections and shingles.
- Persistent cough, fever, night sweats or weight loss, which may signal TB.
- Any infection that does not settle in a few days.

Tell us early. We may pause the biologic during a significant infection and restart when you are well.

## Other Safety Points

- **Injection-site reactions** and headache are common and mild.
- **Allergic reactions** are rare.
- **Heart failure, multiple sclerosis and cancers:** your history is reviewed, and some medicines are avoided in certain conditions.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** planned with your doctor, since some biologics are preferred.
- **Vaccines:** live vaccines are avoided, and inactivated vaccines are given beforehand where possible. See our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination).

## Monitoring During Treatment

Regular reviews, usually every three months at first, then less often. Blood tests are done as required for the medicine, and TB screening may be repeated yearly in some people. You are told what to watch and whom to call.

## Questions to Ask

What exactly are you screening for and why? What infections should I report? What happens if I get an infection? How are vaccines planned? What if I want to become pregnant?

## The Balance

For people with moderate to severe disease, the benefits usually outweigh the risks, and monitoring keeps risks low.
