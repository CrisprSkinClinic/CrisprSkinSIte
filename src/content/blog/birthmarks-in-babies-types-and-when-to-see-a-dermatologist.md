Most birthmarks are harmless, but a few need prompt attention. Knowing the main types helps parents decide when to wait and when to ask.

## Vascular (Red or Pink) Birthmarks

- **Salmon patches ("stork bites"):** flat, pink patches on the eyelids, forehead or back of the neck, very common. Those on the face fade in the first years, while those on the neck often persist but are hidden by hair.
- **Infantile haemangioma ("strawberry mark"):** appears in the first weeks, grows for months, then shrinks. See [infantile haemangioma](/conditions/infantile-haemangioma).
- **Port-wine stains:** flat, deep pink to purple patches present from birth that do not fade. See [port-wine stains](/blog/port-wine-stains-and-haemangiomas-treatment-and-timing).

## Pigmented (Brown or Blue) Birthmarks

- **Congenital moles (naevi):** brown, sometimes hairy. See [congenital melanocytic naevus](/conditions/congenital-melanocytic-naevus).
- **Café-au-lait spots:** flat, light brown patches. One or two are common, but six or more, or freckling in the armpits and groin, should be checked.
- **Mongolian spots:** blue-grey patches on the lower back and buttocks, very common in South Asian babies, usually fading by school age.

## Signs to Show a Doctor Soon

- A red mark that is **growing quickly** or near the eye, nose, lips or ears.
- **Open sores** in a birthmark.
- A **large mole** or a mole with a lump.
- Several café-au-lait spots or other skin findings.
- A port-wine stain on the **forehead or around the eye,** which needs an eye and neurological review.
- Any mark that **changes or bleeds.**

## What Parents Can Do

Photograph the mark every week or month, protect it from the sun, avoid scratching and do not try home remedies. Show it to your paediatrician or a dermatologist at the first routine visit.

## Treatment

Many need no treatment. Others respond to medicine (such as propranolol for haemangioma) or laser (for port-wine stains), best started early.
