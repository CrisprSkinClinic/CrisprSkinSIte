Botox is one of the most popular cosmetic treatments, and also one of the most misunderstood. A first visit should feel unhurried, and the result should look like you, only rested.

## Before You Go

Think about what bothers you: frown lines, forehead lines, crow's feet, or none of these. Tell the doctor your medical history, medicines (especially blood thinners), allergies, and whether you are pregnant or breastfeeding, since Botox is not used then. Avoid alcohol and anti-inflammatory painkillers for a day or two to lower bruising.

## At the Visit

1. **Assessment:** the doctor studies your face at rest and when frowning, raising your brows and smiling.
2. **Plan:** which muscles, how many units and where. A conservative first dose is wise.
3. **Treatment:** tiny injections with a fine needle, taking about ten minutes. It feels like small pinpricks.
4. **Aftercare:** avoid rubbing, lying flat and intense exercise for several hours.

## What You Will Notice

The effect starts in three to five days, peaks at about two weeks, and lasts three to four months. Lines soften, and with repeated treatment, may become less deep. A review at two weeks lets the doctor adjust if needed.

## Natural Results

The goal is to soften lines while leaving expression. Too high a dose or poor placement gives a frozen or heavy look. See [Botox without looking "done"](/blog/botox-natural-results).

## Side Effects

Small bruises and a mild headache are common. Occasionally one brow looks different, or an eyelid droops for some weeks, and settles. Serious problems are rare in trained hands.

## How to Choose a Doctor

- A qualified doctor, ideally a dermatologist, who assesses you properly.
- Genuine, branded product, shown to you.
- A willingness to say no, and to offer a conservative plan.
- Clear information on risks and aftercare.
- Beware of very low prices and "party" injections.

## How Often?

Most people repeat every three to four months, or less often as time passes.
