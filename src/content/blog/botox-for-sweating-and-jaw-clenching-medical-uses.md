Botulinum toxin is better known for lines, but it started as a medical treatment and still has important medical uses.

## Excessive Sweating (Hyperhidrosis)

Hyperhidrosis is sweating beyond what the body needs, often in the underarms, palms, soles or face. It can be embarrassing, wet clothes and make handshakes or writing difficult.

**How Botox helps.** Injected into the skin, it blocks the nerve signal to the sweat glands, so they sweat much less.

**Treatment.** In the underarm, small injections are given in a grid, and a starch-iodine test may show the sweaty area. It takes about fifteen minutes. Palms are more painful, so numbing is used.

**Results.** Sweating falls within a week or two, and the effect lasts around four to eight months, sometimes longer. It can be repeated.

**First steps.** Strong antiperspirants with aluminium chloride are tried first. Botox is for those who still struggle. Sudden or night sweating with other symptoms should be checked for medical causes.

## Jaw Clenching and Masseter Slimming

Botox relaxes the masseter, the jaw muscle used for chewing. It can reduce pain from teeth grinding (bruxism), headaches from clenching and, over months, soften a square jawline when the muscle is large.

**Points.** Doses are higher, and results take two to four weeks. Chewing hard foods may feel weaker for a short time. It should be assessed carefully, since overtreatment can alter the face shape. Your dentist should be told about teeth grinding.

## Other Uses

Migraine prevention, muscle spasm and drooling are treated medically elsewhere, by specialists.

## Risks

Bruising and soreness at the injection sites. In the hands, temporary weakness of grip can occur. In the jaw, chewing weakness or a smile change is rare. A trained doctor lowers these risks.

## Who Should Avoid It

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and some nerve and muscle diseases. Tell us about your medicines.
