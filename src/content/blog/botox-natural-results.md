The most common concern we hear before a first Botox treatment isn't about safety -- it's "will I look frozen." It's a reasonable worry, and understanding where that look actually comes from helps explain why it isn't an inevitable outcome.

## It's a Dosing Decision, Not a Property of the Drug

[Botox](/services/botox) relaxes the specific facial muscles responsible for expression lines. How much movement is preserved afterward is a direct function of dose and placement, not something built into the treatment itself. A conservative dose softens lines while allowing natural expression to remain; a heavier dose produces a flatter, more uniform look.

## Our Default Is Conservative

We favor starting conservatively, particularly for anyone new to Botox, aiming to soften expression lines while preserving natural movement -- rather than defaulting to a stronger, more uniform result. If you want a more complete smoothing effect, that's a conversation we have directly rather than assuming it's what everyone wants.

## The Areas Where Technique Matters Most

The forehead and areas around the eyes are where dosing and placement decisions have the most visible impact on whether the result looks natural or overdone. This is where experience and a conservative starting approach make the most practical difference.

## What to Expect Timing-Wise

Results build over the days following treatment and typically last a few months before gradually fading, which is why many patients choose periodic maintenance sessions rather than one-off treatment. This gives you and your provider ongoing opportunities to fine-tune dosing based on how previous sessions actually looked on you.

## The Bottom Line

A natural-looking result isn't a matter of luck -- it's a deliberate dosing choice, discussed with you at your consultation rather than applied as a fixed, generic protocol. ## What We Discuss Before Treatment

We look at how your face moves, which lines bother you and which ones you would rather keep, since some movement gives expression and character. We discuss goals such as softening the frown without lowering the brow, and check your medical history, medicines and any previous treatments. Photographs at rest and in expression help plan and compare.

## Aftercare in the First Day or Two

Avoid pressing or rubbing the treated areas, and avoid lying flat or vigorous exercise for the period we advise. Small bumps at the injection points settle within hours, and mild bruises can occur. Tell us if you notice drooping or asymmetry, which can often be corrected and tends to settle as the effect wears off.

## Who Should Wait

Botox is not advised in pregnancy or breastfeeding, and certain nerve and muscle conditions or medicines mean it may not be suitable. If you have an infection at the treatment site, we also postpone. Honest answers about your health keep treatment safe.

## Touch-Ups and Maintenance

If the first dose is slightly too light, a small top-up after about two weeks is a safer way to reach the right result than starting heavy. Some people then treat every few months, and others space treatments further apart.
