Most problems after a chemical peel come from what happens in the following week, not from the procedure itself. A few simple habits make a large difference to both comfort and results.

## Expect Some Peeling and Dryness

Mild redness, tightness, and flaking are normal and settle over several days, depending on peel depth. Let skin shed on its own rather than picking or pulling at it, which raises the risk of marks and scarring.

## Moisturize Gently and Protect From the Sun

Use a bland moisturizer and a gentle cleanser. Daily broad-spectrum [sunscreen](/blog/sunscreen-and-visible-light-indian-skin) is essential, because freshly peeled skin is more vulnerable to [sun-triggered pigmentation](/conditions/post-inflammatory-hyperpigmentation), particularly in deeper skin tones.

## Pause Active Products

Hold off on [retinoids](/blog/retinol-vs-retinoids-how-to-start), exfoliating acids, and scrubs until we advise you to restart them. Combining them with healing skin invites irritation.

## When to Call Us

Contact the clinic if you notice increasing pain, blistering, crusting, or signs of infection. Most peels heal uneventfully, and following aftercare closely is the best way to keep it that way.
