Downtime depends on the strength of the peel. Superficial peels have almost none, and medium peels need a few days. Knowing the timeline lets you plan.

## During the Peel

The skin is cleaned, the acid is applied for a set time, and you feel tingling or stinging, sometimes warmth. A fan or cool air helps. The peel is then neutralised or washed off, depending on the acid, and a soothing cream is applied. The visit takes about 30 minutes.

## Superficial Peel (Salicylic, Glycolic, Mandelic, Lactic)

- **Day 0:** pink skin and a mild tight feeling.
- **Day 1 to 2:** tightness, dryness and slight flaking.
- **Day 3 to 5:** fine flaking or peeling in sheets in some people, or only mild dryness.
- **By day 5 to 7:** skin looks fresh and smooth.

Many people go back to work the same day.

## Medium Peel (TCA, Deeper Combination Peels)

- **Day 0:** redness and a feeling like a sunburn. A white frost may appear during the peel.
- **Day 1 to 2:** darkening and tightness, swelling in some.
- **Day 3 to 7:** the skin peels, often in large flakes.
- **Day 7 to 10:** new pink skin appears.
- **Week 2 to 4:** the pinkness fades.

Plan about a week away from social events.

## Aftercare for All Peels

1. **Do not pick or pull peeling skin.** Let it come off by itself, or trim loose skin with clean scissors. Picking causes marks and scars.
2. **Moisturise** with a plain, gentle cream.
3. **Use a mild cleanser** and lukewarm water.
4. **Protect from the sun.** Use sunscreen daily, wear a hat, and avoid direct sun. This is the single most important step.
5. **Avoid actives** such as retinoids, strong acids, scrubs and exfoliating devices until your doctor says to restart.
6. **Avoid** waxing, threading, saunas, swimming pools and heavy exercise for several days.
7. **Makeup:** mineral makeup after the first day or two for superficial peels, and after peeling settles for medium peels.

## What Is Normal

Redness, tightness, dryness and peeling. Mild itch. Slight darkening of the skin before it sheds.

## What Is Not Normal

- Blisters or weeping.
- Severe pain or burning that does not ease.
- Spreading redness, pus or fever.
- Dark patches or crusting that worsens.
- Cold sore blisters.

Contact the clinic promptly if any of these occur.

## How Often Can You Peel?

Superficial peels are repeated every two to four weeks in a series of four to six. Medium peels are spaced months apart.

## Do Not Peel If

You are sunburnt, have active acne with infection, an open wound, cold sores, or are pregnant (for some acids), or recently took isotretinoin. See [chemical peel types](/blog/chemical-peel-types-glycolic-salicylic-tca-explained) and our [chemical peels service](/services/chemical-peels).
