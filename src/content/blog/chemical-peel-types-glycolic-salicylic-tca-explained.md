A chemical peel uses a controlled acid to remove the outer layers of skin and prompt renewal. The type, strength and number of coats are chosen for your skin and goal. Here is how the common peels differ.

## Depth Matters

- **Superficial peels** work on the top layer, with little to no downtime. Most cosmetic peels in clinic are of this kind.
- **Medium-depth peels** reach deeper, with a few days of peeling.
- **Deep peels** are surgical-level treatments rarely used for cosmetic skin care in this setting.

## Glycolic Acid

**Type.** An alpha hydroxy acid from sugar cane, small and quick to penetrate.
**Used for.** Dullness, fine lines, mild pigmentation, rough texture and mild acne marks.
**Points.** Strengths vary, usually 20 to 70 percent in clinic. It can irritate and cause pigment change in darker skin if too strong or left on too long.

## Salicylic Acid

**Type.** A beta hydroxy acid that dissolves oil, so it goes into pores.
**Used for.** Acne, blackheads, oily skin and some mild marks.
**Points.** Usually 20 to 30 percent. Suits acne-prone skin well, and often less irritating for brown skin. Not used in large amounts in pregnancy.

## Mandelic Acid

**Type.** A larger alpha hydroxy acid that penetrates slowly.
**Used for.** Sensitive skin, mild pigmentation, acne and as a gentle first peel in darker skin.

## Lactic Acid

**Type.** A mild alpha hydroxy acid that also hydrates.
**Used for.** Dry or sensitive skin, mild dullness and as a gentle introduction to peels.

## Azelaic and Kojic-Based Peels

**Used for.** Pigmentation and acne, often as part of brightening plans, gentle on sensitive skin.

## TCA (Trichloroacetic Acid)

**Type.** A stronger acid, used from low strength for superficial peels to medium-depth.
**Used for.** Sun damage, deeper pigment, rough texture and, in focal form (TCA CROSS), icepick acne scars.
**Points.** Needs experience, especially in brown skin, because of the risk of pigment change and scarring. Used cautiously, and for the right patient.

## Combination Peels

Dermatologists sometimes combine acids, for example salicylic with mandelic, to treat acne and marks together while limiting irritation.

## Which Is Right for You?

The choice depends on your skin type (oily, dry, sensitive), skin tone, the problem (acne, marks, melasma, ageing, texture), previous reactions and downtime you can accept. In Indian skin, we usually begin with gentler, superficial peels and build up, to avoid post-inflammatory darkening.

## Before a Peel

Prepare the skin with sun protection and, if advised, a brightening or retinoid cream, and stop them when told. Avoid waxing, threading, scrubs and tanning before a peel.

## After a Peel

Expect tightness, mild redness and flaking for a few days. Moisturise, avoid picking, and use sunscreen. See [downtime and recovery](/blog/chemical-peel-downtime-and-recovery-timeline).
