Peels can help with pigmentation, but on Indian skin they have to be used carefully. Done badly, a peel can darken skin rather than lighten it.

## What Peels Can Do

- Lighten **post-inflammatory marks** after acne.
- Improve **sun-related pigmentation** and uneven tone.
- Assist treatment of **melasma,** as part of a plan, not on their own.
- Brighten dull skin and improve texture.

## The Risk on Brown Skin

Skin with more melanin reacts to irritation by making more pigment. A peel that is too strong, left on too long or used too often can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, and make the problem worse. For this reason, we prefer a gentle, gradual approach.

## Peels We Typically Use

- **Mandelic, lactic and low-strength glycolic peels,** which penetrate slowly.
- **Salicylic peels** for oily and acne-prone skin.
- **Azelaic- and kojic-based peels** in brightening plans.
- **Low-strength TCA** in selected cases, with caution.

Strong or deep peels are generally not used for pigment in Indian skin. See [chemical peel types explained](/blog/chemical-peel-types-glycolic-salicylic-tca-explained).

## Melasma: A Special Case

Melasma is driven by hormones, sun and visible light, and it tends to relapse. Peels may help, but strong peels can worsen it. Treatment centres on:

1. **Strict sun and visible-light protection** with a tinted sunscreen.
2. **Prescription brightening creams** and, in some patients, oral medicine.
3. **Very gentle peels** at long intervals, if used at all.

See [melasma, sun protection and triggers](/blog/melasma-sunscreen-and-triggers).

## A Safer Way to Peel

- Prepare the skin for two to four weeks with sun protection and a gentle regimen.
- Begin with a low strength and a short contact time, and test a patch.
- Space peels two to four weeks apart, increasing slowly.
- Use sun protection religiously before and after, as sun after a peel is the commonest cause of problems.
- Stop if the skin becomes irritated, and tell us.

## What Results to Expect

Mild pigment and marks often improve by thirty to fifty percent after a series of four to six peels, together with a good home plan. Melasma improves, but is rarely cleared, and needs maintenance. Results fade without sun protection.

## Who Should Avoid Peels

People with active infection or cold sores in the area, irritated or broken skin, a recent sunburn or tan, recent isotretinoin, or pregnancy (some acids). Tell us about all creams and medicines.

## Side Effects

Redness, dryness and flaking are normal. Burning, blistering, crusting or darkening are not, and need early review.
