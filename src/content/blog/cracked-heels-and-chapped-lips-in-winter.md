Heels and lips are two spots where dryness shows first, because both have few oil glands.

## Cracked Heels

Thick, dry skin around the heel splits under pressure from standing. Early care prevents painful, bleeding cracks.

- Soak feet briefly in lukewarm water, then gently smooth thick skin with a foot file.
- Apply a urea-based cream, 10 percent or higher, once or twice daily.
- Seal with a thick moisturiser and cotton socks at night.
- Wear closed, cushioned shoes, and avoid long barefoot walks.

## When Heels Need a Doctor

Deep cracks that bleed, redness, swelling or discharge suggest infection. A fungal infection of the feet can also look like dry heels. People with diabetes should have any crack checked promptly.

## Chapped Lips

Lips have no oil glands, and licking only makes them drier. Use a plain petrolatum-based balm often, especially at night. Avoid balms with strong menthol, camphor or fragrance, which irritate some people.

## When Lips Do Not Settle

Persistent cracking at the corners, swelling or burning may be an allergy to a product or an infection. Book a visit if it lasts more than two weeks.

## Why Heels Crack

The skin on the soles is the thickest on the body, with no oil glands. When it dries out, it loses flexibility. With each step, the heel pad spreads sideways, and the dry edge splits. Standing long hours, open-back footwear, hard flooring, and excess weight add pressure. Diabetes, an underactive thyroid, eczema and fungal infection can make it worse.

## A Step-by-Step Heel Routine

1. **Soak** feet for ten minutes in lukewarm water. Avoid very hot water.
2. **Smooth** thick skin gently with a pumice stone or foot file while the skin is wet. Do not cut it with blades.
3. **Dry** well, especially between toes.
4. **Apply** a urea cream (10 to 25 percent) to the heels, rubbing it in thoroughly.
5. **Seal** with a thick moisturiser or petroleum jelly and wear cotton socks overnight.
6. **Repeat** daily, with maintenance once or twice a week after healing.

## Deep Fissures

Deep cracks that bleed need more care. Keep them clean, apply an antiseptic or antibiotic ointment if advised, and consider covering with a protective dressing during the day. Medical-grade adhesives are sometimes used by doctors on deep fissures. Do not pick at the edges.

## Footwear Tips

- Shoes with a closed, cushioned heel.
- Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors.
- Change worn-out footwear, and use heel cups if recommended.
- Cotton or moisture-wicking socks, not synthetic ones that trap sweat.

## Lips in Detail

Lips lack oil glands and have a thin outer layer, so they dry quickly. Habitual licking worsens it. Use a plain petroleum-based balm several times a day and before sleep. Be wary of balms with menthol, camphor, phenol or heavy fragrance, which can be mildly irritating and encourage more use. Mouth breathing at night, dehydration, lip-licking in children, and certain medicines such as retinoids or isotretinoin dry the lips further.

## When to See a Doctor

- Cracks that bleed, ooze or show spreading redness.
- Peeling and itching between toes, which may mean athlete's foot. See [tinea infections](/conditions/tinea-infections).
- Cracked, sore corners of the mouth that persist.
- Any foot problems if you have diabetes, even minor.
