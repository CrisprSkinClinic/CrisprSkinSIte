Food is one of the most debated topics in dermatology. Some links are real. Many are myths. A few popular diets do harm.

## Where the Evidence Is Reasonable

- **Acne:** higher glycaemic load diets, some dairy, and whey protein are linked with more acne. See [food and acne](/blog/food-and-acne-what-the-evidence-says).
- **Eczema:** true food allergy occurs in some children. See [food allergy and eczema](/blog/food-allergy-and-eczema-when-to-test-and-when-not-to).
- **Psoriasis:** weight loss and less alcohol help. See [psoriasis and diet](/blog/psoriasis-diet-weight-alcohol-gluten-evidence).
- **Hair:** iron deficiency, low protein intake and crash diets cause shedding. See [nutrition and hair loss](/blog/nutrition-and-hair-loss-iron-vitamin-d-protein).
- **Acanthosis nigricans and weight-linked skin changes:** improved by weight loss. See [acanthosis nigricans](/conditions/acanthosis-nigricans).

## Where Beliefs Outrun Evidence

- Foods that are said to "cause" [vitiligo](/blog/vitiligo-diet-myths-milk-fish-citrus).
- Low-histamine diets for [hives](/blog/hives-and-food-do-low-histamine-diets-work).
- "Detox" and cleansing diets for any skin condition.
- High-dose [supplements](/blog/supplements-for-skin-and-hair-what-helps-and-what-does-not) for hair and skin.

## The Risk of Strict Diets

Cutting out whole food groups can cause deficiencies and worry, especially in children, without improving skin.

## A Sensible Approach

Eat a varied diet with vegetables, fruit, pulses and adequate protein, limit sugary drinks and heavily processed food, keep a healthy weight, and test before you restrict. See our service on [food and skin disease](/services/diet-and-skin-disease).
