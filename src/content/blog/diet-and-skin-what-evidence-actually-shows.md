Food is one of the most debated topics in dermatology. Some links are real. Many are myths. A few popular diets do harm.

## Where the Evidence Is Reasonable

- **Acne:** higher glycaemic load diets, some dairy, and whey protein are linked with more acne. See [food and acne](/blog/food-and-acne-what-the-evidence-says).
- **Eczema:** true food allergy occurs in some children. See [food allergy and eczema](/blog/food-allergy-and-eczema-when-to-test-and-when-not-to).
- **Psoriasis:** weight loss and less alcohol help. See [psoriasis and diet](/blog/psoriasis-diet-weight-alcohol-gluten-evidence).
- **Hair:** iron deficiency, low protein intake and crash diets cause shedding. See [nutrition and hair loss](/blog/nutrition-and-hair-loss-iron-vitamin-d-protein).
- **Acanthosis nigricans and weight-linked skin changes:** improved by weight loss. See [acanthosis nigricans](/conditions/acanthosis-nigricans).

## Where Beliefs Outrun Evidence

- Foods that are said to "cause" [vitiligo](/blog/vitiligo-diet-myths-milk-fish-citrus).
- Low-histamine diets for [hives](/blog/hives-and-food-do-low-histamine-diets-work).
- "Detox" and cleansing diets for any skin condition.
- High-dose [supplements](/blog/supplements-for-skin-and-hair-what-helps-and-what-does-not) for hair and skin.

## The Risk of Strict Diets

Cutting out whole food groups can cause deficiencies and worry, especially in children, without improving skin.

## A Sensible Approach

Eat a varied diet with vegetables, fruit, pulses and adequate protein, limit sugary drinks and heavily processed food, keep a healthy weight, and test before you restrict. See our service on [food and skin disease](/services/diet-and-skin-disease).

## How to Judge Claims About Food and Skin

Research on diet is harder than research on medicines. People cannot easily be blinded to what they eat, and long-term trials are costly, so much of the evidence comes from observational studies that show association, not cause. A good rule of thumb is to ask four questions about a claim: Is it based on studies in people, not just in cells or animals? Does it involve randomised trials? Are the results consistent across studies? Is the effect large enough to matter? Few dietary claims in dermatology pass all four.

## Summary of Evidence by Condition

| Condition | Evidence | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Acne | Moderate for high-glycaemic diet and dairy | Reduce sugary drinks and refined carbs |
| Eczema | True food allergy in a minority | Test only if there is a clear reaction |
| Psoriasis | Good for weight loss and less alcohol | Aim for healthy weight, limit alcohol |
| Vitiligo | No evidence for food restrictions | Eat a balanced diet |
| Chronic hives | Limited for special diets | Trial only under guidance |
| Hair shedding | Good for iron deficiency and crash diets | Test, then correct if low |
| Rosacea | Individual triggers (spicy food, alcohol, hot drinks) | Keep a diary |
| Hidradenitis | Weight loss helps, other links weak | Aim for healthy weight |

## The Gut-Skin Link and Probiotics

There is great interest in the gut microbiome and skin. Some small trials suggest probiotics may help eczema in infants or acne in adults, but results are inconsistent and strain-specific. Probiotics are generally safe in healthy people, but are not a proven substitute for treatment.

## Antioxidants, Collagen and "Glow" Foods

Fruits and vegetables are good for general health and provide vitamins and antioxidants, though there is little evidence that any single food or supplement reverses wrinkles or spots. Collagen supplements show mixed results. See [collagen supplements](/blog/collagen-supplements-what-evidence-says). Sunscreen and not smoking have far stronger evidence for healthy skin.

## Hydration

Drinking adequate water supports health, but extra water does not cure dry skin in people who are already well hydrated. Moisturisers and bathing habits make a bigger difference.

## Indian Diets

Traditional diets rich in vegetables, pulses, whole grains and curd, with less ultra-processed food, are consistent with good health. A shift toward refined carbohydrates, sugary drinks and snacks has been linked with rising rates of acne, obesity and insulin resistance, including [acanthosis nigricans](/conditions/acanthosis-nigricans). Vegetarian patients should watch protein, iron and B12.

## A Balanced Conclusion

Food matters for the body as a whole, and skin benefits. But beware of diets that blame one food for a skin disease, or promise cures. See our [food and skin disease](/services/diet-and-skin-disease) service.
