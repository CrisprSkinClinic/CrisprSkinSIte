Many people ask whether a mole they have had removed needs to be sent away to a laboratory. The short answer is that it usually should, and that skipping it is a gamble that is rarely worth taking.

## What Testing Is

Histopathology means a pathologist (a doctor who specialises in diagnosing disease from tissue) examines thin slices of the removed mole under the microscope. It is the only way to know with certainty what the growth was.

## Why Looking at a Mole Is Not Enough

Even experienced dermatologists, using a dermatoscope, can be unsure about some moles. Most harmless moles look harmless, but a few skin cancers and unusual moles can look similar. A mole that looks ordinary can occasionally show unexpected features under the microscope. The microscope settles it.

## When We Advise Testing

- Any mole that has changed in size, shape, colour, or surface, or that bleeds without injury.
- A mole that looks different from your others, or is new in an adult.
- A mole that is itchy, painful or crusted.
- Any mole where the appearance leaves doubt.
- Removals where the tissue is intact and can be tested, which is why we avoid destroying moles with lasers or burning.

In some cases of clearly harmless, long-standing moles removed for appearance, testing may be optional. We explain, and the decision is shared.

## What Happens to the Sample

The removed mole is placed in a preservative and sent to a pathology laboratory. The tissue is processed, sliced and stained, and examined. A written report comes back, usually in about five to ten days.

## What the Report Can Say

- **A common benign mole,** with edges that are clear (completely removed). No further action is needed.
- **An atypical (dysplastic) mole,** which is not cancer but has unusual cells. It may need a wider removal or follow-up, depending on the degree.
- **A skin cancer,** such as a melanoma or basal cell carcinoma. This needs prompt discussion, and often a further procedure and referral.
- **Another type of growth,** such as a wart or a different skin lump.

## If the Report Shows Something Unexpected

We contact you, explain the finding in plain language, and plan the next step, which may be a wider excision, a scan or referral to a specialist. Early detection makes most skin cancers highly treatable.

## Cost and Convenience

Laboratory testing adds a small cost to a removal, and it is well worth it for peace of mind and safety.
