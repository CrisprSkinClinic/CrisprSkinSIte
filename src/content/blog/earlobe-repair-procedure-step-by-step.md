Earlobe repair is a short, simple operation. Knowing the steps helps you feel confident.

## Before the Procedure

At your visit we examine the earlobe, discuss what you want (a repair, a new piercing site later, or both), and explain the steps. If you have a history of keloids, tell us. You can eat normally and take usual medicines unless advised otherwise. Remove earrings from both ears.

## Step 1: Cleaning and Numbing

The ear is cleaned and a small amount of local anaesthetic is injected into the earlobe. It stings for a few seconds, and then the earlobe is numb. You stay awake.

## Step 2: Preparing the Edges

In a split earlobe, the skin along both edges of the split has healed separately, forming a lining. The surgeon removes this thin strip of lining on each side, so that raw edges can join and heal together. In a stretched hole, the edges are trimmed to make the hole smaller or to reshape the lobe.

## Step 3: Closing the Earlobe

The edges are brought together with very fine stitches, often in layers: one for the deeper tissue and one for the skin, to give a smooth, strong repair that will not notch. Where the patient wants to keep a piercing hole, a small hole can be preserved, or the repair can be planned for re-piercing later.

## Step 4: Dressing

A light dressing or ointment is applied. The procedure usually takes fifteen to thirty minutes per ear.

## Aftercare

- Keep the repair dry and clean for the first day, then clean gently as advised.
- Apply the ointment and avoid pulling, sleeping on the ear and phone pressure.
- No earrings during healing.
- Take painkillers if needed. The earlobe is usually only mildly sore.

## Stitches and Healing

Stitches are removed after about a week to ten days. The scar is pink at first and fades to a fine line over several months. Full strength takes longer, as the tissue matures.

## When Can You Pierce Again?

Usually after about six to eight weeks, sometimes longer, once the repair is firm and the scar settled. A new hole is placed beside the old scar, not through it. See [earlobe repair recovery and re-piercing](/blog/earlobe-repair-recovery-and-re-piercing).

## Risks

Infection, a notch or step in the edge, a visible scar, the wound opening, and keloid formation in prone people. These are uncommon with good technique and aftercare.
