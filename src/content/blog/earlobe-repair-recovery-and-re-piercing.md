Earlobe repair is a short procedure, but the weeks afterwards matter, because how you look after the lobe decides how good the final result is. This guide covers recovery, scar care, and the question most patients ask first: when can I wear earrings again?

## The First Days

After the procedure the lobe is dressed and numb for a few hours. As the anaesthetic wears off, it may be sore, swollen, or slightly bruised, and a little oozing is common. Simple pain relief as advised, and keeping the ear clean and dry, are usually enough. Avoid pressing, pulling, or sleeping on that ear, and keep hair, phone, and headphones away from it.

## Stitches

The stitches usually come out after about a week, depending on the technique used, and we will book a review. After that the wound is still healing and remains delicate, so continue to protect the lobe from tugging.

## Looking After the Scar

The scar is pink or red at first and gradually fades over months to a fine line. These steps help.

**Keep it clean and moisturised.** Gentle washing, then a simple moisturiser or ointment if advised.

**Protect it from the sun.** New scars can darken if exposed to sun, particularly in deeper skin tones, so use sunscreen or cover the area.

**Avoid picking or rubbing.** Let any crusts fall away on their own.

**Watch for thickening.** If the scar becomes raised, itchy, or keeps growing, tell us early. This is more likely in people prone to keloids or thick scars, and early treatment works better.

## When Can I Wear Earrings Again?

Not straight away. The repaired lobe needs time to heal and strengthen, and a new piercing too soon can reopen the repair. A common guide is to wait several weeks to a few months until the scar is soft, flat, and settled, and we will tell you when it is safe for you.

## Re-Piercing: What to Know

**Placement.** The new hole is usually placed slightly away from the scar line, in firm, healthy tissue, so the repair is not weakened. We can discuss this at your review.

**Who does it.** Choose a clean, professional setting, ideally with medical oversight, and let the piercer know about the repair.

**Earring choice.** Use lightweight earrings made of suitable materials, such as surgical-grade steel, gold, or titanium, especially at first. Avoid heavy earrings, which caused the problem in the first place.

**Wearing time.** Build up gradually, and take them out at night and for sport.

## Alternatives to Re-Piercing

Some people choose not to re-pierce the repaired lobe and instead wear clip-on or magnetic earrings, or use the other ear. That is perfectly reasonable, and a good way to protect the repair.

## Daily Life During Healing

**Washing.** Keep the ear dry for the first day or two unless advised otherwise, then clean gently as instructed, and pat dry rather than rubbing. Take care when washing hair so shampoo and pulling do not disturb the lobe.

**Sleeping.** Sleep on the other side or on your back for the first week or so, and a soft pillow helps.

**Phones and headphones.** Avoid pressing a phone against the ear and avoid earphones that clip or press on the repaired lobe.

**Make-up and hair products.** Keep hairspray and cosmetics away from the wound until it has healed.

## Warning Signs

Contact us if you have increasing pain, spreading redness, swelling, pus, fever, or if the wound edges separate. Report any new lump or thickening at the scar.
