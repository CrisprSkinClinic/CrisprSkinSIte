Many people with eczema find that skin gets worse when the weather changes. Dry air weakens the skin barrier, which lets irritants in and moisture out.

## Why Winter Is Harder

Low humidity, hot showers, heaters, air-conditioning and woollen clothes all irritate eczema-prone skin. Infections such as colds can trigger flares too.

## Calming a Flare

- Moisturise generously, at least twice a day, with a thick, fragrance-free cream or ointment.
- Bathe briefly in lukewarm water.
- Use the medicated cream your doctor has prescribed on affected patches, exactly as advised.
- Avoid scratching, and keep nails short.
- Choose soft cotton next to the skin.

## Know Your Triggers

Our guide to [identifying eczema triggers](/blog/eczema-trigger-identification) helps you spot patterns. Winter is also when [damaged skin barrier](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier) from too many products shows up.

## When to See a Doctor

Book a visit if the skin is weeping, crusted or painful, if itching disturbs sleep, or if creams have not helped in two weeks. Infected eczema needs treatment.
