Finasteride and minoxidil act in different ways. One reduces the hormone that shrinks follicles. The other prolongs the growth phase and thickens hair. Used together they complement each other.

## Why Combine

Studies comparing them show that the combination usually gives better results than either alone, with more men achieving stable or improved hair counts. It is a reasonable starting point for many men with progressive loss.

## Building the Plan

We usually start with one or both depending on your stage, preferences and health. Some begin with finasteride alone and add minoxidil at three to six months if needed. Others start together. Minoxidil may be a lotion or a low-dose tablet.

## Reviewing the Plan

Review at three to four months for tolerability, and at six and twelve months with photographs. If there is poor response, we check that doses are taken, and look at other causes before changing treatment.

## Practical Points

- Take medicines at the same time each day.
- Photograph your scalp every three months in the same lighting.
- Do not judge before six months.
- Report side effects early. See [finasteride side effects](/blog/oral-finasteride-for-hair-loss-benefits-and-side-effects) and [oral minoxidil](/blog/oral-minoxidil-for-hair-loss-what-to-know).

## Beyond Medicines

Nutrition matters too, as deficiency can add to shedding. See [nutrition and hair loss](/blog/nutrition-and-hair-loss-iron-vitamin-d-protein).

## What the Evidence Says About Combining

Studies that compared finasteride alone, minoxidil alone and both together over six to twelve months consistently found larger gains in hair count and patient-rated improvement with the combination. This is plausible, because the two act on different parts of the problem: finasteride addresses the hormonal driver, minoxidil the follicle's growth cycle. The combination is not essential for everyone. Many men do well on one.

## Example Starting Plans

**Early thinning, mostly at the vertex or temples:** finasteride alone for three to six months, adding minoxidil if progress is slow or you want a bigger effect.

**Moderate loss with limited density:** start both at the same time.

**Concerns about sexual side effects:** begin with minoxidil, and discuss topical finasteride or delayed addition of finasteride.

**Women:** minoxidil with or without anti-androgens, not finasteride. See [treatment for female pattern hair loss](/blog/medical-treatment-for-female-pattern-hair-loss).

These are examples, not prescriptions. A scalp examination decides.

## Adding Other Elements

Some patients add a medicated shampoo for dandruff, supplements to correct a proven deficiency, or short-term treatments for scalp inflammation. Do not add products without need, since complexity lowers adherence. In selected patients, other procedures may be suggested by your dermatologist.

## Tracking Progress

At each review, we compare photographs, check side effects and adjust the plan. Typical milestones:

- **Month 3:** reduced shedding, perhaps early baby hairs.
- **Month 6:** visible improvement in some, stable hair in most.
- **Month 12:** the best time to judge. Decide whether to continue, adjust or add other options.
- **Years 2 onwards:** maintenance, with annual review.

## Cost and Convenience

Daily medicines are inexpensive compared with other hair treatments, but they are long-term. Choosing a plan you can sustain, such as one tablet and one lotion in a routine you do not forget, matters more than choosing the most complex one.

## If the Combination Is Not Enough

Consider other causes, review adherence, and discuss options such as dutasteride or a transplant for suitable candidates. See [hair transplant candidacy](/blog/hair-transplant-candidacy) and [what happens if you stop](/blog/stopping-hair-loss-medicines-what-happens).
