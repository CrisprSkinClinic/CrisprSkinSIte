Pimples that centre on hair follicles, and larger painful lumps, are very common. Knowing which kind you have decides what to do.

## Kinds of Folliculitis

- **Bacterial:** tender red pustules, from Staphylococcus.
- **Razor bumps (pseudofolliculitis):** ingrown hairs after shaving. See [razor bumps](/blog/razor-bumps-pseudofolliculitis-barbae-causes-and-treatment).
- **Yeast (Pityrosporum) folliculitis:** itchy, uniform small bumps on the chest, back and shoulders, often mistaken for acne and unresponsive to acne creams.
- **Hot-tub or pool folliculitis,** from contaminated water.
- **Heat and friction folliculitis,** from tight clothing and sweat.

## How a Boil Forms

A boil (furuncle) is a deeper infection of a follicle, with a tender red lump that grows, softens and may drain pus. Several joined together form a carbuncle, with fever.

## Home Care

- **Warm compress** for ten to fifteen minutes, three to four times a day, to bring a boil to a head.
- **Keep clean** with an antibacterial wash, and wash hands after touching.
- **Do not squeeze or prick** a boil, which can spread infection.
- **Loose clothing,** and avoid shaving over inflamed skin.
- **Do not share** towels and razors.

## Medical Treatment

- **Topical antibiotics or benzoyl peroxide wash** for limited folliculitis.
- **Antifungal shampoo or cream** for yeast folliculitis.
- **Oral antibiotics** for widespread, deep or recurrent infection.
- **Drainage** by a doctor when a boil is soft and full of pus, under local anaesthetic. It gives rapid relief.

## When to See a Doctor Quickly

Boils on the face, near the spine or groin, a boil with fever or spreading redness, several boils at once, or boils in a person with diabetes. See [recurrent boils and staph prevention](/blog/recurrent-boils-and-staph-prevention).

## Is It Something Else?

Deep, repeated lumps in the armpits, groin or under the breasts may be hidradenitis. See [hidradenitis suppurativa](/conditions/hidradenitis-suppurativa).
