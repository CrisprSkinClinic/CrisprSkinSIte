Fractional laser is one of the most effective treatments for boxcar and rolling acne scars and for skin texture. It also carries more risk and downtime than microneedling, so choosing the right type and settings for your skin is important.

## How It Works

A fractional laser treats thousands of tiny columns of skin, leaving the skin between them untouched. The treated columns heal quickly from the surrounding skin, and new collagen forms. Two main types are used:

- **Ablative (for example fractional CO2 or Er:YAG):** removes tiny columns of skin. It has the strongest effect and the longest downtime.
- **Non-ablative:** heats the deeper skin without removing the surface. It has less downtime, and a more gradual effect.

## Who Is a Good Candidate?

People with shallow to moderate boxcar scars, rolling scars (usually after subcision) and rough texture, whose acne is under control. Deep icepick scars respond better to TCA CROSS and other methods. See [acne scar treatments compared](/blog/acne-scar-treatments-by-scar-type-compared).

## What Recovery Looks Like

**Ablative fractional laser:**
- Days 1 to 3: swelling, redness and a raw feeling.
- Days 3 to 7: dryness, crusting and peeling.
- Weeks 2 to 6: lingering pink skin.
- Strict sun protection for months.

**Non-ablative laser:** a day or two of redness and mild swelling.

Plan time off work for a week for ablative treatment.

## Results

Ablative laser can improve scars by thirty to sixty percent, sometimes more, in one to three sessions. Non-ablative needs more sessions for a smaller effect. Texture and tone improve gradually over three to six months.

## Risks on Indian Skin

The main risk is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, a darker patch that appears after healing, more likely in medium to dark brown skin. Other risks are infection, cold sore flares, prolonged redness, scarring and, rarely, lightening of the skin.

## Reducing the Risks

- Choose lower settings and fewer passes for darker skin, and consider non-ablative options.
- Prepare the skin for several weeks with sun protection and, if advised, a brightening cream.
- Take antiviral cover if you get cold sores.
- Avoid tanning before, and sun exposure after.
- Follow aftercare, and never pick at crusts.
- Go to a clinic where a dermatologist plans treatment and manages complications.

## Who Should Wait

People with active acne, infection, a keloid tendency, recent isotretinoin, recent tanning or pregnancy should postpone.
