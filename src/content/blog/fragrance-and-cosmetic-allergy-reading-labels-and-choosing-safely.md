Cosmetics are a common cause of allergic skin reactions, and often a hidden one. Knowing what to look for helps you choose well.

## Typical Culprits

- **Fragrance and perfume,** present in many products, even those labelled "unscented", which can contain masking fragrances.
- **Preservatives,** needed to keep creams safe, and a frequent allergen.
- **Hair dye chemicals (PPD),** a well-known cause of severe allergy, including from black henna and dark hair dyes.
- **Nail products,** such as acrylates and nail polish.
- **Sunscreen filters, lanolin, essential oils and plant extracts.**
- **Metals,** particularly nickel in jewellery and cosmetics tools.

## How Reactions Show

Itchy, red, swollen or blistering skin where the product touched, often on the eyelids, face, neck, scalp margins or hands, appearing a day or two after use. Reactions to hair dye can swell the face and scalp severely.

## Reading Labels

Ingredients are listed from most to least. Look for:

- **"Parfum," "fragrance," "aroma" and essential oils** if you are fragrance-sensitive.
- **Preservatives** such as methylisothiazolinone, formaldehyde-releasers and parabens, depending on your allergy.
- **"Hypoallergenic" and "dermatologist-tested"** are marketing terms and do not guarantee safety.
- **"Fragrance-free"** is better than "unscented."

## Choosing Safer Products

1. Choose short ingredient lists and fragrance-free products.
2. Introduce one new product at a time, a week apart.
3. **Patch test at home:** apply a small amount to the inner forearm twice daily for several days before using it on the face.
4. Avoid mixing many actives.
5. For hair dye, do a strand and skin test as the pack instructs, every time, and avoid black henna.

## After a Reaction

Stop the product, rinse the skin, and use a plain moisturiser. See a doctor for severe swelling, blisters or a reaction near the eyes. Keep the product to show the doctor, since patch testing may identify the ingredient. See [patch testing](/blog/patch-testing-what-it-is-and-how-it-works).
