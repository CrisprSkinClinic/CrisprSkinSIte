Knowing what happens on the day makes a hair transplant much less daunting. This is a typical FUE day, in order. Details differ with the number of grafts and the surgeon's plan.

## Before You Arrive

Follow the pre-surgery checklist: no blood-thinning medicines unless your doctor has agreed, no alcohol or smoking for the days advised, a light meal, and loose clothing that opens at the front. See [preparing for your hair transplant](/blog/preparing-for-hair-transplant-checklist).

## 1. Final Planning and Hairline Design

The surgeon reviews your plan, confirms the number of grafts, and draws your hairline with you. The line is designed for your face shape and age, with a slightly irregular, natural edge, and with allowance for future hair loss. Photographs are taken.

## 2. Anaesthesia

The donor area at the back of the head is numbed with local anaesthetic, usually after a short trim of the hair. The injections sting for a short time, and then the area is numb. The recipient area at the front is numbed next. You are awake and comfortable throughout.

## 3. Graft Extraction

Using a fine punch about one millimetre or less in size, the surgeon extracts individual follicular units, one at a time, from the donor area. This takes several hours for a large session. There is no cut and no stitch, and each tiny extraction site heals into a small, dot-like mark.

## 4. Break

Most people take a meal break while the grafts are sorted and counted. Grafts are kept in a cool, protective solution to stay healthy.

## 5. Creating the Recipient Sites and Implanting the Grafts

Tiny sites are made at the planned angle, direction and density, which decides how natural the result looks. The grafts are then placed into the sites one by one. This stage takes several hours too.

## 6. Dressing and Instructions

A dressing is placed on the donor area, and you receive written instructions: how to sleep (head raised for the first few nights), when and how to wash, medicines for pain, swelling and infection, and what to avoid. A follow-up is booked.

## How Long Does It Take?

A full day, often six to nine hours, depending on the number of grafts. You can have a book, music or a film, and you can eat and use the bathroom.

## Afterwards

Expect mild pain, swelling of the forehead that peaks at day two to four, and tiny scabs for about seven to ten days. Most people go back to desk work within five to seven days.
