Fungal nail infection (onychomycosis) is common, stubborn and slow. Patients are often disappointed that nails do not look better after a few weeks of treatment. Understanding how nails grow, and how long treatment takes, makes the process much easier.

## Is It Really Fungus?

About half of abnormal-looking nails are not fungal. [Nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis), eczema, injury, and certain tight shoes can produce similar changes. Because treatment lasts months, doctors prefer to confirm the diagnosis with a nail clipping examined under the microscope and, where needed, cultured. See [nail changes: when to see a doctor](/blog/nail-changes-when-to-see-a-doctor).

## Signs of Fungal Nail Infection

- Yellow, white or brown discolouration, often beginning at the tip.
- Thickened, brittle or crumbling nail.
- Debris collecting under the nail.
- Lifting of the nail from the nail bed.
- Often an associated athlete's foot. See [athlete's foot](/blog/athletes-foot-tinea-pedis-treatment-and-prevention).

## How Nails Grow

Fingernails grow about three millimetres a month, and toenails about one millimetre. A toenail takes twelve to eighteen months to be replaced completely. Medicine can clear the infection, but the damaged part of the nail stays until it has grown out. This is why you may see little change for months, and why a nail can be cured and still look abnormal for a while.

## Treatment Options

- **Topical treatments** (medicated nail lacquer or solution): used for early, mild disease or when tablets are not suitable. They are applied daily or weekly for many months, and work best when the infection is confined to the tip. Cure rates are modest.
- **Antifungal tablets:** the most effective option for established infection. A typical course is around six weeks for fingernails and about twelve weeks for toenails, depending on the medicine. They can affect the liver and interact with other medicines, so your doctor will take a history and sometimes check blood tests.
- **Nail care:** trimming and filing the thickened nail, which helps medicine reach the infection.
- **Other options:** lasers and other devices have limited evidence for fungal nail cure and are not first-line.
- **Nail removal:** reserved for severe painful nails, and used alongside medicine.

## What to Expect

- Within three months, the new nail growing from the base should look clear and healthy.
- By nine to twelve months, most of a toenail will have been replaced.
- Even with good treatment, a proportion of toenail infections return or do not clear completely, so prevention matters.

## Preventing Reinfection

- Treat athlete's foot at the same time.
- Keep feet dry, and wear breathable socks and shoes.
- Do not walk barefoot in communal areas, and do not share nail clippers or files.
- Disinfect or replace old shoes, and use antifungal powder or spray in them.
- Trim nails straight and keep them short.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if you have a nail that is changing colour or shape, especially if you have diabetes, or if you have a painful, swollen nail or one with a dark streak. See our page on [nail fungus](/conditions/onychomycosis) and our [nail disorders service](/services/nail-disorders).
