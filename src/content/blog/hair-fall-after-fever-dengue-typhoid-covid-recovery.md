A few months after a bad fever, many people notice sudden, heavy hair fall and fear permanent baldness. It is usually a temporary reaction, not a lasting loss.

## What Is Happening

Hair follows a cycle of growth, rest and shedding. A major physical stress, such as high fever, dengue, typhoid, COVID-19, major surgery, a severe infection or sudden blood loss, can push many growing hairs into the resting phase at once. About **two to three months later**, they all shed together. This is called [telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium).

## Why the Delay Matters

Because shedding starts weeks after recovery, people often do not link it to the illness. The scalp itself usually looks normal, with no bald patch.

## What It Looks Like

- Handfuls of hair in the shower or on the pillow.
- An overall reduction in thickness, mostly at the sides and the top.
- Usually no bald spots, redness or itch.

## How Long Does It Last?

Shedding typically lasts **three to six months** and then settles. New short hairs appear, and density recovers over **six to twelve months**. Most people regain their previous thickness.

## What Helps

- Treat the cause. Make sure the illness has fully resolved.
- Eat enough protein, iron and calories, and avoid crash diets. See [nutrition and hair loss](/blog/nutrition-and-hair-loss-iron-vitamin-d-protein).
- Ask your doctor to check ferritin, thyroid and vitamin D, since low levels slow recovery.
- Handle hair gently.
- Avoid expensive products that promise a quick fix. They cannot change the natural cycle.

## What Medicines Do

Most people need no medicine. Minoxidil may be considered in selected cases if recovery is slow. Hair loss medicines for pattern loss, such as finasteride, are not treatments for this. See [medical treatment for hair loss](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment).

## When to See a Doctor

- Shedding lasts more than six months.
- You notice patches, a widening parting, or scalp redness, itch or scaling.
- You have no history of illness or stress to explain it.
- It occurs with fatigue, weight change or irregular periods.
