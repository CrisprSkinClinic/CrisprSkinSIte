Many mothers notice handfuls of hair in the shower two to four months after delivery. It is alarming, and very common. In most women, it is temporary.

## Why It Happens

During pregnancy, higher oestrogen keeps more hairs in the growth phase, so hair feels thicker. After delivery, hormones fall, and the extra hairs move together into the shedding phase, three or so months later. This is called telogen effluvium. See our page on [postpartum hair loss](/conditions/postpartum-hair-loss).

## The Usual Timeline

- **Weeks 0 to 8:** hair looks normal.
- **Months 2 to 5:** heavy shedding begins and peaks.
- **Months 5 to 9:** shedding settles.
- **Around 6 to 12 months:** new short hairs ("baby hairs") appear along the hairline, and density recovers.

Most women are back to normal by the baby's first birthday. Hair recovery can be slower with breastfeeding, and in women who had other triggers.

## What Does Not Matter

You do not lose permanent hair, and shedding does not mean you are weak or that breastfeeding is harming you.

## What Helps

- Eat enough protein, iron and calories. Strict dieting while recovering and nursing makes shedding worse.
- Ask for a ferritin, thyroid and vitamin D test if shedding is heavy or prolonged. Blood loss at delivery, a vegetarian diet, and thyroid changes after pregnancy are common reasons for a slow recovery. See [nutrition and hair loss](/blog/nutrition-and-hair-loss-iron-vitamin-d-protein).
- Be gentle: loose hairstyles, a wide-toothed comb and mild shampoo.
- Be patient. No product speeds up the natural cycle.

## Medicines While Breastfeeding

Avoid starting hair medicines, including oral minoxidil, finasteride and anti-androgens, while pregnant or breastfeeding without medical advice. Topical products should also be used only as advised.

## Supplements

Check with your doctor before taking supplements while breastfeeding. Biotin is not helpful unless you are deficient.

## When to See a Doctor

- Shedding continues beyond nine to twelve months.
- You see a widening parting or a thinning crown, which suggests [female pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss) unmasked by pregnancy.
- Patches of hair loss, scalp redness or pain.
- Tiredness, weight changes, or palpitations, which may suggest a thyroid problem.
