A hair transplant is a surgical procedure on your scalp, and the result stays with you for life. The clinic you choose matters far more than the headline price. The market is crowded, and offers can look similar on the surface while differing greatly in who actually does the work, how carefully your case is planned, and what happens if something goes wrong. Here is how to compare clinics sensibly.

## Who Performs the Procedure?

This is the single most important question. Hair transplantation involves several steps, including designing the hairline, deciding on graft numbers and distribution, extracting grafts from the donor area, creating recipient sites, and implanting the grafts. Ask who does each step, and what their qualifications and experience are. A qualified doctor should be responsible for the plan, the hairline design, and the key surgical steps, and should be present and accountable throughout.

In some clinics, large parts of the procedure are carried out by technicians with limited supervision. Technical assistance is a normal part of modern practice, but the doctor's responsibility for planning and oversight should be clear. Ask directly, and be wary if you receive vague answers.

## Is There a Proper Consultation?

A good consultation involves an in-person examination of your scalp, an assessment of donor density and hair characteristics, a diagnosis of the cause of your hair loss, and a discussion of your goals. It should also cover medical history and any medicines you take. If you are given a firm quote from photographs alone, or after only a few minutes, that is a warning sign.

An honest clinic tells you if you are not a good candidate, or if you should wait or try medical treatment first. A clinic that says yes to everyone is not necessarily acting in your interest.

## Be Cautious With These Red Flags

**Guaranteed results.** No one can guarantee how every graft will grow. Results vary between individuals.

**Pressure to book quickly.** Limited-time discounts and urgency tactics are not appropriate for a surgical decision.

**Very large graft counts promised in one session.** Overharvesting can permanently thin the [donor area](/conditions/donor-area-depletion) and may compromise graft survival.

**Unclear plans for follow-up.** You should know how and when you will be reviewed, and whom to contact if there is a problem.

**Lack of transparency.** You should be able to ask questions about the procedure, the people, the facilities, and the risks, and receive clear answers.

**Unrealistic promises about density or timelines.** Final results take about a year.

## Where and How It Is Done

Check that the facility is clean, properly equipped, and suitable for minor surgery. Ask about sterilisation, emergency arrangements, and what happens if you have a reaction to medicines. The setting should feel professional and unhurried.

## What Is Included

Compare offers like for like. Ask what is included: the procedure itself, anaesthesia, medicines, post-operative care, follow-up visits, and any additional sessions or touch-ups. Some low headline prices exclude items that become additional charges later, and some do not include any follow-up. We discuss costs openly after examining you, since an accurate plan depends on your donor supply and goals.

## Travelling for a Procedure

Some patients are tempted by low-cost packages that involve travel. The main concerns are follow-up and accountability. [Healing, shedding, and growth take a year](/blog/hair-transplant-timeline-month-by-month), and you may need advice or review during that time. It is much harder to get timely help if the clinic is far away. Consider how complications or concerns would be handled before you commit.

## Aftercare and Accessibility

A good clinic explains aftercare clearly, gives you written instructions, and is reachable if you have concerns. Ask how to contact them, how quickly they respond, and what the follow-up schedule is. Good aftercare protects the result and your safety.

## Reviews and Results

Look at patient experiences, but interpret them carefully. Photographs can be selected or edited, and individual results vary. Ask about typical outcomes for patients like you, and whether you can speak to someone who has had the procedure, if possible. We do not promise results, and we will tell you what to expect for your own case.

## Questions to Ask

(For graft-number questions in particular, see [how to read a transplant quote](/blog/hair-transplant-graft-numbers-and-quotes).) Who will design my hairline? Who will perform the extraction and implantation? How many grafts are you planning, and why? How will my donor area look afterwards? What if I need a second session? What follow-up is included? What are the risks, and how do you handle complications?

## The Bottom Line

Price matters, but it should not be the deciding factor for a procedure that affects your appearance for life. Choose a clinic where a qualified doctor examines you, explains the plan honestly, and stands behind the result. If you are comparing options, we are happy to examine you and give a second opinion.
