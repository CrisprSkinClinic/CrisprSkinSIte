Hair transplantation is a safe procedure in experienced hands, but it is surgery, and honest information about risks is part of good care. Most problems are minor and temporary. A few are avoidable with the right planning.

## Common and Temporary

- **Swelling of the forehead and around the eyes,** peaking at two to four days and settling within a week. Sleeping with your head raised and medicines as advised help.
- **Scabbing and redness** for one to two weeks.
- **Numbness or tingling** at the donor or recipient area, which usually returns to normal over weeks to months.
- **Shock loss,** where transplanted and nearby native hair falls out temporarily. See [shock loss after a hair transplant](/blog/shock-loss-after-hair-transplant).
- **Itching** during healing.

## Less Common

- **Folliculitis,** small pimple-like bumps around grafts, from a few weeks, treated with simple measures.
- **Infection,** which is rare with good hygiene and aftercare.
- **Ingrown hairs and cysts** as new hairs emerge.
- **Bleeding** during or after surgery, particularly with blood thinners.
- **Poor graft growth,** from damaged grafts, poor healing, smoking, or unrealistic planning.

## Risks That Come From Planning

- **Visible donor thinning,** when too many grafts are taken or taken from an unsafe area. A careful donor assessment is vital. See [how many grafts do you need?](/blog/hair-transplant-graft-numbers-and-quotes).
- **An unnatural hairline,** when the line is too low, too straight or too dense. Experienced hairline design avoids this.
- **Further hair loss behind the transplanted area,** when native hair keeps thinning. Medical treatment lowers this risk. See [medical treatment for hair loss](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment).
- **Poor candidacy,** such as transplanting in unstable or very advanced loss, or a hair loss that is not pattern loss. See [who should wait](/blog/who-should-delay-a-hair-transplant).

## How to Lower Your Risk

1. Choose a doctor-led clinic, where a doctor does the planning and the key steps, and ask who does what.
2. Get a proper diagnosis first, since not all hair loss needs a transplant.
3. Follow the checklist and aftercare to the letter.
4. Be honest about medicines, allergies and smoking, which impair healing.
5. Continue prescribed medicines for your own hair.
6. Avoid offers that promise very large grafts at a very low cost. See [choosing a clinic](/blog/hair-transplant-clinic-choice-and-low-cost-offers).

## When to Call Your Surgeon

Call for fever, spreading redness or pus, severe pain, heavy bleeding, or sudden swelling around the eyes that does not ease.

## A Balanced View

For well-chosen patients, results are natural and long-lasting, and satisfaction is high. The risks are real but small, and they are lower with careful selection, a conservative plan and good aftercare.
