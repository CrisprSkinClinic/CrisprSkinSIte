The most common source of worry after a hair transplant is not knowing what is normal. The early weeks can look alarming, and the wait for results is long. Knowing the usual sequence helps you stay patient and spot anything that genuinely needs attention. Individual timelines vary, but this is how most patients' progress unfolds.

## The Day of Surgery

Good [preparation](/blog/preparing-for-hair-transplant-checklist) makes the day smoother. The procedure is done under local anaesthesia, so you are awake but comfortable. Individual follicular units are removed from the donor area and then placed into the recipient area following the hairline design agreed beforehand. Most patients go home the same day with instructions on washing, sleeping position, and medicines. The grafts are fragile in the first days, so you will be asked to avoid touching, scratching, or rubbing the area.

## Days 1 to 7: Early Healing

Expect redness, swelling, and small scabs around each graft in the recipient area, and some tenderness or small dot-like marks in the donor area. Swelling, particularly of the forehead, is common in the first few days, and can sometimes spread toward the eyes. Sleeping with your head raised helps. We advise a specific washing routine, usually starting gently after the first day or two, to soften and remove scabs without dislodging grafts.

In the first days, the grafts are held in place mainly by the clotting process, so avoid anything that rubs or presses on the area, including hats and helmets unless advised.

## Weeks 1 to 2: Scabs Fall Away

Scabs typically fall off within the first one to two weeks, often taking the hair shafts with them. The skin may stay pink for a while. Most patients feel comfortable returning to non-strenuous routines during this period, though visible redness can persist longer in some people, particularly those with fair skin.

## Weeks 2 to 6: The Shedding Phase

This is the stage that surprises people most. The transplanted hair shafts commonly shed, and the area may look as though the transplant has not worked. This is normal and expected. The hair shaft falls out but the follicle, which carries the growth potential, stays safely under the skin and enters a resting phase. Shedding does not mean the grafts have failed.

Some existing native hair near the transplanted area can also shed temporarily, which is called shock loss, and it usually regrows. See our article on [shock loss](/blog/shock-loss-after-hair-transplant) for more detail.

## Months 2 to 3: The Quiet Phase

For several weeks, you may see little change. The area looks similar to before the transplant, or slightly thinner if native hair has shed. This quiet period tests patience, but the follicles are preparing to grow new hair. Occasional pimple-like bumps can appear as new hairs start to emerge, and these usually settle.

## Months 3 to 6: New Growth Begins

Fine, short hairs start to appear, often thin and light in colour at first. Growth may be uneven, with some areas producing hairs sooner than others. By around four to six months, many patients see a noticeable improvement in coverage, though the hairs are still thinner than they will eventually become.

## Months 6 to 9: Thickening

Hairs lengthen and gradually thicken, and the overall density improves. Texture often changes as the hair matures, and it may be wavier or finer at first. Most patients notice a clear difference at this stage, and styling becomes easier.

## Months 9 to 12 and Beyond

Most of the final result is visible around a year after surgery. Some patients see continued maturing and increased density up to fourteen months or slightly beyond. Final results are assessed once growth has fully matured. If density is lower than expected in some areas, we discuss options, which may include a further session if [donor supply](/conditions/donor-area-depletion) allows.

## Follow-Up Visits

We review progress at set intervals, check graft growth and healing, and adjust medical treatment where needed. These visits are also a chance to ask about concerns, and photographs help track change over time, since gradual improvement is hard to notice day to day.

## Things That Help

Follow the aftercare instructions closely, protect the scalp from sun, avoid smoking and heavy alcohol around the time of surgery, and maintain any [medical treatment](/blog/medical-therapy-with-hair-transplant) advised to protect native hair. Good nutrition supports healing and growth.

## When to Call Us

Contact the clinic if you have increasing pain, spreading redness, discharge, fever, or any concern about how healing is going. Most recoveries are uneventful, and clear guidance in advance makes the process far less stressful.
