Hepatitis B is a liver infection spread through blood and body fluids. Many people carry it without knowing. Checking your status is a routine part of care before surgical procedures and before certain medicines.

## Why It Matters

Surgical procedures, including [hair transplant](/services/hair-transplant-fue) and skin surgery, involve instruments and blood. Strict sterilisation protects patients, and knowing your own status protects you. Before biologics and other immunosuppressants, doctors also screen for hepatitis B, because these medicines can reactivate an old infection.

## The Blood Test

A simple blood test shows whether you are infected, immune from past infection or vaccination, or not protected at all.

## If You Are Not Protected

Vaccination is a short course of injections. It is safe and highly effective, and doses are best completed before treatment starts. Your physician may give it, or ask us to coordinate.

## If You Are Infected

This is not a reason for panic. Your doctor will plan safe care, often with a liver specialist, and treatment for the skin condition can usually still go ahead with precautions.

## How Hepatitis B Spreads, and Why Clinics Care

Hepatitis B spreads through blood and body fluids: contaminated instruments, needle-stick injuries, sexual contact and from mother to baby. In a clinic, the risks are managed through sterilisation, single-use items and safe handling. Patients are encouraged to know their own status so that staff and other patients are protected, and so that you get the right care.

## Understanding the Blood Test

The usual panel looks at three markers:

- **HBsAg:** positive means current infection.
- **Anti-HBs:** positive (at a protective level) means immunity from vaccination or past infection.
- **Anti-HBc:** positive suggests past or present exposure.

Your doctor interprets the combination. A person with none of the markers positive is unprotected, and should be vaccinated.

## The Vaccination Course

The standard course is three doses given over six months. Faster schedules exist, and your doctor may use one when time is short. After the course, a blood test a month or two later confirms that you have responded, which is especially useful before procedures or biologics. A small number of people do not respond and need a repeat or higher-dose course.

## Why It Matters for Specific Treatments

**Biologics and immunosuppressants:** these can reactivate hepatitis B in people who carry the virus, sometimes seriously, so screening is standard.

**Methotrexate and some other tablets:** the liver is monitored, and an unrecognised hepatitis B or C changes the plan.

**Hair transplant and dermatosurgery:** safe practice protects everyone, and screening gives extra reassurance. See our page on [hair transplant (FUE)](/services/hair-transplant-fue).

## If the Test Shows You Are a Carrier

You will be referred to a liver specialist for assessment. Your skin treatment may still go ahead, often with antiviral cover when strong immune-lowering medicines are planned. Family members and partners should be tested and vaccinated if not immune.

## Common Questions

**I was vaccinated as a baby. Do I need it again?** Many adults have lost measurable antibodies but still have immune memory. A blood test, and a booster dose if low, settles it.

**Is it safe in pregnancy?** Yes, when needed. Tell your doctor if you are pregnant.

**Do I need to fast for the test?** No.
