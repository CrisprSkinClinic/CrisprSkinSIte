Women with hirsutism often try laser, are pleased for a while, and then watch hair return. The reason lies in how the two treatments work.

## What Each Does

Laser targets pigment in existing hair follicles and damages them, so hair there grows back finer and sparser. Medicines treat the hormonal drive that makes new hair, and the hair follicles that were dormant, switch on.

## Why Laser Alone May Fall Short

If hormone levels stay high, new follicles are recruited, and new hair appears in areas you treated. Without addressing the cause, you may need repeated top-up sessions.

## Why Medicines Alone Are Slow

Medicines slow new growth, but they do not remove existing hair, and take many months to show results.

## The Combined Plan

1. Evaluate and treat the cause, with medicines where indicated.
2. Start laser hair reduction, usually a course spaced weeks apart, to clear existing hair.
3. Review at regular intervals, then space out maintenance sessions.

## Practical Points

Laser works best on dark, coarse hair, and settings need care on darker skin. Avoid plucking and waxing between sessions, as the laser needs the hair root. ## How Laser Hair Reduction Works

Laser light is absorbed by melanin in the hair shaft and follicle. The heat damages the follicle, so hair grows back finer, lighter and sparser, or not at all. It is most effective on dark, coarse hair, and less so on light, grey or very fine hair. Hair is only vulnerable during its growth phase, so a course of sessions, usually six to eight, spaced four to six weeks apart for the face, is needed. Body areas are spaced further apart.

## Settings and Skin Types

For darker skin, the choice of laser type and settings matters, since more pigment in the skin increases the risk of burns or colour change. Longer-wavelength lasers are commonly used, with test patches. Avoid sun exposure and tanning before and after, and tell us about creams and tablets that increase sun sensitivity.

## How Hormonal Treatment Changes the Picture

Women who are treated for their raised androgens usually need fewer laser sessions and have longer-lasting results. Those with untreated hormonal drive often notice regrowth between sessions, and may need ongoing maintenance. Hair loss from the follicles that were destroyed is permanent, but new follicles can become active.

## Common Questions

**Can I wax or thread between sessions?** No. The laser needs hair in the follicle. Shave between sessions.

**Does it hurt?** It feels like a quick snap of a rubber band. Cooling and numbing creams help.

**How long until I see a difference?** Often within two to three sessions. Shedding of treated hair continues for a couple of weeks.

**Is laser safe for PCOS?** Yes. There is no connection between laser and hormonal function.

**What about pregnancy?** Laser hair reduction is generally postponed during pregnancy as a precaution.

## Setting Expectations

You may achieve a 70 to 90 percent reduction in suitable hair, but results vary. Some regrowth of finer hair is common, and maintenance sessions once or twice a year are typical. We aim for a realistic plan, not a promise of permanent removal.
