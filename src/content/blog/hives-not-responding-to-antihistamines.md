If you have taken antihistamines for weeks and still get [hives](/conditions/antihistamine-resistant-urticaria), you are not alone, and it does not mean nothing can be done.

## First, Review the Basics

We check the medicine, dose, and timing. Many people respond when antihistamines are used at higher doses under medical guidance, so this step comes before assuming treatment has failed.

## Look for Contributing Factors

Some medicines, infections, or associated conditions can play a role. Testing is guided by your history rather than a routine battery of tests, and in many chronic cases no clear trigger is found.

## Add-on Treatment

When antihistamines alone do not give control, further options exist, including [injectable treatment](/blog/biologics-in-dermatology-explained) targeting part of the allergic pathway. Selection depends on assessment and your circumstances.

## Realistic Expectations

[Chronic hives](/blog/hives-when-to-worry) often settle over time, even if that takes months or longer. The goal is good symptom control while it does.

## When to Seek Urgent Help

Swelling of the throat or tongue, breathing difficulty, or faintness needs emergency care.

## Why Antihistamines Sometimes Seem to Fail

Not every dose is taken consistently, and some people stop after a few days because the hives have not vanished. Antihistamines work best when taken regularly rather than only after hives appear, and the dose that works can be higher than the standard one. Another reason is that histamine is only one of several signals driving hives, so a person can improve without becoming completely clear.

## What We Review in the Clinic

We ask about the pattern of your hives, how long each lasts, what makes them worse, and what you have tried. We check for medicines that can aggravate them, such as certain painkillers, and look for associated problems like deeper swelling. Blood tests are chosen on the basis of your history, not as a routine, and a normal result is reassuring rather than disappointing.

## Keeping Track of Control

A simple record of how many days you have hives and how much they itch helps us decide whether treatment is working and when to step up or step down. Many people can reduce treatment gradually once hives have been quiet for some time.

## Where to Read More

Our page on [urticaria](/services/urticaria) explains the full range of options, and the guide to [antihistamine-resistant urticaria](/conditions/antihistamine-resistant-urticaria) covers what to expect when the first step is not enough.

## What to Bring to Your Visit

Bring a list of every tablet you have taken and the dose, along with photographs of the hives and any diary of when they appear. Note any swelling of the lips or eyelids. This helps us see the pattern quickly and choose the next step.
