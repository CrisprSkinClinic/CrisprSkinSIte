Hives -- raised, itchy welts that appear and fade within hours -- are common and usually harmless. Understanding the pattern helps you respond appropriately.

## Acute vs. Chronic

Hives lasting less than six weeks are called acute and are often triggered by infections, foods, or medicines. Hives recurring for more than six weeks are chronic, and in most cases no external trigger is found.

## Seek Urgent Care If

Hives come with swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat, difficulty breathing, dizziness, or vomiting soon after a new food or medicine. These can signal a serious allergic reaction.

## Managing Ongoing Hives

Antihistamines are the first-line treatment, sometimes at higher doses under medical guidance. Keeping a simple diary of when hives appear can help, though chronic hives often do not have an identifiable trigger.

## Outlook

[Chronic hives](/blog/hives-not-responding-to-antihistamines) can last months to years, but most cases eventually settle. Effective treatment keeps symptoms controlled while that happens.

## What Can Set Hives Off

Common triggers include viral infections, certain medicines such as some painkillers and antibiotics, and foods in some people. Heat, pressure from tight clothing, exercise and stress can bring on or worsen hives in those who are prone. In many people, particularly with chronic hives, no single trigger is ever found, and that is not a failure of the work-up.

## What to Do During an Episode

Take the antihistamine as advised, wear loose cotton clothing, keep cool and avoid scratching. Do not start long courses of strong tablets without guidance. Note what you ate, took or did in the preceding hours; a short diary over a few weeks is more useful than guessing.

## Swelling Under the Skin

Some people also get deeper swelling, called angioedema, especially around the eyes and lips. This often comes with hives and usually settles, but swelling that involves the tongue or throat, or any trouble breathing, needs emergency care straight away.

## When to Book an Appointment

Book a visit if hives last more than a few days, keep returning, disturb sleep, or interfere with work. Our page on [urticaria](/services/urticaria) describes how we assess it, and our guide to [acute and chronic urticaria](/conditions/urticaria-acute-chronic) covers the patterns in more detail.

## Children and Hives

Hives are common in children, often after a viral illness, and usually settle within days. Give any medicine as advised by your doctor, keep your child cool, and watch for lip or tongue swelling or breathing difficulty. Those signs need emergency care, while ordinary hives rarely do. Bring your child in if hives keep recurring or last more than a few weeks.
