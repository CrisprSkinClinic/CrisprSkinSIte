Not every woman with adult acne needs a hormonal work-up. Knowing when it helps saves time and worry.

## Tests Are Worth Considering If

- Periods are irregular, very infrequent or absent.
- There is new or increasing coarse hair on the face or body.
- Hair is thinning at the parting.
- The neck or armpits are dark and velvety.
- Acne is severe, began suddenly, or has not improved with proper treatment.
- You have a family history of PCOS or diabetes.

## Tests Not Usually Needed

Regular periods and acne limited to the jawline, with no other signs, often need no hormone tests.

## What Might Be Checked

Hormone levels, blood sugar, lipids, thyroid, and a pelvic ultrasound, as your doctor judges.

## What a Result Does Not Decide

Normal tests do not mean the acne is not hormonal in nature. Treatment is based on the pattern as much as on numbers.

## The Rotterdam Criteria in Plain Language

PCOS is diagnosed when at least two of three features are present, after other causes are excluded: irregular or absent ovulation (shown by irregular periods), signs or blood tests of raised androgens (such as acne, excess hair or high testosterone), and polycystic-appearing ovaries on ultrasound. In teenagers, the diagnosis is made more cautiously, since irregular periods and acne are common in the first years after periods start.

## Why Skin Doctors Ask About Your Periods

Because acne, excess hair and hair thinning can all be androgen effects. A dermatologist is often the first to see these signs, and recognising a pattern leads to earlier diagnosis. It also influences treatment: for example, hormone-targeting medicines are more helpful when androgens are the driver.

## Sensible Questions to Ask Yourself

- Are my periods regular, every 21 to 35 days?
- Has my weight gradually increased, especially around the waist?
- Do I have thick dark hair on my face, chest or abdomen?
- Is my hair thinning at the parting?
- Does my mother or sister have PCOS or diabetes?
- Do I have trouble conceiving?

## What Testing Involves

A blood test for hormones, usually early in the cycle, a glucose or HbA1c and lipid profile, and often a pelvic ultrasound arranged with your gynaecologist. Some people are advised not to have scans unless there are other reasons, depending on age and circumstances.

## If the Tests Are Normal

You may still have hormonal-pattern acne. Treatment focuses on controlling the skin, with the option of hormone-targeting medicines when appropriate.

## If PCOS Is Confirmed

Management combines lifestyle measures, treatment of cycles and fertility goals by your gynaecologist, and targeted skin and hair care. Weight loss of even five percent improves hormones and skin. See [PCOS and your skin](/blog/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs-checklist).

## Teenagers

Teenage acne is common and mostly not a sign of a hormonal disorder. Testing is considered if acne is severe, resistant, or accompanied by excess hair, very irregular periods or rapid weight gain.
