Acne that begins or persists in adulthood, especially in women, often has a hormonal pattern: deep, tender spots on the jawline, chin and neck, flaring before periods.

## Why It Happens

Androgens increase oil production and make pores more prone to blockage. Skin may be unusually sensitive to normal androgen levels. Stress, poor sleep and insulin resistance can also add to this.

## What Helps

- **Topical treatment** such as retinoids, benzoyl peroxide or azelaic acid, used consistently.
- **Hormone-directed medicines** such as combined oral contraceptives or anti-androgens, in suitable women, prescribed and monitored by a doctor.
- **Isotretinoin** for severe or scarring acne, under supervision and strict pregnancy prevention.
- **Skin-friendly habits**: gentle cleansing, not picking, sunscreen. See [why acne treatment takes time](/blog/why-acne-treatment-takes-time).

## What Does Not Help

Harsh scrubs, frequent switching of products, and crash diets.

## When to Look for a Cause

Check for PCOS if you also have irregular periods, excess hair, scalp thinning or a dark neck. See our [PCOS checklist](/blog/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs-checklist).

## Protect Against Scars

Early, effective treatment prevents marks and scars. Read about [acne scar types](/blog/acne-scar-types-explained). Our page on [hormonal acne](/conditions/hormonal-acne) and our [acne service](/services/acne) have more.

## Why the Jawline?

Oil glands in the lower face are particularly sensitive to androgens. Women with normal hormone levels can still have more sensitive glands, and levels of androgens and oestrogen shift through the menstrual cycle, so spots often cluster in the week before a period. Stopping or starting hormonal contraception, pregnancy, and PCOS can all change the pattern.

## What Hormonal Acne Looks Like

Deep, tender, red bumps without a head, often along the jaw, chin and neck, sometimes with blackheads and whiteheads in the cheeks. Lesions heal slowly and can leave dark marks or scars.

## Topical Treatment Options

- **Retinoids** (adapalene, tretinoin): unclog pores and reduce inflammation. Start slowly, and avoid in pregnancy.
- **Benzoyl peroxide:** reduces bacteria and inflammation, good for short contact use.
- **Azelaic acid:** useful for acne with dark marks, and safe in pregnancy.
- **Topical antibiotics:** short-term use with benzoyl peroxide to prevent resistance.
- **Topical anti-androgen (clascoterone):** where available, for suitable patients.

## Oral Treatment Options

- **Combined oral contraceptives** (certain types).
- **Spironolactone** and other anti-androgens.
- **Short courses of oral antibiotics,** such as doxycycline, for inflammatory spots, limited to a few months.
- **Isotretinoin** for severe, scarring or persistent acne.

Each has benefits, risks and monitoring requirements. See [hormonal acne medicines explained](/blog/hormonal-acne-medicines-explained).

## Procedures

Chemical peels, light-based treatments and treatments for scars may help alongside medicines. See [chemical peels](/services/chemical-peels) and [acne scars](/services/acne-scars).

## Everyday Habits That Help

- Wash twice a day with a gentle cleanser. Do not scrub.
- Choose non-comedogenic moisturisers and sunscreen.
- Do not pick or squeeze. It leads to marks and scars.
- Keep hair products, pillow covers and phone screens clean.
- Manage stress and sleep.

## Realistic Timeline

Hormonal therapy usually takes two to three months for clear benefit, and six months for best results. Acne can recur when treatment stops, so many plans include maintenance with a retinoid.
