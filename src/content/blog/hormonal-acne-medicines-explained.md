When creams are not enough for hormonal acne, a doctor may add a medicine. Here is what they do, and the points that matter for safety.

## Combined Oral Contraceptives

Certain pills lower androgen activity, which reduces oil and breakouts. They suit some women, and not those with certain risk factors such as migraine with aura, smoking over 35, or a history of clots. A doctor checks first.

## Spironolactone and Other Anti-Androgens

These block the effect of androgens on the skin. They can be effective for jawline acne in adult women. They must not be taken in pregnancy, so contraception is essential, and your doctor may check blood pressure and sometimes blood tests.

## Isotretinoin

A powerful medicine for severe, scarring or resistant acne. It needs blood tests, close follow-up, and strict avoidance of pregnancy, because it can cause serious birth defects. It is highly effective when used correctly.

## Topical Treatment Continues

Medicines are added to, not instead of, a good topical routine.

## Timelines

Improvement takes two to three months, with the best results after six months. Stopping early is a common reason for relapse.

## Do Not Self-Medicate

Buying these medicines over the counter, or borrowing a friend's, is risky. See our [hormonal acne page](/conditions/hormonal-acne) and the guide to [jawline acne](/blog/hormonal-acne-jawline-causes-and-treatment).

## Before Starting Any Hormonal Medicine

Your doctor will ask about your periods, plans for pregnancy, blood pressure, smoking, migraine, clotting history, family history and other medicines. Baseline checks may include blood pressure, weight and, in some cases, blood tests. Reliable contraception is required with spironolactone and isotretinoin, since both can harm a developing baby.

## Combined Oral Contraceptives in Detail

They contain an oestrogen and a progestogen, and some progestogens have anti-androgen effects, which is why certain brands are preferred for acne. Benefits include regular periods and improved acne after three to six months. Possible side effects include nausea, breast tenderness, mood changes and, rarely, blood clots. Women with migraine with aura, high blood pressure, a clotting history or smoking over 35 need individual advice.

## Spironolactone in Detail

An old blood-pressure medicine that blocks androgen receptors and reduces oil production. Common effects include increased urination, menstrual spotting, breast tenderness and dizziness. Potassium monitoring is considered, especially in older women or those with kidney problems. It takes two to three months to work, and it is often continued for months.

## Isotretinoin in Detail

It shrinks oil glands and treats severe nodular, scarring or persistent acne. Almost everyone notices dry lips and skin. Others notice muscle aches, sun sensitivity or raised cholesterol and liver enzymes, so blood tests are done. Mood changes have been reported, and we ask about mood throughout. A pregnancy test and contraception are mandatory for women. Courses commonly last four to six months, with long remissions in most patients.

## Antibiotics

Doxycycline and similar tablets reduce inflammatory spots, but resistance and gut effects limit long use. They are usually given for a few months, with a topical treatment to maintain control.

## Common Questions

**Will I gain weight on the pill?** Studies show little effect on weight for most women.

**Will acne return when I stop?** It can, which is why we plan maintenance treatment.

**Can I use these medicines if I want to conceive?** Not spironolactone or isotretinoin. Plan with your doctor, and stop well before trying.
