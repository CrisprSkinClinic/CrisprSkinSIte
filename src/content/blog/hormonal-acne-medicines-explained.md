When creams are not enough for hormonal acne, a doctor may add a medicine. Here is what they do, and the points that matter for safety.

## Combined Oral Contraceptives

Certain pills lower androgen activity, which reduces oil and breakouts. They suit some women, and not those with certain risk factors such as migraine with aura, smoking over 35, or a history of clots. A doctor checks first.

## Spironolactone and Other Anti-Androgens

These block the effect of androgens on the skin. They can be effective for jawline acne in adult women. They must not be taken in pregnancy, so contraception is essential, and your doctor may check blood pressure and sometimes blood tests.

## Isotretinoin

A powerful medicine for severe, scarring or resistant acne. It needs blood tests, close follow-up, and strict avoidance of pregnancy, because it can cause serious birth defects. It is highly effective when used correctly.

## Topical Treatment Continues

Medicines are added to, not instead of, a good topical routine.

## Timelines

Improvement takes two to three months, with the best results after six months. Stopping early is a common reason for relapse.

## Do Not Self-Medicate

Buying these medicines over the counter, or borrowing a friend's, is risky. See our [hormonal acne page](/conditions/hormonal-acne) and the guide to [jawline acne](/blog/hormonal-acne-jawline-causes-and-treatment).
