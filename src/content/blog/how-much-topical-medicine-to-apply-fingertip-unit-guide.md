"Apply a thin layer" is the commonest instruction, and the least clear. Too little means the medicine does not work. Too much wastes it, and with some medicines causes side effects. The right amount varies by medicine, and by area.

## The Fingertip Unit

For creams and ointments squeezed from a tube, a fingertip unit (FTU) is the amount that runs from the tip of an adult index finger to the first crease, with a standard nozzle. It is about **0.5 g** and covers an area about **twice the size of an adult flat hand with fingers together**.

Approximate FTUs for an adult, per application:

| Area | FTUs |
|---|---|
| Face and neck | 2.5 |
| One arm (whole) | 3 |
| One hand (both sides) | 1 |
| One leg (whole) | 6 |
| One foot | 2 |
| Front of trunk | 7 |
| Back of trunk | 7 |

Children need much less, based on size and age, and your doctor will tell you. The tube nozzle also matters, so a larger opening gives a larger FTU.

## Steroid Creams

Steroids are where the amount counts most. Using too little is the commonest reason they appear not to work, and people respond by using more strength for longer. Use the amount your doctor advises, for as long as advised, and not beyond it. Apply on affected skin only, and avoid thin-skinned sites such as the face, groin and folds unless directed. See [steroid creams and ringworm](/blog/steroid-creams-and-tinea-why-they-make-it-worse).

## Calcineurin Inhibitors (Tacrolimus, Pimecrolimus)

Applied as a thin layer to affected skin, typically using the same FTU guide, usually twice a day at first and less as it settles.

## Moisturisers and Emollients

Be generous. Adults with dry skin or eczema may use around 250 to 500 g a week over the whole body. Apply after bathing and as often as needed. See [a winter routine that works](/blog/winter-dry-skin-routine-that-works).

## Minoxidil

Lotion or solution: about 1 mL, applied to the scalp areas that are thinning, once or twice a day as prescribed. Foam: about half a capful. More does not grow more hair, and may irritate and cause facial hair. See [oral and topical minoxidil](/blog/oral-minoxidil-for-hair-loss-what-to-know).

## Retinoids and Acne Gels

A pea-sized amount covers the whole face. Using more increases dryness and irritation without improving results. Apply on dry skin, in the evening, and build up the frequency slowly. See [retinol and retinoids: how to start](/blog/retinol-vs-retinoids-how-to-start).

## Benzoyl Peroxide

A thin layer on the affected areas. It bleaches fabrics, so use white linen.

## Antifungal Creams

A thin layer covering the rash, and two to three centimetres beyond its edge, applied once or twice a day for the full duration. See [tinea treatment compliance](/blog/tinea-treatment-why-it-fails-and-compliance).

## Sunscreen

Studies use about 2 mg per square centimetre. In practice that is about **a quarter teaspoon, or two finger-lengths, for the face and neck**, and about six teaspoons for the whole body. Most people apply a quarter to half of this, which cuts the protection a great deal. See [do you need sunscreen in winter](/blog/sunscreen-in-winter-still-needed).

## Scabies Treatment

The cream is applied from neck to toes, covering every part of the body, including skin folds, between fingers and toes and under nails. An adult usually needs about 30 g. Children need less. Follow the exact instructions of your doctor.

## Tips for Measuring

- Squeeze the cream along a fingertip, then dab it in dots over the area and spread it gently.
- Do not rub it in until it vanishes. Skin should look slightly shiny, then dull in a few minutes.
- Wash your hands after applying, unless hands are being treated.
- Count how many grams you use, and check that the tube lasts as long as expected. A 15 g tube should not last an adult a few days for a large area.

## If You Are Unsure

Ask your doctor to show you how much, and which areas. Please do not guess with a steroid. Our [guide to formulations](/blog/creams-ointments-gels-lotions-topical-formulations-explained) explains the choice of cream or ointment.
