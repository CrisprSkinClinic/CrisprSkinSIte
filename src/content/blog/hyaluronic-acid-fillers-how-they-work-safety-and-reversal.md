Dermal fillers restore volume and shape. The most common are hyaluronic acid (HA) fillers, which have a long safety record and, importantly, can be reversed.

## What HA Fillers Are

Hyaluronic acid is a natural sugar molecule in the skin that holds water. In fillers, it is made in a laboratory and cross-linked so that it lasts longer. Products differ in firmness: softer gels for lips and fine lines, firmer ones for cheeks and chin.

## What They Can Do

- Restore **cheek and mid-face volume.**
- Soften **nose-to-mouth and marionette lines.**
- Add shape to **lips and chin.**
- Improve **hollow tear troughs** in suitable people.
- Smooth **depressed scars** in selected cases.

They do not freeze movement like Botox. See [fillers vs Botox](/blog/fillers-vs-botox).

## How Long They Last

Typically six to eighteen months, depending on the product, the area and the person. Cheeks and chin last longer, and lips fade sooner.

## The Procedure

The skin is cleaned and numbed with cream or the filler contains local anaesthetic. Small amounts are placed with a fine needle or a blunt cannula, and the doctor shapes the result with gentle massage. It takes thirty to sixty minutes, with little downtime apart from swelling and possible bruising for a few days.

## Risks and How They Are Reduced

- **Bruising and swelling,** common and temporary.
- **Lumps or asymmetry,** often settling or correctable.
- **Infection,** rare with clean technique.
- **Blocked blood vessel (vascular occlusion),** a rare but serious complication that can cause skin damage or, very rarely, vision loss. It is avoided by knowing the anatomy, using careful technique and having a plan to act immediately if it happens.

## Reversal

HA fillers can be dissolved with an enzyme called hyaluronidase, which is why a doctor who offers fillers should keep it available. This is a major safety advantage over permanent fillers, which we do not use.

## Choosing Safely

Choose a doctor with facial anatomy training who uses branded products you can see, plans a conservative first treatment and has a plan for complications. Avoid very cheap offers and settings without medical oversight.
