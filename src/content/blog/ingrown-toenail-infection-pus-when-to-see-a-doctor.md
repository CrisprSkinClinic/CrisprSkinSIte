An ingrown toenail starts as pressure and tenderness at the edge of the nail. If the nail edge breaks the skin, bacteria get in, and the toe can become infected. Acting at the right time keeps the problem small.

## The Stages

1. **Stage 1:** redness, swelling and tenderness at the nail edge, with no pus.
2. **Stage 2:** more swelling, drainage of clear or yellow fluid and sometimes pus.
3. **Stage 3:** overgrowth of raw, bleeding tissue (granulation tissue) over the nail edge, and persistent pain.

## Signs of Infection

- Throbbing pain, especially at night.
- Yellow or green discharge or a bad smell.
- Spreading redness up the toe or foot.
- Heat, marked swelling and tenderness.
- Fever or feeling unwell, which needs prompt care.

## What You Can Do at Home Early On

In stage 1, without pus:

- Soak the foot in warm water with a little salt for ten to fifteen minutes, two to three times a day.
- Dry well, and keep the area clean.
- Wear open or wide shoes and avoid pressure.
- Trim nails straight across, not curved, and not too short.
- Avoid digging out the nail edge with a blade or pin, which can push the nail in deeper and cause infection.

See [ingrown nail prevention](/blog/ingrown-toenail-recurrence-prevention-and-when-to-operate).

## When You Need a Doctor

- Pus, or increasing pain and swelling.
- Redness that is spreading.
- Granulation tissue or a toe that bleeds easily.
- Pain that interferes with walking or sleep.
- No improvement within a few days of home care.
- Any infection in a person with diabetes, poor circulation or reduced feeling in the foot, who should be seen the same day.

## How a Doctor Treats an Infected Toenail

- **Antibiotics,** when there is spreading infection.
- **Drainage** of pus and cleaning of the nail edge.
- **Partial nail avulsion** removing the ingrown edge, often with a matricectomy to prevent recurrence. See [ingrown toenail surgery](/services/nail-avulsion-lateral-matricectomy).
- **Treatment of granulation tissue.**

Surgery is often best done when the infection is controlled, though in painful, pus-filled toes it may be done promptly. The decision depends on the toe.

## Why Surgery Prevents Repeat Episodes

Treating only the infection leaves the nail edge that caused it, and the problem returns. Removing the ingrown part and the matching root stops that edge growing back.
