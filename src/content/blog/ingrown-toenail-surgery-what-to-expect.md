Most of the worry about toenail surgery comes from not knowing what happens. The procedure itself is short and done under local anaesthetic, but the recovery runs for several weeks, and knowing the sequence in advance makes it much easier. This guide covers the whole process for [partial nail avulsion with lateral chemical matricectomy](/services/nail-avulsion-lateral-matricectomy). Your own instructions take precedence, because we tailor them to you.

## Before the Procedure

**The consultation.** We examine the toe, assess how severe the problem is, and check for infection. We ask about your general health, particularly diabetes, circulation problems, bleeding tendencies, allergies, and the medicines you take. If the toe is actively infected, we usually treat the infection first.

**Planning your day.** Wear loose, open footwear to the clinic if you can, since you will need to go home with a bulky dressing. Arrange transport if you do not want to drive with a bandaged foot. Eat normally unless advised otherwise.

**Questions to ask.** How long will recovery take? When can I return to work and exercise? What will the nail look like afterwards? What are the risks? We answer these in advance.

## During the Procedure

**Anaesthesia.** Local anaesthetic is injected at the base of the toe. This is the most uncomfortable part, a brief sharp sting, and after a few minutes the toe becomes completely numb.

**Avulsion.** The strip of nail at the side is separated from the nail bed and removed, together with any overgrown tissue pressing against it. You will feel some pressure or movement but not pain.

**Chemical treatment.** The exposed corner of the nail-forming tissue is treated with a chemical for a measured time, then cleaned. This is what [stops the nail edge from growing back](/blog/lateral-chemical-matricectomy-explained).

**Dressing.** The toe is dressed and you are given aftercare instructions. The whole procedure is short, and you go home the same day.

## The First 48 Hours

As the anaesthetic wears off, the toe may throb. Keeping the foot raised, taking simple pain relief as advised, and resting help. Some bleeding through the dressing is common; if it soaks through, press and elevate and contact us if it continues.

## The First Few Weeks

**Drainage.** The treated area typically drains clear or yellowish fluid for a few weeks as it heals. This is expected and is part of how the area settles. It does not by itself mean infection.

**Dressings.** We will tell you when and how to change them, and how to clean the toe, often with gentle soaks.

**Pain.** Tenderness eases steadily over the first week or two, though it can take longer to feel completely normal.

**Footwear and activity.** Wear open or roomy shoes at first, and avoid pressure on the toe. You can return to desk work soon, while strenuous exercise and tight shoes wait until healing allows.

## Warning Signs

Contact us if you develop increasing pain after the first few days, spreading redness, swelling, a bad smell, thick pus, fever, or red streaks tracking up the foot. People with diabetes should be especially alert and should contact us early.

## How Long Healing Takes

The skin usually settles over a few weeks, with the full cosmetic result taking longer as the nail continues to grow. The treated side of the nail will be a little narrower than before.

## Looking After the Result

Cut your nails straight across and not too short, avoid tight or pointed shoes, and keep the area clean and dry. See [preventing ingrown toenails](/blog/ingrown-toenail-recurrence-prevention-and-when-to-operate) for more. If you are not sure whether you need the procedure, an examination gives you a clear answer, and we will tell you if conservative care is enough.
