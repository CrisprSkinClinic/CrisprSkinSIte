Isotretinoin is the most effective treatment for severe acne, and the only one that can produce long remissions. It is a powerful medicine, so it is used under close medical supervision.

## Who Is It For?

- Severe, nodular or cystic acne.
- Acne that scars or that leaves lasting marks.
- Acne that has not responded to proper courses of other treatments.
- Acne with a strong effect on mood or confidence.

## How It Works

It shrinks the oil glands, reduces oil by about ninety percent, calms inflammation and slows the cycle of blocked pores. Courses commonly last four to eight months, with the total dose tailored to your weight and response.

## Before Starting

- A full history, including mood, liver and cholesterol conditions, and other medicines.
- Blood tests: liver function and fats, repeated during treatment.
- **A pregnancy test and reliable contraception for women.** Isotretinoin causes severe birth defects, so pregnancy must be prevented from a month before to a month after treatment, with monthly pregnancy tests.
- A discussion of side effects and a signed consent.

## What to Expect During Treatment

- **Dry lips, skin and eyes,** almost universal. Use lip balm, moisturiser and artificial tears, avoid contact lens overuse and use sunscreen.
- **An early flare** of acne in the first weeks, which settles.
- **Muscle and joint aches, headache and nosebleeds** in some.
- **Raised cholesterol or liver enzymes,** found on blood tests and usually reversible.
- **Mood changes:** rare, but important. Tell us if you feel low, anxious or unlike yourself.
- **Sun sensitivity.**

## Things to Avoid

Vitamin A supplements, tetracycline antibiotics, waxing, threading, laser and peels for several months, heavy alcohol, and pregnancy.

## After the Course

Most people have clear skin for a long time. A minority relapse and may need a second course or maintenance treatment. Marks fade over months, and scars can be treated afterwards, usually after waiting some months. See [acne scar treatments](/blog/acne-scar-treatments-by-scar-type-compared).

## Myths

It is not a cure-all, it does not cause permanent dryness, and it does not "damage" the liver in people who are monitored.
