JAK inhibitors have attracted a lot of attention for conditions such as [alopecia areata](/blog/alopecia-areata-newer-treatment-options) and [severe atopic dermatitis](/conditions/moderate-to-severe-atopic-dermatitis). They deserve both interest and care.

## What They Are

They are not [biologics](/blog/biologics-in-dermatology-explained). They are small molecules, taken as tablets or applied as creams, that block signalling inside immune cells.

## Where They Are Used

Depending on the specific medicine, uses include certain forms of severe atopic dermatitis, alopecia areata, and some other inflammatory conditions, such as [vitiligo](/blog/vitiligo-newer-treatment-developments) in cream form. Approved uses differ between medicines, and not every JAK inhibitor suits every condition.

## Benefits

For some patients with severe disease that has not responded to other treatment, they can produce meaningful improvement.

## Risks and Monitoring

They can affect the immune system, blood counts, cholesterol, and infection risk, and are used with caution in people with certain risk factors. Pre-treatment assessment and regular monitoring are part of responsible use.

## Continuing Treatment

For some conditions, improvement may fade after stopping. Decisions about starting, continuing, or stopping are made together with your doctor, based on your response and safety results.

## Questions to Ask Before Starting

Ask what condition the medicine is approved for, how long it takes to work, what monitoring you will need, and what happens if you want to stop. Ask also about any vaccines you should have first and what to do if you develop a fever or infection while on treatment. These questions help you take part in the decision.

## What Monitoring Usually Involves

Before treatment, we review your medical history and arrange blood tests and infection screening. During treatment, follow-up blood tests and check-ups help us pick up changes early. The exact schedule depends on the medicine and your health, so we agree it with you at the start.

## Who May Not Be Suitable

People with certain infections, a history of some cardiovascular or clotting problems, or particular blood abnormalities may need a different option. Pregnancy and breastfeeding need specific advice. This is one reason the medicines are prescribed after assessment, not simply supplied on request.

## Creams Versus Tablets

When a JAK inhibitor is used as a cream on limited areas, less of the medicine reaches the bloodstream than with tablets, so the monitoring and risk picture differs. Your doctor will explain which form applies to you.
