An itchy, red, spreading rash in the groin and inner thighs is one of the commonest skin complaints in hot, humid cities. Most of the time it is a fungal infection called tinea cruris, or jock itch. It responds well to treatment, if the treatment is done properly.

## What It Looks Like

- Red or brown patches that begin in the crease between the thigh and the groin.
- A raised, scaly border that spreads outwards, with clearer skin in the middle.
- Itching, sometimes burning, worse with sweat and friction.
- Usually the scrotum is spared. Involvement of the scrotum, with bright red skin and small spots at the edge, points more to a yeast infection called candida.

Other conditions that look similar include [eczema](/services/eczema), erythrasma (a bacterial infection with a coral-red glow under a special light), psoriasis in the folds and friction rashes. If it does not behave like fungus, ask for a proper examination.

## Who Gets It

Men more often than women, people who sweat heavily, those who are overweight, people with diabetes, athletes and anyone who wears tight, damp or synthetic clothing. It often comes from athlete's foot, as the fungus passes from the feet to the groin on underwear.

## Treatment

For limited disease, an antifungal cream applied once or twice a day for the period your doctor advises is usually enough, with the area covered two to three centimetres beyond the visible edge. Tablets are added for widespread, stubborn or recurrent disease. The usual mistake is stopping when the itch settles. See [how long to continue treatment](/blog/tinea-how-long-to-continue-treatment).

## Avoid These Mistakes

- **Steroid or combination creams.** They calm itch quickly but feed the fungus and thin the delicate groin skin. See [why they make ringworm worse](/blog/steroid-creams-and-tinea-why-they-make-it-worse).
- **Scrubbing or using harsh antiseptics,** which irritate the skin.
- **Treating the groin but not the feet.** Foot fungus reinfects the groin. See [athlete's foot](/blog/athletes-foot-tinea-pedis-treatment-and-prevention).
- **Changing creams every few days.**

## Preventing a Repeat

- Dry the groin thoroughly after bathing, using a separate clean towel, or a hairdryer on cool.
- Wear loose cotton underwear and change it daily, and after sweating.
- Wash underwear and towels in hot water and dry them in the sun.
- Put on socks before underwear, so that fungus from the feet does not travel upward.
- Use a dusting powder if advised, on dry skin only.
- Lose weight if it applies, since skin folds hold moisture.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if the rash has not improved after two weeks of treatment, is spreading, keeps returning, or has been treated with steroid creams for a long time. See our page on [tinea infections](/conditions/tinea-infections) and the [fungal infection service](/services/fungal-infections).
