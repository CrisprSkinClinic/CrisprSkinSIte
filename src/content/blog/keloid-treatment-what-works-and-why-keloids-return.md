Keloids are among the most frustrating scars, because they grow beyond the wound and often return after treatment. A well-planned, combined approach gives the best chance.

## Why Keloids Form

In keloid-prone people, the repair process does not switch off, so collagen keeps being made after the wound has healed. Genetics, darker skin, young age and tension on the skin all play a part. Triggers include piercings, acne, surgery, burns, vaccinations and even minor injuries.

## What Does Not Work Well

- **Surgery on its own.** Cutting out a keloid creates a new wound, and recurrence rates after surgery alone are high, with regrowth often bigger than the original.
- **Home remedies,** onion extracts and unproven creams, which do little.
- **Repeated picking, rubbing or cutting.**

## Treatments With Evidence

1. **Steroid injections.** The mainstay. The medicine is injected into the keloid every four to six weeks, usually for several months. They flatten and soften most keloids, and relieve itch and pain.
2. **Cryotherapy.** Freezing the keloid, often combined with injections, particularly for smaller keloids. It can lighten dark skin.
3. **Silicone sheets or gel.** Useful for prevention and as part of care.
4. **Pressure.** Pressure garments or specially designed earring clips help prevent regrowth, especially after surgery.
5. **Other injected medicines** such as 5-fluorouracil, used with steroids in some cases.
6. **Laser.** Vascular laser can reduce redness and thickness in selected scars.
7. **Surgery combined with other measures.** When a keloid is large, removal is followed by steroid injection, pressure and sometimes radiation, delivered by specialists.

## Why Keloids Return

The tendency to overgrow is part of the person's biology, and any wound, including one made to treat the keloid, can trigger regrowth. Recurrence is lowest when treatment is combined, started early and followed for a year or more.

## What You Can Do

- Treat any raised, itchy scar early.
- Avoid unnecessary piercings, tattoos and elective surgery in keloid-prone areas, or plan it with your doctor.
- Treat acne early to avoid scarring.
- Protect new scars from sun, tension and infection.
- Tell any surgeon about your keloid history so they can plan prevention.

## Realistic Outcomes

Most keloids can be flattened and made more comfortable, and many are kept under control with follow-up. Some remain a long-term problem. A fair aim is a flatter, softer scar, with a lower chance of regrowth.

## When to Seek Help

See a dermatologist for any scar that grows beyond the wound, itches, hurts or looks thick and shiny, and before any surgery on a known keloid.
