A firm, rubbery lump that grows at an ear piercing is often a keloid. The earlobe is one of the commonest sites, and keloids there can be both troublesome and frustrating to treat.

## What Is a Keloid?

A keloid is a scar that grows beyond the original wound. It is firm, smooth, often shiny, and pink, red or darker than the skin. It may itch, ache or feel tender, and it can keep growing for months. Keloids are more common in darker skin, in teenagers and young adults, and run in families.

## Why the Earlobe?

Piercing is a small wound in a site that is under tension from earring weight. In people who form keloids, even a small wound can set off the overgrowth, sometimes weeks or months after piercing. Infected piercings and heavy earrings raise the risk.

## Treatment Options

- **Steroid injections** into the keloid, repeated every few weeks, soften and flatten it. They are the usual first treatment.
- **Cryotherapy (freezing)** before or with injections, with care in darker skin because it can lighten the colour.
- **Surgical removal,** combined with steroid injection, pressure and other measures. Surgery alone has a high rate of recurrence, often a larger keloid, so it is never done in isolation.
- **Pressure earrings** worn after surgery, designed to keep constant pressure on the repaired area for months.
- **Other medicines** injected or applied, and in selected cases radiation, are used by specialist centres.

## Will It Come Back?

Recurrence is common, particularly after surgery alone. The risk falls when surgery is paired with steroid injections and pressure, and when follow-up is kept for a year or more.

## Earrings After a Keloid

- Do not re-pierce the same site until the area has been quiet for a long time, and discuss it with your dermatologist.
- Many people with keloids are advised to avoid piercing altogether.
- If you wear earrings, choose light, hypoallergenic metals and clip-on or screw-on designs that do not pierce.

## Preventing Earlobe Keloids

- Avoid piercing if you or close relatives form keloids.
- Have piercings done by a trained professional with sterile equipment.
- Treat any infection of the piercing early.
- Avoid heavy earrings. See [preventing torn and stretched earlobes](/blog/preventing-torn-and-stretched-earlobes).
