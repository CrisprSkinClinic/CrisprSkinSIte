Laser hair reduction has little downtime, but how you care for your skin afterwards affects both comfort and results.

## What You May See and Feel

- **Redness and small bumps around each hair** (perifollicular swelling), within minutes. This usually settles within a few hours to a day, and means the laser has worked on the follicle.
- **A warm, sunburn-like feeling** for a few hours.
- **Hair that seems to grow** over the next one to three weeks. This is the treated hair shedding, not new growth. Do not pluck it. Let it fall out or shave gently.
- **Dry or itchy skin** in the treated area for a few days.

## Aftercare for the First Days

1. Cool the area with a clean, cool compress if it feels hot.
2. Apply the soothing cream or aloe-based gel we advise.
3. Avoid hot showers, saunas, steam, swimming pools and vigorous exercise for 24 to 48 hours.
4. Do not scrub, exfoliate or use harsh products for a few days.
5. Avoid sun exposure, and use a broad-spectrum sunscreen on exposed treated areas every day.
6. Do not wax, thread or pluck between sessions. Shave if needed.
7. Skip deodorant on freshly treated underarms until the redness has gone.

## Side Effects That Can Happen

- **Temporary pigment change:** darker or lighter patches, more common with tanned skin or darker skin tones. It usually fades over weeks to months. Sun protection matters.
- **Blisters or crusting:** uncommon, and a sign that the settings or sun exposure were too high. Tell us promptly.
- **Infection:** rare, with pain, pus or spreading redness.
- **Paradoxical hair growth:** rarely, fine hair grows in treated areas, more often on the face and neck.
- **Scarring:** very rare, and mostly from burns.

## When to Call the Clinic

Call if you have blisters, severe pain, swelling that lasts more than two days, signs of infection, or colour change that does not settle.

## Reducing the Risk

Choose the right laser for your skin tone, do a test patch, avoid recent tanning, follow the settings your dermatologist chooses, and keep to the aftercare. A doctor who knows your skin type and medicines helps avoid most problems.

## When to Postpone a Session

Postpone if your skin is sunburnt or tanned, irritated or infected, if you are on a medicine that increases light sensitivity, or if you are pregnant.
