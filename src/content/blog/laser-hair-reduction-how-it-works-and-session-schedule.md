Laser hair reduction is one of the most requested cosmetic dermatology treatments. It works well when the right patient, the right laser and the right schedule come together.

## How the Laser Works

The laser gives out light that is absorbed by melanin, the pigment in hair. The light turns to heat in the hair shaft and damages the follicle, the structure that grows the hair. Hair that has been treated falls out over the following one to three weeks, and the follicle grows finer, lighter hair, or none.

## Why Several Sessions Are Needed

Hair grows in cycles, and the laser works best on hair in the active growth phase (anagen). On any day, only a portion of your hairs are in that phase, so one session catches some follicles, and the rest are treated at later visits as they enter growth. That is why a course is needed, spaced by the length of the hair cycle for each area.

## Typical Schedule

These are general guides. Your plan depends on your hair, skin and hormones.

- **Face (upper lip, chin, cheeks):** every 4 to 6 weeks, usually 6 to 8 sessions.
- **Underarms and bikini line:** every 6 to 8 weeks, usually 6 to 8 sessions.
- **Arms and legs:** every 8 to 10 weeks, usually 6 to 8 sessions.
- **Back and chest:** every 8 to 10 weeks, often 8 or more sessions.

After the course, many people need a maintenance session once or twice a year.

## What Results to Expect

Most people notice thinner, sparser hair after two or three sessions, and a lasting reduction of seventy to ninety percent in suitable hair after a full course. Some fine hair can remain or return. That is why we call it "reduction". See [permanent or not?](/blog/laser-hair-reduction-expectations).

## Who Does Best

Dark, coarse hair on lighter to medium skin responds best. Light, grey, white or very fine hair responds poorly to any laser. With darker skin, a suitable laser type and careful settings are essential. See [laser hair reduction on Indian skin](/blog/laser-hair-reduction-on-indian-skin-safety).

## Before You Start

- Tell us about medicines, especially those that make skin sensitive to light, and about any recent isotretinoin course.
- Avoid sun exposure and tanning for several weeks before.
- Do not wax, thread or pluck for at least four weeks before. Shave instead, since the laser needs the hair root in place.
- Avoid the area being treated after recent skin irritation or infection.

## If Excess Hair Is Hormonal

In women, hair that is coarse, dark and in a male pattern may reflect PCOS or another hormonal cause. Laser then gives the best results when the hormonal cause is treated too. See [laser and medical treatment together](/blog/hirsutism-laser-and-medical-treatment-together).
