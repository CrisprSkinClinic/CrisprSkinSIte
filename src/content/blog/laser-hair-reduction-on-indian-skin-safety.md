Most Indian skin falls between medium brown and dark brown. In this skin, the laser has to find the hair without being absorbed by the skin's own pigment. Getting this wrong leads to burns and dark or pale patches, and getting it right gives good, safe results.

## Why Skin Tone Matters

Melanin in the skin competes with melanin in the hair for the laser's energy. In darker skin, more energy is absorbed at the surface, so the risk of burns, blisters and pigment change rises. The answer is not to avoid laser, but to use the right type and settings.

## Which Lasers Suit Darker Skin?

- **Long-pulse Nd:YAG (1064 nm):** the safest wavelength for darker skin, since it passes the skin's pigment better. It may need more sessions, but it has the best safety record.
- **Diode (810 nm):** widely used, and safe with the right settings, cooling and operator skill.
- **Alexandrite (755 nm):** best for lighter skin, and generally avoided in darker skin tones.
- **IPL devices:** a broad light source, less precise, and not advised for darker skin.

## Settings and Technique

A trained clinician chooses a longer pulse, lower energy to start, and effective skin cooling. A test patch is done first, and the response is checked before treating the full area. Settings are adjusted at every visit, since your skin colour changes with sun exposure.

## What Raises the Risk

- Recent tanning, or a tan that has not faded.
- Sunburn, or sun exposure soon after treatment.
- Using the wrong laser or settings.
- Treating over irritated, broken or infected skin.
- Skipping the test patch.

## Reducing Your Risk

- Keep the area out of the sun for several weeks before and after, and use sunscreen.
- Be honest about recent tanning and medicines.
- Choose a clinic where a dermatologist plans and supervises treatment.
- Ask which laser is used and why it suits your skin.
- Report any blistering or colour change early.

## How to Judge a Clinic

Look for: a doctor's assessment first, a test patch, a choice of laser appropriate to your skin, written aftercare, and a willingness to say no. Be wary of promises of "permanent removal in two sessions", very low price offers, and clinics that treat everyone with the same settings.
