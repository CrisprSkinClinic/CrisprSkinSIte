If you have had an ingrown toenail that keeps returning, you may have heard that the real fix is a small procedure with an intimidating name: lateral chemical matricectomy. The term sounds complicated, but the idea is simple, and understanding it makes the decision much easier.

## Why Ingrown Toenails Come Back

A nail grows from a small area of tissue at its base called the matrix, hidden under the skin at the bottom of the nail. The edges of the nail are formed by the corners of this matrix. When a side edge of the nail grows into the skin, simply trimming or removing that sliver of nail relieves pain for a while, but the matrix at that corner is still working. It can produce a new sliver, often a sharp spike, that grows back into the skin, and the cycle starts again.

## What the Procedure Does

The procedure has two parts, and both matter.

**Partial nail avulsion.** Under local anaesthetic, the ingrowing strip of nail at the side of the toe is separated from the nail bed and removed, along with any overgrown tissue pressing against it. This gives immediate relief from pressure.

**Lateral chemical matricectomy.** The exposed corner of the matrix, which would otherwise regrow that strip of nail, is treated with a chemical applied precisely to that area. Phenol is the agent most widely used for this purpose. It destroys the nail-forming cells at that corner so the strip of nail does not grow back, while leaving the rest of the nail intact.

The word "lateral" simply means it is done at the side of the nail, and "chemical" distinguishes it from methods that use surgery or other energy to destroy the matrix.

## Why the Chemical Step Matters

Studies comparing nail avulsion alone with avulsion plus chemical treatment of the matrix consistently find that adding the chemical step reduces the chance of the ingrown edge returning, compared with removing the nail edge alone. That is the whole point of doing it: you deal with the cause rather than just the symptom.

## Who It Suits

It is usually considered for ingrown toenails that are persistent, moderate to severe, or recurrent, and for people who have had the nail edge removed before and had it return. It is less appropriate for a first, mild episode that may respond to careful nail care, or when there is a significant active infection that needs treating first. People with diabetes or poor circulation need extra caution and a careful assessment of healing.

## What It Is Like

A local anaesthetic is injected at the base of the toe, which is the main uncomfortable moment. After that, the toe is numb, and you should feel pressure but not pain. The procedure itself takes a short time. See [what to expect before and after ingrown toenail surgery](/blog/ingrown-toenail-surgery-what-to-expect) for the full picture.

## What You Should Know About the Result

The treated side of the nail will be slightly narrower, since that corner no longer produces nail. Most people find this acceptable and barely noticeable, given that the painful problem is resolved. The treated area will drain for some weeks while it heals, and the skin takes time to settle.

## Risks

As with any procedure, there are risks: infection, delayed healing, ongoing drainage, an unsatisfactory nail shape, and, occasionally, recurrence. We discuss these in advance, and give clear aftercare guidance to keep them low.
