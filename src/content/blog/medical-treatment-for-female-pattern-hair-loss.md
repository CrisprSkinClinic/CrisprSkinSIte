Thinning at the parting or crown in women is common and usually gradual. It responds to treatment, especially when started early.

## First, Look for Causes

Iron, thyroid and vitamin D levels, [PCOS](/conditions/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs), recent illness, crash diets and some medicines can all contribute. Treating these is part of the plan.

## Minoxidil

Topical minoxidil is the standard first treatment. Low-dose oral minoxidil is an option for those who cannot use the lotion, prescribed off-label with monitoring. Extra facial hair is the commonest side effect, and fluid retention and a fast heartbeat are possible. See [what to know about oral minoxidil](/blog/oral-minoxidil-for-hair-loss-what-to-know).

## Anti-Androgens

In women with signs of raised androgens, such as acne or excess hair, medicines like spironolactone may help. They are not safe in pregnancy, so contraception is part of the plan.

## Finasteride

It is not used in women who could become pregnant, and is considered only in selected cases after menopause.

## What to Expect

Three to six months for early change, a year for a fair assessment. Treatment is long-term, as benefits fade after stopping.

## Practical Tips

Take pictures of the parting every three months, avoid tight hairstyles, and do not use crash diets. Read our service on [female hair loss](/services/female-hair-loss) and on [medical treatment](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment).

## Recognising Female Pattern Hair Loss

It typically begins with widening of the central parting and diffuse thinning over the crown, with the frontal hairline preserved. It can start after puberty, but is most common after the late twenties and after menopause. Hair thickness varies, and the scalp becomes more visible. Family history is common. Sudden, heavy shedding is more likely to be telogen effluvium. See [hair fall after pregnancy](/blog/hair-fall-after-pregnancy-timeline-nutrition-and-when-to-treat) and [after fever](/blog/hair-fall-after-fever-dengue-typhoid-covid-recovery).

## Tests That Help

Ferritin, thyroid tests, vitamin D and B12, and hormone tests where there are other signs of androgen excess, such as acne, hirsutism or irregular periods. See [PCOS signs](/conditions/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs).

## Minoxidil Options

**Topical 2 percent or 5 percent:** twice daily on a dry scalp. The 5 percent foam suits women who dislike lotions. Facial hair growth from drips is possible.

**Low-dose oral minoxidil:** typically 0.25 to 1 mg daily in women, adjusted to response. See [oral minoxidil: what to know](/blog/oral-minoxidil-for-hair-loss-what-to-know).

## Anti-Androgen Options

Spironolactone, and in some countries cyproterone acetate or other agents, can help women with signs of androgen excess or hair loss resistant to minoxidil. Evidence is moderate. They require contraception, and monitoring when appropriate.

## Other Considerations

**Iron and nutrition:** correct deficiencies, but avoid unnecessary supplements.

**Finasteride:** not for women who could become pregnant. Used in some postmenopausal women under specialist supervision.

**Procedures and devices:** low-level laser devices have limited evidence. Your dermatologist can advise.

**Hair transplant in women:** this is a specialised area, with limited suitability. Our clinic does not offer it.

**Camouflage:** fibres, hair thickening sprays and styling can improve appearance while treatment takes effect.

## Menopause

Falling oestrogen exposes the effect of androgens, and hair thinning increases. Management is the same as for other women, tailored to health and medicines. Discuss hormone therapy with your gynaecologist.

## What to Expect

Results build over six to twelve months. Treatment is long-term. Stopping leads to gradual loss of benefit.
