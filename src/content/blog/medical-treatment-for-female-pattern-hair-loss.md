Thinning at the parting or crown in women is common and usually gradual. It responds to treatment, especially when started early.

## First, Look for Causes

Iron, thyroid and vitamin D levels, [PCOS](/conditions/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs), recent illness, crash diets and some medicines can all contribute. Treating these is part of the plan.

## Minoxidil

Topical minoxidil is the standard first treatment. Low-dose oral minoxidil is an option for those who cannot use the lotion, prescribed off-label with monitoring. Extra facial hair is the commonest side effect, and fluid retention and a fast heartbeat are possible. See [what to know about oral minoxidil](/blog/oral-minoxidil-for-hair-loss-what-to-know).

## Anti-Androgens

In women with signs of raised androgens, such as acne or excess hair, medicines like spironolactone may help. They are not safe in pregnancy, so contraception is part of the plan.

## Finasteride

It is not used in women who could become pregnant, and is considered only in selected cases after menopause.

## What to Expect

Three to six months for early change, a year for a fair assessment. Treatment is long-term, as benefits fade after stopping.

## Practical Tips

Take pictures of the parting every three months, avoid tight hairstyles, and do not use crash diets. Read our service on [female hair loss](/services/female-hair-loss) and on [medical treatment](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment).
