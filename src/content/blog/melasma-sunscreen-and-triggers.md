Melasma is a common pattern of brown-grey patches on the face, particularly in women and in people with deeper skin tones. It is a chronic condition that responds to treatment but can return, which is why long-term habits matter more than any single product.

## Light, Not Just Sun, Is the Trigger

UV and visible light both stimulate pigment cells in melasma-prone skin. A sunscreen that offers broad-spectrum cover, ideally tinted to block visible light, is the cornerstone of management, applied daily and reapplied outdoors.

## Other Contributors

Hormonal factors such as pregnancy or contraceptive use, heat, and a family tendency all play a part. Identifying yours helps us tailor the plan.

## What Treatment Looks Like

Treatment typically combines pigment-reducing topical therapy with sun protection, and in selected cases in-clinic procedures. Aggressive treatment can backfire and worsen pigmentation, so a measured approach is safer.

## Setting Expectations

Improvement is gradual over months, and maintenance is ongoing. Patients who protect their skin consistently see the best and most lasting results.
