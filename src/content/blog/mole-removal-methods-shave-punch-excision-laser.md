Moles are removed for comfort, for appearance and when a doctor advises. The method should be chosen for the mole, not the other way round.

## Before Removal: Examination

A dermatologist looks at the mole, often with a dermatoscope, and asks how long it has been there and whether it has changed. This decides whether to remove it, which method to use and whether the tissue should be tested. See [do removed moles need testing?](/blog/do-removed-moles-need-testing-histopathology).

## 1. Shave Removal

**How.** After local anaesthetic, the raised part of the mole is shaved level with the skin, and the base is cauterised or treated to stop bleeding.

**Best for.** Raised, clearly benign moles, especially on the face.

**Healing.** One to two weeks, leaving a flat pale or pink mark. Some cells may lie deeper, so the mole can occasionally regrow.

## 2. Punch Excision

**How.** A small circular blade removes the mole and its root as a plug. The wound is closed with a stitch.

**Best for.** Small moles, flat or deeper.

**Healing.** Stitches out in about a week on the face, two weeks on the body. A small, round or line scar.

## 3. Elliptical Surgical Excision

**How.** The mole is removed with a small margin, in an oval shape, and the wound is closed with fine stitches along the natural skin lines.

**Best for.** Larger moles, moles that need complete removal and testing, and moles that have been changing.

**Healing.** A thin line scar that fades over six to twelve months.

## 4. Laser and Cautery

Lasers and burning methods destroy the tissue, so the mole cannot be tested. We do not use them on moles that have not been examined and, where there is any doubt, we prefer methods that keep the tissue for testing.

## 5. Methods We Do Not Advise

- **Home removal creams, acids and pastes:** they burn the skin, scar and can hide a dangerous mole.
- **Cutting, tying or scraping at home:** infection, bleeding and incomplete removal.
- **Beauty clinics without medical assessment:** the mole is not examined or tested.

## How We Choose

We weigh the size, depth and site of the mole, how it looks under the dermatoscope, your skin type and scarring tendency, and your goals. A mole on the face is often shaved or punched for a fine result. A mole that needs complete removal is excised.

## Comfort and Recovery

Local anaesthetic numbs the area, so you feel only the injection. Mild soreness follows. Keep the wound clean, covered as advised and out of the sun. See [mole removal scar and aftercare](/blog/mole-removal-scar-and-aftercare).

## Risks

Infection, bleeding, a visible or raised scar, pigment change and regrowth of the mole. These are lower with the right method and aftercare.
