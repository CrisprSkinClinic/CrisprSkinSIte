Molluscum contagiosum worries parents, because the bumps spread and look alarming. In most children, the safest approach is patience, with treatment for selected cases.

## What It Is

A viral skin infection that causes small, firm, pearly bumps with a tiny central dimple, mainly in children aged one to ten. It spreads by skin contact, shared towels and scratching. See [molluscum contagiosum](/conditions/molluscum-contagiosum).

## Why Waiting Is Often Best

Molluscum usually clears on its own within six to twelve months, sometimes up to two years. It rarely scars when left alone, and it does not cause illness. Treatments can be uncomfortable, and for young children, the distress can outweigh the benefit.

## When to Consider Treatment

- Bumps are spreading rapidly or are numerous.
- They are on the face, eyelids or genital area.
- They are itchy, sore or infected.
- The child has eczema, which makes molluscum spread and flare.
- Social or school concerns, or a weakened immune system.
- Adults with molluscum, where it can be sexually transmitted.

## Treatment Options

- **Gentle topical treatments** applied at home, which cause mild irritation to bring on clearance.
- **Cryotherapy (freezing),** quick but stinging, suited to older children.
- **Curettage,** removal with a small instrument, done with numbing cream.
- **Treatment of eczema** and any infection alongside.

Squeezing, picking or home remedies can cause scarring and spread.

## Limiting Spread

- Cover bumps if possible with clothing or a plaster.
- Do not share towels, bath sponges or clothes.
- Wash hands after touching the bumps.
- Avoid scratching, and keep nails short.
- Children can usually go to school and swim, with covered lesions.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist for lesions near the eyes, widespread molluscum, red and painful bumps, or a child with eczema. See [viral skin infections](/services/viral-skin-infections) and [molluscum and warts at school](/blog/viral-skin-infections-school).
