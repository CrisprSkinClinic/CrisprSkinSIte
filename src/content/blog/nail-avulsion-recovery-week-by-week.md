Recovery from ingrown toenail surgery is usually straightforward. Following a few simple steps keeps healing smooth and lowers the chance of infection.

## The Procedure in Brief

After numbing the toe with a ring block, the ingrown edge of the nail is removed, and a small amount of chemical (usually phenol) is applied to the nail root on that side to stop the edge growing back. The toe is dressed. The procedure takes about twenty to thirty minutes. See [what the procedure involves](/blog/lateral-chemical-matricectomy-explained).

## Day 0 to 2

- The numbness lasts for several hours, so take a painkiller before it wears off.
- Keep the foot raised as much as you can.
- Keep the dressing dry and in place until the day advised.
- Wear a loose open shoe or sandal. Avoid driving if the numbness persists or pain is strong.

## Day 2 to 7

- The first dressing is changed, and you are shown how to dress it.
- Clean gently, usually with saline or soapy water, then apply the antiseptic or ointment advised.
- Some clear, yellowish or blood-stained fluid is normal, and drainage can last one to three weeks because of the chemical.
- Pain is mild, and settles over a few days.

## Week 2

- Drainage is lessening, and the toe is less tender.
- Return to normal shoes when comfortable, often by the end of week two, choosing a roomy toe box.
- Avoid pressure, kicking, sports and tight shoes.

## Weeks 3 to 6

- Healing finishes, and the skin edge becomes dry and firm.
- Most people are back to normal footwear and activity.
- The nail is narrower than before, which is the intended result.

## Warning Signs of Infection

- Increasing pain after the first few days.
- Spreading redness, swelling or heat.
- Thick yellow or green pus, or a bad smell.
- Fever.

Call us, since infection is treated with antibiotics and sometimes needs a dressing change.

## Special Cases

People with diabetes, poor circulation or reduced foot sensation need careful follow-up and an early review for any change. See [nail surgery and diabetes](/blog/ingrown-toenail-surgery-what-to-expect).

## Results

Most people have no further trouble. A small number get a recurrence, which can be treated again. See [ingrown toenails: when to operate](/blog/ingrown-toenail-recurrence-prevention-and-when-to-operate).
