Nails reflect both local problems and, occasionally, wider health. Most changes are benign, but knowing which ones deserve attention saves both worry and delay.

## Usually Harmless

Fine vertical ridges that become more visible with age and the occasional white spot after minor injury are common and rarely need treatment.

## Worth Getting Checked

Thickened, yellowed, or crumbling nails may be [fungal infection](/conditions/onychomycosis) or [psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis). Pitting, separation from the nail bed, [painful swelling at the nail fold](/conditions/paronychia), or a dark streak that is new, widening, or changing all merit examination.

## Why Diagnosis Comes First

Fungal infection, psoriasis, and injury can look alike, but treatments differ and fungal therapy runs for months. Confirming the cause before starting avoids a long course of the wrong treatment.

## Patience Is Part of the Plan

Nails grow slowly, so even correct treatment takes months to show a clean new nail. Early evaluation shortens that wait.

## What We Look At During an Examination

We examine all twenty nails, the skin around them and often the scalp and skin as well, because nail changes can be the first sign of a skin condition such as [psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis). We ask when the change began, whether one nail or many are affected, and whether you have had injury, frequent water exposure, or recent illness.

## Tests You Might Be Offered

If a fungal infection is suspected, a small clipping or scraping is sent for testing, because treatment should be based on a confirmed diagnosis. A dark streak that is new, widening or changing needs a closer look with a magnifying instrument, and sometimes a small sample, to be sure it is harmless.

## Everyday Nail Care That Helps

Keep nails short and dry, avoid biting or picking at the cuticle, wear gloves for wet work, and do not trim into the corners, which can cause an [ingrown toenail](/conditions/ingrown-toenail). Artificial nails and aggressive manicures can damage the nail and trigger infection, so give your nails regular breaks.

## When to Come Sooner

See a doctor promptly for severe pain, spreading redness, pus, a nail lifting off the bed after injury, or any dark streak that changes. Diabetes or poor circulation raises the stakes, so earlier review is wise. Our page on [nail disorders](/services/nail-disorders) explains how we assess and treat them.

## Keeping a Record

A photograph of the affected nail each month, taken in the same light, makes slow changes easier to judge. Note when the change began, any injury, and any new medicines. Bring the photographs to your visit, since they help us judge whether a nail is improving, stable or changing.
