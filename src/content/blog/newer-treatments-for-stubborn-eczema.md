For many years, the options for severe eczema were limited and often blunt. That has changed, but the first steps have not.

## Start With the Basics

Before considering anything advanced, we check that [moisturizing and topical treatment](/services/eczema) are being used correctly and consistently, and look for triggers, infections, or another diagnosis. Many "stubborn" cases improve at this stage.

## When Disease Is Still Uncontrolled

Options for severe disease include phototherapy, established oral medicines, and newer targeted treatments such as [biologics](/blog/biologics-in-dermatology-explained) and [JAK inhibitors](/blog/jak-inhibitors-safety-and-monitoring). These work on specific parts of the immune response involved in eczema.

## What They Involve

Targeted treatments need pre-treatment assessment, screening, and regular follow-up. Each has its own benefits and risks, and availability and approved uses differ between medicines.

## Choosing Well

Age, other health conditions, pregnancy plans, and cost all matter. We discuss realistic options with you rather than steering toward a particular product.

## The Outlook

[Severe eczema](/conditions/moderate-to-severe-atopic-dermatitis) is better controlled now than it used to be. If you have been struggling despite good basic care, it is reasonable to ask whether your plan needs a fresh look.

## Questions to Ask Your Dermatologist

Is my eczema truly atopic dermatitis, or could it be a contact allergy, fungal infection or another condition? Have we maximised moisturizers, topical steroids or non-steroidal creams, and are there any triggers we can remove? What do the advanced options involve in terms of tests, injections or tablets, and follow-up? These questions often show whether a step-up is genuinely needed.

## Daily Care Still Matters

Even on advanced treatment, daily moisturizing, gentle cleansing and trigger avoidance remain important. Medicines work best on skin that is well cared for, and good habits often allow lower doses over time. Read more about [adult eczema](/conditions/atopic-eczema-adults) and [damaged skin barrier](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier) for everyday care.

## Eyes, Infections and Sleep

Eczema around the eyes, frequent skin infections and loss of sleep are signs that disease is not controlled and deserve a review. Report new eye symptoms such as redness or discomfort promptly, as some eczema treatments need eye monitoring.

## Newer Creams

Non-steroidal creams offer another option for areas where steroids are best limited, such as the face and skin folds. We explain them in our guide to [newer non-steroidal creams](/blog/newer-non-steroidal-creams-psoriasis-eczema), and our page on [eczema](/services/eczema) describes how we assess and treat it.

## Keeping Expectations Realistic

Targeted treatments can bring big improvements, but they are not a cure, and daily skin care still matters. Some people need weeks to see a clear change, and the first choice may be adjusted. We review progress together and aim for the lowest treatment that keeps your skin comfortable.
