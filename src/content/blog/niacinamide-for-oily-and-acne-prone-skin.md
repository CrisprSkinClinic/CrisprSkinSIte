Niacinamide, a form of vitamin B3, has become a staple in skincare. It earns its popularity, with some caveats.

## What It Can Help With

It supports [the skin barrier](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier), can reduce visible oiliness and the look of enlarged pores, and may help with mild acne and uneven tone. It is generally well tolerated, including by sensitive skin.

## Strength Is Not Everything

Benefits appear at modest concentrations, and higher percentages do not reliably do more but can irritate some people. If your skin is reactive, begin at a lower strength and increase gradually.

## What It Will Not Do

It will not clear moderate or severe acne on its own, and it does not replace prescription treatment where that is needed.

## Combining It With Other Products

It pairs easily with most routines, including [vitamin C](/blog/does-vitamin-c-serum-work) and [retinoids](/blog/retinol-vs-retinoids-how-to-start), though introducing one new product at a time makes any reaction easier to trace.

## When to Get Help

If [acne](/services/acne) is leaving marks, [scarring](/services/acne-scars), or persisting despite a good routine, an evaluation is worthwhile. Treating acne early protects the skin in the long run.

## How to Introduce It

Start with a product in the range of two to five percent, once a day, in the morning or evening. If your skin tolerates it after a week or two, you can increase the frequency. Apply it to clean skin before moisturizer and sunscreen. Mild flushing or tingling can happen at first and usually settles, but persistent redness means you should step back.

## Realistic Results

Expect gradual changes over eight to twelve weeks, such as less shine in the T-zone and a smoother appearance. Niacinamide will not shrink pores permanently, because pore size is partly genetic, but it can make them look less noticeable by controlling oil and improving the surface of the skin.

## Who Should Be Careful

Rarely, people react to niacinamide, with itching, redness or a rash. If this happens, stop using it and let us know. If you have a damaged skin barrier or active eczema, introduce it slowly or wait until your skin has calmed.

## A Routine That Fits

A simple routine, with a gentle cleanser, niacinamide, a light moisturizer and sunscreen, suits many oily and acne-prone skins.
