Hair follicles are among the most active cells in the body. When nutrition falls short, hair is often the first to show it.

## Causes Linked to Shedding

- **Iron deficiency**, very common in women and in vegetarians, can increase shedding. A ferritin test is more informative than haemoglobin alone.
- **Low protein intake**, including in strict vegetarian diets without enough pulses, dairy or eggs.
- **Crash diets and rapid weight loss**, which can trigger a heavy shed two to three months later. See [weight-loss medicines and hair shedding](/blog/weight-loss-medicines-and-hair-shedding).
- **Vitamin D and B12 deficiency**, possibly contributing, particularly in people with low levels.
- **Zinc deficiency**, uncommon but real.

## What Does Not Cause Pattern Baldness

Diet does not cause [male or female pattern hair loss](/conditions/androgenetic-alopecia), though poor nutrition can worsen shedding on top of it.

## Tests Worth Doing

Ferritin, vitamin D, B12 and thyroid tests, if shedding is heavy or prolonged. Do not take iron without a test, as excess iron is harmful.

## What Helps

A varied diet with enough protein, iron-rich foods such as pulses, greens, and eggs or meat, and slow, steady weight loss if needed.

## What Does Not Help

High-dose biotin in someone who is not deficient, and mega-doses of vitamins A or selenium, which can themselves cause hair loss. See [supplements](/blog/supplements-for-skin-and-hair-what-helps-and-what-does-not).

## Iron in Detail

Hair follicles are sensitive to iron stores. Low ferritin, even with normal haemoglobin, is associated with more shedding in women, and correcting it often reduces shedding over months. Many Indian women have low iron because of heavy periods, pregnancy, and diets with limited absorbable iron. Ferritin levels below about 30 ng/mL are commonly regarded as low in the context of hair loss, and some doctors aim for higher. Iron tablets cause constipation or stomach upset in some, and should be taken as advised, ideally on an empty stomach with vitamin C, and not with tea or coffee. Do not take iron without a test.

## Protein

Hair is made of keratin, a protein. In people who eat very little protein, such as those on extreme diets, shedding may increase. Most people with normal diets get enough. Vegetarians can get protein from pulses, dairy, soy, nuts and eggs, if they eat them.

## Vitamin D

Receptors for vitamin D are found in hair follicles. Low levels are very common in India, even in sunny regions, and have been linked with several kinds of hair loss. Whether correcting vitamin D regrows hair is less clear, but treating deficiency is good for bones and general health.

## Vitamin B12 and Folate

Deficiency can cause shedding, anaemia, tiredness and tingling. It is common in strict vegetarians and people on metformin or long-term acid-reducing medicines.

## Zinc, Biotin and Others

Zinc deficiency can cause hair loss and skin changes, though it is uncommon with normal diets. Biotin deficiency is rare, and biotin supplements do not help hair in people who have normal levels. High-dose biotin can distort blood test results, including thyroid and cardiac tests.

## What About Crash Diets?

Rapid weight loss, very low-calorie diets, extreme fasting, and some weight-loss procedures can shed hair two to three months later, called [telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium). Hair usually returns after nutrition is restored. See [weight-loss medicines and hair shedding](/blog/weight-loss-medicines-and-hair-shedding).

## Dangers of Too Much

Excess vitamin A (including from some supplements) and selenium can cause hair loss. Large amounts of vitamin E and iron are not harmless.

## A Short Checklist

1. If shedding is heavy, get ferritin, vitamin D, B12 and thyroid tests.
2. Eat balanced meals with protein at each one.
3. Do not follow extreme diets.
4. Correct only the deficiencies found, and recheck.
5. Be patient: recovery takes three to six months.
