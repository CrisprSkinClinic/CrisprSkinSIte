Hair follicles are among the most active cells in the body. When nutrition falls short, hair is often the first to show it.

## Causes Linked to Shedding

- **Iron deficiency**, very common in women and in vegetarians, can increase shedding. A ferritin test is more informative than haemoglobin alone.
- **Low protein intake**, including in strict vegetarian diets without enough pulses, dairy or eggs.
- **Crash diets and rapid weight loss**, which can trigger a heavy shed two to three months later. See [weight-loss medicines and hair shedding](/blog/weight-loss-medicines-and-hair-shedding).
- **Vitamin D and B12 deficiency**, possibly contributing, particularly in people with low levels.
- **Zinc deficiency**, uncommon but real.

## What Does Not Cause Pattern Baldness

Diet does not cause [male or female pattern hair loss](/conditions/androgenetic-alopecia), though poor nutrition can worsen shedding on top of it.

## Tests Worth Doing

Ferritin, vitamin D, B12 and thyroid tests, if shedding is heavy or prolonged. Do not take iron without a test, as excess iron is harmful.

## What Helps

A varied diet with enough protein, iron-rich foods such as pulses, greens, and eggs or meat, and slow, steady weight loss if needed.

## What Does Not Help

High-dose biotin in someone who is not deficient, and mega-doses of vitamins A or selenium, which can themselves cause hair loss. See [supplements](/blog/supplements-for-skin-and-hair-what-helps-and-what-does-not).
