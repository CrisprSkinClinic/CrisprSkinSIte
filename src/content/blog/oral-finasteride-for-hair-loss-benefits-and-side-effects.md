Finasteride is the most studied tablet for male pattern hair loss. This guide covers what it does, what to expect, and the side effects you should know about before deciding.

## How It Works

Male pattern hair loss happens when hair follicles become sensitive to DHT, a hormone made from testosterone. Finasteride blocks the enzyme that makes DHT, so follicles stop shrinking. The dose for hair is 1 mg a day.

## How Well Does It Work?

In trials lasting two years or more, most men taking finasteride stopped losing hair, and roughly half or more also regrew some, mainly at the crown and mid-scalp. It does less for a receding front hairline and cannot revive follicles that have been gone for years. Starting early gives the best result. ## How Long Does It Take?

Three to six months to notice anything, and about twelve months to judge. Some men shed a little at the start.

## Side Effects

Most men have none. A small number notice a drop in sex drive, difficulty with erections or ejaculation, or breast tenderness. These usually settle on stopping. Low mood and anxiety have been reported, and a few men have described symptoms that persisted after stopping. Tell your doctor about any change in mood or sexual function so the plan can be reviewed.

## Things to Know

- Finasteride lowers the PSA blood test by about half, so tell any doctor who checks your prostate.
- It is not for women who may be pregnant, who should not handle crushed tablets either.
- Stopping it means losing the benefit over six to twelve months. See [what happens when you stop](/blog/stopping-hair-loss-medicines-what-happens).

## Who It Suits

Men with pattern hair loss who understand the commitment. It is often combined with minoxidil. See our [medical treatment for hair loss](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment) and [male pattern baldness](/conditions/norwood-scale-male-pattern-baldness).

## The Evidence in More Detail

The pivotal trials, in men aged 18 to 41 with mild to moderate vertex and frontal thinning, showed that after one year most men taking finasteride 1 mg had an increase or no further loss in hair count, whereas those on placebo continued to lose. At five years, a majority of treated men had maintained or improved hair, while a large majority of untreated men had progressive loss. Independent reviewers rated visible improvement in about two-thirds of treated men at two years. The vertex and mid-scalp respond best. The frontal hairline responds less.

## Who Is a Good Candidate?

- Men with early to moderate pattern hair loss and a family history.
- Men who want to stop progression and are willing to take a daily tablet.
- Men planning a hair transplant, to protect native hair.

Less suitable: those with a history of depression or sexual dysfunction who are anxious about side effects, those planning to conceive soon (finasteride is generally stopped some months before conception as per your doctor's advice, although the evidence on the effect of male use is limited), and men with completely bald, shiny scalps.

## Understanding the Safety Data

In trials, sexual side effects were reported by roughly 2 to 4 percent of men on finasteride compared with 1 to 2 percent on placebo, and mostly resolved on stopping or even while continuing. Post-marketing reports of persistent sexual symptoms and mood changes led regulators in several countries to add warnings. The size of this risk is debated and appears small, but it is real enough to be discussed honestly before starting. If you notice new symptoms, contact your doctor early.

## Practical Information

- **Dose:** 1 mg once a day. Higher doses do not work better.
- **Timing:** any time of day, with or without food.
- **Missed dose:** skip it, and do not double.
- **Duration:** continue indefinitely, or until you decide to stop.
- **Blood donation:** donors should wait at least a month after the last dose.
- **PSA:** finasteride halves PSA, so your doctor should double the reading when screening for prostate cancer after six months of treatment.

## Topical Finasteride

Topical formulations have shown benefit in trials with lower blood levels, and may suit men who worry about side effects, though availability and evidence are more limited.

## What About Dutasteride?

Dutasteride blocks both types of the enzyme and is used off-label in some countries for hair loss. It has more potent DHT suppression, and a longer half-life. Your doctor may consider it in selected cases, with discussion of limited evidence.

## Questions to Ask Before Starting

Do I understand the benefits and the possible side effects? Will I be able to take this long-term? Have I had photographs taken? Do I know when to review? A good consultation covers all of these.
