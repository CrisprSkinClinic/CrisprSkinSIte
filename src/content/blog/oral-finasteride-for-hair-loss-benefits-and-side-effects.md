Finasteride is the most studied tablet for male pattern hair loss. This guide covers what it does, what to expect, and the side effects you should know about before deciding.

## How It Works

Male pattern hair loss happens when hair follicles become sensitive to DHT, a hormone made from testosterone. Finasteride blocks the enzyme that makes DHT, so follicles stop shrinking. The dose for hair is 1 mg a day.

## How Well Does It Work?

In trials lasting two years or more, most men taking finasteride stopped losing hair, and roughly half or more also regrew some, mainly at the crown and mid-scalp. It does less for a receding front hairline and cannot revive follicles that have been gone for years. Starting early gives the best result. See our overview on [how effective medical treatment is](/blog/how-effective-is-medical-treatment-for-hair-loss).

## How Long Does It Take?

Three to six months to notice anything, and about twelve months to judge. Some men shed a little at the start.

## Side Effects

Most men have none. A small number notice a drop in sex drive, difficulty with erections or ejaculation, or breast tenderness. These usually settle on stopping. Low mood and anxiety have been reported, and a few men have described symptoms that persisted after stopping. Tell your doctor about any change in mood or sexual function so the plan can be reviewed.

## Things to Know

- Finasteride lowers the PSA blood test by about half, so tell any doctor who checks your prostate.
- It is not for women who may be pregnant, who should not handle crushed tablets either.
- Stopping it means losing the benefit over six to twelve months. See [what happens when you stop](/blog/stopping-hair-loss-medicines-what-happens).

Men with pattern hair loss who understand the commitment. It is often combined with minoxidil. See our [medical treatment for hair loss](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment) and [male pattern baldness](/conditions/norwood-scale-male-pattern-baldness).
