Minoxidil began as a blood pressure tablet. At much lower doses, it is now widely used for hair loss. The lotion and foam are approved. The tablet is used off-label, which means doctors prescribe it based on evidence and experience, not an official hair-loss approval.

## How It Works

Minoxidil widens blood vessels and prolongs the growth phase of the hair cycle, so hairs become longer and thicker. It does not act on hormones, which is why it is used in both men and women.

## Why a Tablet?

Many people find the lotion messy, irritating or hard to keep up. The tablet is taken once a day. Studies, many of them small, suggest it can work as well as the lotion for many people, though larger trials are still needed.

## What to Expect

Hair shedding in the first two to six weeks is common, as resting hairs make way for new growth. Visible thickening usually takes three to six months, with the best response at around twelve.

## Side Effects

- Extra hair on the face and body, the commonest effect, especially in women.
- Ankle or face puffiness from fluid retention.
- Fast heartbeat, dizziness or headache.

Rarely, more serious effects such as chest pain or significant breathlessness need urgent attention.

## Who Should Not Take It

People with certain heart conditions, very low blood pressure, or fluid around the heart, and those who are pregnant or breastfeeding. Tell your doctor about all medicines, as some interact.

## Monitoring

Blood pressure and pulse are checked before starting and at review visits. Doses are low, and individualised.

## Dosing in Practice

Oral minoxidil for hair is used at a fraction of the blood-pressure dose. Common starting doses are 0.25 to 1 mg daily in women, and 1 to 2.5 mg in men, with some people taking up to 5 mg under supervision. Tablets are often split, and doses are adjusted to the lowest effective level. Because the tablets come in strengths made for hypertension, dosing needs to be done exactly as your doctor advises.

## What the Studies Suggest

Retrospective studies and a few controlled trials show that most people on low-dose oral minoxidil have stable or improved hair density at six to twelve months, with benefit comparable to, and sometimes better than, topical 5 percent minoxidil. It has also been used in alopecia areata and other hair loss under specialist care. Large, long-term randomised trials are still limited, which is why it is described as off-label.

## Side Effects in More Detail

**Hypertrichosis:** extra hair on the face, arms and body in a sizeable minority, which is dose-related. It is more bothersome in women and usually fades after stopping. Laser hair reduction can manage it.

**Fluid retention:** puffy ankles, face or fingers. Usually mild at low doses, but needs attention.

**Heart rate and blood pressure:** a faster pulse and, in some, a drop in blood pressure causing light-headedness. People with low blood pressure, heart disease or on blood-pressure medicines need special care.

**Initial shedding:** common in the first weeks.

**Headache and flushing:** uncommon.

**Pericardial effusion and serious events:** rare and mostly reported at higher doses, but a reason for careful screening and for reporting new breathlessness or chest pain.

## Who Should Avoid It

People with certain heart conditions (heart failure, significant arrhythmia, pericardial effusion), severe low blood pressure, kidney disease needing dose adjustment, phaeochromocytoma, or during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Mention all current medicines, including those for blood pressure.

## Monitoring Plan

We check blood pressure and pulse at the start and at follow-up, ask about swelling and breathlessness, and review the dose at three months. Some doctors order baseline tests depending on health.

## Lotion vs. Tablet: How to Choose

The lotion has the strongest evidence and few body-wide effects, but is messy and may irritate the scalp. If you manage it consistently and are happy with results, there is no need to switch. The tablet is convenient and may be better if you cannot sustain the lotion. Some people use both.

## Practical Tips

Take it at the same time each day. Weigh yourself or check for swelling now and then. Do not buy it online without a prescription. Photograph the scalp every three months in the same light.
