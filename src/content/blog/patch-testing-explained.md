If you have a rash that keeps coming back and the cause is not obvious, the trigger may be an allergen in something you touch regularly. Patch testing is the standard way to find out.

## How It Works

Small amounts of common allergens are applied to the skin of the back under adhesive patches, left in place for about 48 hours, and read at one or more follow-up visits. A reaction under a patch points to that substance as a likely culprit.

## Who Benefits

People with suspected allergic contact dermatitis, persistent hand or facial rashes, or reactions to cosmetics, metals, or workplace substances are the usual candidates.

## What to Know Beforehand

Keep the back dry during testing and avoid strong sun exposure. Some medicines can affect results, so tell us what you take.

## What Comes After

Identifying the trigger lets you avoid it, which is usually more effective than any long-term medication. A positive result is a practical guide rather than a diagnosis of a serious condition.
