When a rash keeps coming back and no cause is clear, patch testing can find out whether a substance you touch is responsible.

## What It Tests For

Patch testing finds **delayed allergic contact reactions** (type IV), which show up one to three days after contact. It does not test for food allergy, hives or reactions that occur within minutes, which need other tests. See [contact dermatitis](/conditions/contact-dermatitis).

## Who Needs It

- Recurring eczema of the hands, face, eyelids or feet with no clear cause.
- Rashes in the shape of a watch, jewellery, shoe or cosmetic.
- Eczema that does not respond to treatment.
- People suspected of reacting to hair dye, cosmetics, rubber, nickel, fragrance or preservatives.

## How It Works

1. **First visit:** small patches containing tiny amounts of common allergens (a standard series, plus products you bring) are stuck on your upper back with tape.
2. **Two days later:** the patches are removed, and the first reading is made. The back must stay dry and you should avoid sweating, strenuous exercise and scratching in the meantime.
3. **Third or fourth day:** a second reading picks up late reactions.

Positive tests show as red, raised, itchy spots under a patch.

## Preparing

- Avoid steroid creams on the back for a week, and steroid or immune-suppressant tablets if your doctor agrees.
- Avoid sunburn and tanning on the back.
- Bring your own products: creams, cosmetics, hair dye, gloves, shoes.
- Wear old, loose clothing.

## Understanding Results

A positive result means you are allergic to that substance, and the doctor links it to your rash and gives you a list of products to avoid. A negative result makes allergic contact dermatitis less likely, and the rash may be irritant, atopic or another disease.

## After the Test

You receive a written list of the allergen and where it is found, and tips for reading labels and choosing safe products. Avoiding the allergen usually clears the rash.

## Safety

Reactions are limited to the test spots, and rarely a strong reaction needs treatment. Tell us about any severe itch or blistering.
