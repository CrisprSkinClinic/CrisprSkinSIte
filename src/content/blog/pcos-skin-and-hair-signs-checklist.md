PCOS is diagnosed by a doctor, but your skin often gives the first clues. Here is a checklist.

## The Skin and Hair Signs

- **Acne**, especially persistent or adult-onset, along the jawline and chin. See [hormonal acne](/blog/hormonal-acne-jawline-causes-and-treatment).
- **Excess coarse hair** on the face, chest or abdomen. See [hirsutism](/blog/hirsutism-vs-normal-hair-growth).
- **Hair thinning** at the parting or crown. See [female hair loss](/services/female-hair-loss).
- **Dark, velvety patches** on the neck or armpits. See [acanthosis nigricans](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-what-dark-velvety-patches-mean).
- **Skin tags** in the neck and folds.

## Other Clues

Irregular, infrequent or absent periods, weight gain around the waist, and trouble conceiving.

## What to Do

If you recognise several of these, book a visit. We examine your skin and hair, may suggest tests, and, if PCOS is likely, coordinate with a gynaecologist or physician.

## Lifestyle Matters Most

Modest weight loss of five to ten percent, regular exercise and a balanced diet improve nearly all the signs, including acne, hair and dark patches.

## Treatment Is Specific

Each sign has its own treatment, and the hormonal plan runs alongside. See our service for [hormonal skin and hair conditions](/services/hormonal-skin-conditions) and the page on [PCOS skin signs](/conditions/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs).
