PCOS is diagnosed by a doctor, but your skin often gives the first clues. Here is a checklist.

## The Skin and Hair Signs

- **Acne**, especially persistent or adult-onset, along the jawline and chin. See [hormonal acne](/blog/hormonal-acne-jawline-causes-and-treatment).
- **Excess coarse hair** on the face, chest or abdomen. See [hirsutism](/blog/hirsutism-vs-normal-hair-growth).
- **Hair thinning** at the parting or crown. See [female hair loss](/services/female-hair-loss).
- **Dark, velvety patches** on the neck or armpits. See [acanthosis nigricans](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-what-dark-velvety-patches-mean).
- **Skin tags** in the neck and folds.

## Other Clues

Irregular, infrequent or absent periods, weight gain around the waist, and trouble conceiving.

## What to Do

If you recognise several of these, book a visit. We examine your skin and hair, may suggest tests, and, if PCOS is likely, coordinate with a gynaecologist or physician.

## Lifestyle Matters Most

Modest weight loss of five to ten percent, regular exercise and a balanced diet improve nearly all the signs, including acne, hair and dark patches.

## Treatment Is Specific

Each sign has its own treatment, and the hormonal plan runs alongside. See our service for [hormonal skin and hair conditions](/services/hormonal-skin-conditions) and the page on [PCOS skin signs](/conditions/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs).

## Each Sign Explained

**Acne:** typically jawline and lower face, deep and slow to heal, often resistant to standard creams. See [jawline acne](/blog/hormonal-acne-jawline-causes-and-treatment).

**Hirsutism:** coarse, dark hair on the upper lip, chin, chest or abdomen. Present in a majority of women with PCOS in many studies.

**Female pattern hair loss:** widening of the parting and thinning at the crown, usually with preserved frontal hairline. See [female pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss).

**Acanthosis nigricans:** dark, velvety skin in the neck and folds, reflecting insulin resistance.

**Skin tags:** small, soft growths in the neck and underarms.

**Oily skin and seborrhoea:** greasy skin and hair that is hard to control.

## The Metabolic Side of PCOS

PCOS is not just a skin and gynaecology problem. Insulin resistance is common, even in lean women, and is associated with a higher risk of prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, fatty liver, high cholesterol and high blood pressure. This is why a doctor may suggest checking blood sugar and lipids. Sleep apnoea and mood disorders are also more common.

## Fertility and Periods

Irregular ovulation leads to infrequent or absent periods and difficulty conceiving. Many women with PCOS do conceive, with lifestyle change or help. Long gaps between periods can cause thickening of the uterine lining, which your gynaecologist will monitor.

## How the Team Works

Your dermatologist treats acne, hair growth, hair thinning and pigmentation. Your gynaecologist or endocrinologist manages cycles, fertility and metabolic risk. We communicate so that treatments are consistent. For example, an anti-androgen prescribed for hair needs to be aligned with pregnancy plans.

## A Practical Plan

1. Book a consultation with symptoms, periods and family history noted.
2. Have recommended tests.
3. Start lifestyle measures: a balanced diet, regular exercise and sleep.
4. Begin skin and hair treatment that suits your goals.
5. Review at three, six and twelve months.

## Mental Health

Body image concerns, anxiety and low mood are common. Seek support, and tell your doctor. Treatment for the skin can improve confidence, and counselling helps many women.
