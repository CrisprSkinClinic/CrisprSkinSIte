Phototherapy is among the best-established treatments for vitiligo, particularly when patches are widespread.

## Narrowband UVB

A specific wavelength of ultraviolet light that calms immune activity and stimulates remaining pigment cells, including those around hair follicles. Sessions are brief and usually given two to three times a week, with eye protection, over many months. It is the usual first-line light treatment for extensive vitiligo.

## Excimer Light and Laser

These deliver light to a small, targeted area, sparing the surrounding skin. They suit limited or localised patches, and sometimes areas that have not responded to other treatment.

## PUVA

This older approach combines a light-sensitising medicine with UVA light. It is used less often today, because narrowband UVB is generally simpler and better tolerated, but may still have a role in selected cases.

## What to Expect

Pigment usually returns first as small dots around hair follicles, which gradually merge. Treatment takes months, and response varies by body area. Side effects include redness, dryness, itching, and occasionally sunburn-like reactions, which dose adjustment helps to avoid.

## Combining Treatments

Phototherapy is often combined with creams, and sometimes used alongside surgery to improve results. Long-term skin safety and cumulative dose are considered when planning courses.
