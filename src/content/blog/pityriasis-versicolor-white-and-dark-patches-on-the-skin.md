Many people worry that pale patches on the neck, chest or back are vitiligo. Very often they are a common, harmless yeast infection called pityriasis versicolor, sometimes called tinea versicolor. It is easy to treat, but it tends to come back.

## What Causes It

A yeast called Malassezia lives naturally on everyone's skin. In warm, humid weather, or with heavy sweating and oily skin, it multiplies and interferes with skin pigment. It is not caused by poor hygiene, and it is not easily passed to others.

## What It Looks Like

- Round or oval patches on the upper back, chest, shoulders, neck and upper arms.
- Colour may be paler than normal skin, pink, brown or tan, which is why it is called "versicolor".
- A fine, bran-like scale that shows when the skin is scratched lightly.
- Mild itching, or none.
- Patches become more obvious after sun exposure, because the affected skin does not tan.

## How It Differs From Vitiligo

[Vitiligo](/services/vitiligo) patches are completely white, smooth, with no scale, and often appear in the hands, face and skin folds. Versicolor patches are usually partly pigmented with fine scale. A dermatologist can often tell by looking, and a scraping under the microscope confirms the yeast. Ask for an examination before treating, since the treatments are very different.

## Treatment

- **Anti-yeast shampoos or washes** (such as ketoconazole or selenium sulphide), applied to the affected skin and left for the time advised before rinsing, for several days to a couple of weeks.
- **Antifungal creams or lotions** for small areas.
- **Tablets** for widespread disease or repeated attacks, chosen by your doctor.

Strong steroid creams do not help and may worsen the condition.

## Why the Colour Takes Time to Return

The yeast can be cleared in a couple of weeks, but the colour difference takes weeks to months to even out, because skin has to regrow its pigment. Mild sun exposure then helps the pale patches match. People often think treatment has failed when the patches are still visible, so a review at four to six weeks is useful.

## Stopping It Coming Back

Relapse is common, especially in hot, humid climates like Chennai. Options include:

- A once-a-month wash with an anti-yeast shampoo during the warm months, as advised.
- Loose, breathable cotton clothing, and changing out of sweaty clothes promptly.
- Not sharing towels.
- Treating oily skin or dandruff, which can feed the yeast. See [dandruff and scalp care](/services/dandruff).

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if patches do not clear with a couple of weeks of treatment, are very widespread, or if you are not sure whether they might be vitiligo. Read more on [pityriasis versicolor](/conditions/pityriasis-versicolor) and [fungal infections](/services/fungal-infections).
