Two red birthmarks are often confused, but they behave very differently, and so does their treatment.

## The Differences

| | Haemangioma | Port-wine stain |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance at birth | Often absent or a pale patch | Present at birth, flat pink to red |
| Growth | Grows quickly for months | Grows with the child |
| Course | Shrinks over years | Stays for life, darkens with age |
| Treatment | Medicine, often propranolol | Pulsed dye laser |

## Haemangiomas

Most are small and need no treatment. Those near the eye, nose or mouth, on the lips, ulcerated, large or multiple should be seen early. Propranolol, started in the first weeks, shrinks many haemangiomas, and works best during the growth phase. Lasers help leftover redness and ulcers. See [infantile haemangioma](/conditions/infantile-haemangioma).

## Port-Wine Stains

A port-wine stain is a patch of widened blood vessels. It does not fade, and in adulthood it can thicken and form bumps. **Early treatment with a pulsed dye laser,** starting in infancy, gives the best results. Treatment is repeated every two to three months for several sessions, and the colour lightens gradually. Many are not completely cleared, and maintenance sessions may be needed.

**On the forehead or around the eye,** a port-wine stain should be checked for links to eye pressure problems and neurological conditions.

## What Laser Treatment Involves

- Numbing cream is applied, and in young children, treatment is often done with sedation or in a specialised setting.
- Eye protection is worn.
- The laser feels like a rubber band snap, and the area looks bruised for about a week.
- Sun protection is vital between sessions.

## Risks

Bruising, blistering and temporary pigment change. Scarring is uncommon with an expert team.
