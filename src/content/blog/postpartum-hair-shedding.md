If you've had a baby in the last few months and are suddenly noticing a lot more hair coming out in the shower or brush, you're not imagining it, and in most cases, this isn't a sign that something has gone wrong.

## Why It Happens

During pregnancy, elevated hormone levels keep more hair than usual in its active growth phase, which is why many women notice thicker, fuller hair while pregnant. After delivery, hormone levels drop sharply, and that extra hair shifts into its resting phase together, shedding over the following months rather than gradually as it normally would.

## The Typical Timeline

Shedding usually starts around two to three months after delivery, peaks somewhere in that window, and then gradually tapers off. Most women see this settle back to normal within six to twelve months. It can feel alarming in the moment, especially if you're already sleep-deprived and adjusting to a new baby, but it is a [well-recognized, temporary pattern](/conditions/telogen-effluvium).

## When It's Worth a Visit

If shedding continues well beyond a year, or if you're noticing thinning that seems disproportionate or isn't following this typical improving pattern, it's worth [an evaluation](/services/female-hair-loss). Iron deficiency and thyroid changes are both common in the postpartum period and can compound normal postpartum shedding -- these are identifiable and very treatable when caught.

## The Reassurance That Actually Helps

For most new mothers we see with this concern, the single most helpful thing we can offer is accurate information: this is expected, it has a typical timeline, and it resolves on its own for the large majority of women. We're also happy to check the less common contributing factors if your pattern doesn't match this typical course, or if the reassurance alone doesn't put your mind at ease.

## What Helps While You Wait

Be gentle with your hair: avoid tight ponytails, harsh brushing and heat styling where you can, and use a mild shampoo. A haircut or layers can make thinner areas look fuller while hair regrows. Eat a balanced diet with enough protein and iron, and continue any prenatal vitamins advised by your doctor. If you are breastfeeding, check before starting any new supplement or medicine.

## Why You Might See Short "Baby Hairs"

As hair regrows after the shedding phase, you may notice short new hairs along the hairline, often standing up. This is a good sign that regrowth is under way. Hair usually takes several months to catch up in length and density.

## When Thinning Does Not Follow the Pattern

If you notice a widening parting, thinning at the crown or hairline recession that continues, [pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss) may have been unmasked by the postpartum change. This is separate from simple shedding and benefits from assessment and treatment. Our guide to [understanding hair loss causes](/blog/understanding-hair-loss-causes) helps separate the different patterns.
