Good preparation makes the day of your hair transplant smoother and supports healing afterwards. Most of what helps is simple and practical. This checklist covers what to tell us, what to adjust, and what to arrange. We will give you personalised written instructions before your procedure, and those take precedence over any general guidance here.

## Weeks Before: Health and Medicines

**Share your full medical history.** Tell us about conditions such as diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, bleeding or clotting problems, thyroid disease, and any history of keloid or thick scarring. Also mention allergies, particularly to local anaesthetic or medicines, and any previous surgery.

**List every medicine and supplement you take.** Include prescription medicines, over-the-counter drugs, herbal products, and vitamins. Some medicines and supplements can affect bleeding, such as blood thinners, certain pain relievers, and some herbal products. Do not stop any prescribed medicine on your own. We will advise which, if any, need to be adjusted and when.

**Get any recommended tests done.** We may ask for blood tests or other checks depending on your history, to make sure it is safe to proceed.

**Stabilise other health problems.** If you have uncontrolled blood pressure or blood sugar, it needs to be managed beforehand.

## Lifestyle Before the Procedure

**Smoking.** Smoking can impair blood flow and healing, and we advise stopping for a period before and after surgery. If you smoke, ask us how long to abstain.

**Alcohol.** Alcohol can affect bleeding and healing, so we advise avoiding it for a period before and after the procedure.

**Haircuts and styling.** Follow our advice on hair length in the donor area. Do not have chemical treatments, colouring, or aggressive styling shortly beforehand unless advised.

**Sun and scalp care.** Avoid sunburn on the scalp in the weeks before surgery. Treat any scalp problems, such as dandruff or irritation, early and tell us about them.

## Skincare and Hair Products

Follow any specific instructions about shampoos, scalp treatments, or medicated products. If you are using [medical treatment for hair loss](/blog/medical-therapy-with-hair-transplant), continue it unless told otherwise.

## The Day Before

**Rest and eat well.** A good night's sleep helps. Eat normally, stay hydrated, and avoid heavy alcohol.

**Clothing.** Plan to wear a loose, front-opening shirt or button-down top, so you do not have to pull anything over your head afterwards.

**Wash your hair.** Follow our instructions about washing before the procedure.

**Plan the logistics.** Confirm timing, how long you should expect to be at the clinic, and who will be with you.

## On the Day

Eat a proper meal beforehand unless advised otherwise, and bring any medicines you take regularly. Arrive on time, and bring something to occupy you, such as a book, music, or headphones, since the procedure takes several hours with breaks. Wear comfortable clothes.

The procedure is done under local anaesthesia, so you are awake but comfortable. We will explain each stage and encourage you to speak up if you feel any discomfort.

## Arrange Your Recovery

**Transport and company.** Arrange for someone to accompany you home if advised, and avoid driving yourself if you feel tired or unwell.

**Time off work.** Many patients with desk-based jobs return within a few days, though visible redness and scabbing may take about a week or more to settle. Physical work and exercise usually need a longer break. Plan for this ahead of time.

**Sleeping setup.** You will usually be advised to sleep with your head raised for the first few days. Arrange pillows in advance, and consider a travel pillow to avoid pressure on the grafts.

**Supplies.** Have the recommended gentle shampoo, saline spray if advised, and prescribed medicines ready at home.

**Social plans.** Be realistic about events, photographs, or travel in the first couple of weeks. Visible scabs and redness are temporary but noticeable.

## Questions to Ask Before You Go In

What will the aftercare routine be? When can I wash my hair, and how? When can I exercise? When can I return to work? Whom do I call if I am worried? What will follow-up involve?

## Set Your Expectations

Remember that shedding of the transplanted hair is a [normal stage in the first weeks](/blog/hair-transplant-timeline-month-by-month), and that results take about a year. Having this clear in advance makes recovery less stressful.

## The Bottom Line

Preparation is mostly about honesty, planning, and following instructions. If anything in your health or routine is unclear, ask before the day. We would much rather answer a question beforehand than deal with a problem afterwards.
