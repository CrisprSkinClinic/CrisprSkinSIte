Diet cannot cure psoriasis, but some measures help.

## Weight

Excess weight is linked to more severe psoriasis and reduces the response to treatment. In people who are overweight, weight loss improves psoriasis, and can make treatments work better.

## Alcohol

Higher alcohol intake is linked to more severe psoriasis and can interact with medicines such as methotrexate. Reducing or stopping alcohol helps.

## Gluten

A gluten-free diet helps only in people who have coeliac disease or positive coeliac antibodies. Others should not cut gluten.

## Mediterranean-Style Eating

A diet rich in vegetables, fruit, pulses, nuts and fish, with less processed and red meat, is sensible for overall health and the heart, which matters in psoriasis. Evidence for direct skin benefit is limited.

## Supplements

Fish oil and vitamin D have not shown reliable benefit for psoriasis skin, unless a deficiency is present.

## What to Avoid

Extreme "detox" diets, and stopping prescribed treatment in favour of food changes.

## Weight and Psoriasis

People with obesity are more likely to develop psoriasis, have more severe disease, and respond less well to treatment. Fat tissue releases inflammatory chemicals that fuel psoriasis. In trials, people who lost weight through a low-calorie diet saw improved severity scores, and better responses to biologics and other medicines. Losing five to ten percent of body weight is a realistic target for many.

## Alcohol

Heavy drinking is linked to more severe psoriasis, more flares and poorer response to treatment. Alcohol also increases risk of liver problems with methotrexate and other medicines. Reducing intake or avoiding alcohol altogether is advised during such treatment, and your doctor can guide you.

## Gluten

About 10 to 15 percent of psoriasis patients have gluten sensitivity markers, and some of these may improve on a gluten-free diet. Testing for coeliac antibodies identifies those people. If tests are negative, there is no proven benefit, and a gluten-free diet is restrictive and costly.

## Other Diets and Nutrients

**Mediterranean-style diet:** an eating pattern rich in vegetables, fruit, pulses, fish, olive oil and nuts. It may reduce cardiovascular risk, and some studies suggest lower psoriasis severity.

**Fish oil:** results are inconsistent.

**Vitamin D:** useful for bones if deficient, but oral supplements have not shown benefits for skin in trials in people with normal levels.

**Fasting and trendy diets:** insufficient evidence, and some fasting can cause flares or stress.

## Why Psoriasis and Heart Health Are Linked

Psoriasis increases cardiovascular risk. Eating well, exercising, controlling blood pressure and cholesterol, and avoiding smoking matter as much as the skin treatment, and are part of comprehensive care.

## Supplements and Herbal Remedies

Some herbal products are marketed for psoriasis, and a few can cause liver injury or interact with prescribed medicines. Discuss any product with your doctor first.

## Practical Steps

1. Aim for a healthy weight, with help from a dietitian if needed.
2. Cut back on alcohol.
3. Eat plenty of vegetables, pulses, fruit and fish if you eat them.
4. Ask about coeliac testing if you have digestive symptoms.
5. Keep following your treatment plan.
