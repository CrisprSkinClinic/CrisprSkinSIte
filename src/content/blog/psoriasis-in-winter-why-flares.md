Many people with psoriasis notice that it gets worse in the cooler months. Several things add up.

## Why Winter Can Make It Worse

Dry air cracks and irritates plaques. Less sunlight means less natural UV, which usually helps psoriasis. Colds and throat infections are common triggers, and so are stress and changes in routine.

## What Helps

- Thick, plain moisturiser to soften scale and reduce itching, applied after bathing.
- Short, lukewarm showers without scrubbing.
- Continuing your prescribed treatment, even when you feel better.
- Treating sore throats promptly, as strep infections can trigger guttate psoriasis in some.
- Managing stress, sleep and alcohol.

## Scalp and Nails

The scalp often itches and flakes more in dry weather. See our page on [scalp psoriasis](/conditions/scalp-psoriasis) and the guide to the [psoriasis treatment ladder](/blog/psoriasis-treatment-ladder).

## When to Step Up Treatment

If plaques are spreading, painful, or affecting joints, tell your doctor. Persistent or widespread disease may need a stronger plan, including [biologics](/services/biologics) in selected patients, and vaccine planning beforehand.

## The Science Behind Winter Flares

Sunlight has a gentle anti-inflammatory effect on psoriasis plaques, and many people improve in summer. Shorter days, less skin exposure and lower UV in winter remove that benefit. Cold, dry air also dehydrates plaques, so they scale and crack, and infections are more common indoors.

## Triggers to Watch For

- **Strep throat and respiratory infections,** a classic trigger of small drop-like (guttate) psoriasis, especially in young people.
- **Stress,** including festive and exam periods.
- **Alcohol,** heavier consumption in the holiday season.
- **Skin injury** from scratching or friction, which can trigger new plaques at the same spot.
- **Certain medicines,** such as some blood-pressure medicines, anti-malarials, lithium and sudden withdrawal of steroid tablets. Tell your doctor about everything you take.

## Practical Winter Care

1. **Moisturise** thickly, after every bath.
2. **Do not scrub** plaques. Soak briefly and let scale soften with ointment.
3. **Keep up your treatment,** even when skin looks better. Stopping often leads to a bounce-back.
4. **Get sensible sun** where possible, unless your doctor has said otherwise, and do not burn.
5. **Look after your joints.** Morning stiffness, swollen fingers or heel pain can signal psoriatic arthritis, which needs early treatment. See [psoriatic arthritis](/conditions/psoriatic-arthritis).

## Scalp, Nails and Folds

The scalp often flares in winter, and thick scale can be softened with an oil or a keratolytic product overnight, then washed out. Nails may thicken or pit. Skin folds can develop smooth red patches rather than scale, and need gentle, specific treatment.

## When Creams Are Not Enough

If more than a hand-sized area is involved, if plaques are on visible areas, or if the condition affects work and sleep, treatment can be stepped up. Options include phototherapy, tablets, and biologics for suitable people. Our guide to the [psoriasis treatment ladder](/blog/psoriasis-treatment-ladder) explains each step.

## Vaccines and Infections

Because infections can trigger flares and some treatments lower immunity, being up to date with influenza, pneumococcal and other vaccines is sensible. See [vaccines and autoimmune skin disease](/blog/vaccines-and-psoriasis-vitiligo-alopecia-flares-myths).
