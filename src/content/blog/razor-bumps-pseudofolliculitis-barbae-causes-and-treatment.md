Razor bumps are small, itchy or tender spots that appear after shaving, particularly on the beard area and neck in men, and on the bikini area, legs and underarms in women. Curly or coarse hair is the main risk.

## Why They Happen

After a close shave, a sharp-tipped hair can curl back into the skin (growing in) or get trapped beneath it. The skin reacts with inflammation, forming red bumps or pustules, and can leave dark marks, especially in brown skin.

## Who Gets Them

People with tightly curled hair, which is common in South Asian and African hair, those who shave very close or against the grain, and those who stretch the skin while shaving.

## Shaving Habits That Help

1. **Soften** the beard with warm water first.
2. **Use a clean, sharp razor** or a single-blade, and change blades often.
3. **Shave with the grain** (the direction of growth), not against it.
4. **Do not stretch** the skin or go over the same area repeatedly.
5. **Leave some stubble** instead of a very close shave, using a longer setting on a trimmer.
6. **Rinse** with cool water and moisturise.
7. **Avoid** picking out ingrown hairs with tweezers when the skin is inflamed.

## Treatment

- **A gentle exfoliant,** such as salicylic acid or glycolic acid, used on non-shaving days.
- **A retinoid** at night to reduce plugging.
- **Benzoyl peroxide or a topical antibiotic** for inflamed bumps.
- **Short courses of anti-inflammatory creams** for flares, under advice.
- **A break from shaving** for a few weeks lets the bumps settle.
