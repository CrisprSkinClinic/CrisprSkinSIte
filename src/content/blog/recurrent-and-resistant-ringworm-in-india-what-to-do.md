Over the last several years, dermatologists in India have seen a surge in widespread, stubborn and repeatedly returning ringworm. Patients tell the same story: a cream, then another, a few weeks of tablets, brief improvement and relapse. This guide explains what is going on and what actually helps.

## What Has Changed

Several things have come together:

- **More widespread infection.** Larger patches, involving the groin, thighs, buttocks, trunk and sometimes the face, often more inflamed than the old textbook ring.
- **Overuse of steroid and combination creams.** These make the infection spread and alter its look, so it is harder to recognise. See [steroid creams and ringworm](/blog/steroid-creams-and-tinea-why-they-make-it-worse).
- **Incomplete courses.** Treatment stopped when the itch settled, leaving fungus behind. See [why tinea treatment fails](/blog/tinea-treatment-why-it-fails-and-compliance).
- **Changing fungal strains.** Reports from India and elsewhere describe strains of dermatophytes that respond less well to some commonly used antifungal tablets, including terbinafine. Some patients need a different medicine or a longer course.
- **Household spread.** Shared towels, clothes, bedding and close contact keep the infection circulating in families.

## Do I Have Resistant Ringworm?

True resistance is less common than incomplete treatment, a wrong diagnosis or reinfection. Warning signs that your doctor will consider:

- No clear improvement after a properly taken course of treatment.
- Quick relapse after stopping.
- Many family members affected.
- A history of repeated steroid cream use.

## What Dermatologists Do

1. **Confirm the diagnosis** with a skin scraping under the microscope, and a fungal culture where possible. Some rashes that look like ringworm are eczema, psoriasis or other problems.
2. **Stop steroid creams** and treat the skin damage they have caused.
3. **Choose the medicine** based on the pattern and the response so far. This may mean a different antifungal tablet, a higher dose and a longer course, taken with fat-containing food for better absorption, guided by monitoring.
4. **Check safety.** Antifungal tablets can affect the liver and interact with other medicines, so your doctor will ask about your health and medicines and may order blood tests.
5. **Treat everyone and everything:** family, feet and nails, and clothing. See [preventing tinea from coming back](/blog/tinea-recurrence-prevention-household-and-hygiene).
6. **Review** until the skin is clear, and then again after a few weeks.

## What You Can Do

- Do not buy antifungal tablets or creams without a prescription, and do not use steroid creams.
- Complete the course your doctor gives, and keep follow-up appointments.
- Bring every cream and tablet you have used to the visit.
- Wash clothes and bedding in hot water, iron them or dry them in the sun.
- Keep skin dry, and wear loose cotton.

## How Long Does It Take?

Resistant or widespread ringworm often needs weeks to a few months of treatment, longer than people expect, and the dark marks left behind can take months to fade even after the infection has gone. Persistence pays off, and most people do clear with the right plan.
