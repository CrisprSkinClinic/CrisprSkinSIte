One boil is a nuisance. Repeated boils are a sign to look for a reason.

## Why Boils Recur

- **Carriage of Staphylococcus aureus** in the nose, armpits or groin, which reseeds the skin.
- **Diabetes or high blood sugar,** which weakens defences.
- **Anaemia or iron deficiency,** and other nutritional issues.
- **Skin conditions** such as eczema and scabies, which break the skin.
- **Household spread,** through shared towels and razors.
- **Obesity, friction and heavy sweating.**
- **Hidradenitis suppurativa,** which can look like boils but is a different condition. See [hidradenitis suppurativa](/conditions/hidradenitis-suppurativa).
- Rarely, **weak immunity.**

## What the Doctor Does

A history and examination, a swab from a boil for culture, and tests such as blood sugar, blood count and iron. A nose swab may check for carriage, and household members may need review.

## Treatment

1. **Treat the current boil:** warm compresses, drainage when ready, and antibiotics if needed, guided by culture.
2. **Treat the cause:** control diabetes, correct anaemia, treat eczema or scabies.
3. **Decolonisation,** for carriers: a few days of a nasal antibiotic ointment, antiseptic body wash and attention to hygiene, repeated at intervals as advised, often treating household members as well.

## Practical Hygiene

- Wash hands often, and especially after touching a boil.
- Do not share towels, razors or clothing.
- Wash towels and bedding hot, and change them often.
- Cover draining boils with a clean dressing.
- Shower after exercise, and wear clean, loose clothing.
- Avoid squeezing boils.

## What Does Not Help

Long courses of antibiotics without a plan, unproven herbal pastes and scrubbing skin harshly.

## When to Be Concerned

Fever, a boil on the face, spine or hands, rapidly spreading redness, or recurrent boils in a child or in someone unwell need prompt review.
