Retinoids, derived from vitamin A, are among the most thoroughly studied ingredients in dermatology. They are also easy to use badly.

## What They Do

They speed up skin renewal, help unclog pores, and over time can improve fine lines, texture, and uneven tone.

## Retinol vs. Prescription Retinoids

Over-the-counter retinol is converted by the skin into its active form, so it tends to be gentler and slower. Prescription retinoids are stronger and used for [acne](/services/acne) and more significant [ageing changes](/conditions/photoageing). Which suits you depends on your skin and goals.

## How to Start

Use a small amount, two or three evenings a week, on dry skin, and build up gradually. Pair it with a plain moisturizer and use [sunscreen](/blog/sunscreen-and-visible-light-indian-skin) every morning.

## What Is Normal

Some dryness and flaking in the first few weeks is common. Severe burning, swelling, or persistent peeling is not, and means you should stop and speak to us (see [skin barrier damage](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier)).

## Pregnancy

Retinoids are avoided in pregnancy and when trying to conceive. Tell your doctor if you may be pregnant.

## Patience

Visible results usually take months. Consistency matters more than strength.

## Which Strength and Form

Retinoids come as creams, gels and serums. Creams are gentler and suit dry or sensitive skin, while gels can feel lighter on oily skin but may irritate more. Start with the lowest suitable strength and the gentlest base, and move up only when your skin is comfortable. Adapalene is a retinoid available without a prescription in some forms and is often a sensible starting point for acne.

## The "Sandwich" Method

If your skin is sensitive, apply moisturizer first, then a pea-sized amount of the retinoid, then moisturizer again. This lowers irritation while still delivering benefit. Avoid applying retinoids to wet skin right after washing, since damp skin absorbs more and may sting more.

## What to Avoid While Starting

Do not use scrubs, strong exfoliating acids or waxing on the same area. Avoid applying near the corners of the nose, mouth and eyes, where skin is thinner. If you are planning a [chemical peel](/services/chemical-peels), tell us you use a retinoid so we can time it properly.

## Skin Types and Dark Marks

In deeper skin tones, too much irritation can leave dark marks, so gentle, gradual use is especially important.
