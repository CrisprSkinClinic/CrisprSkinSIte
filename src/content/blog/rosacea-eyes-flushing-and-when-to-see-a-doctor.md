Rosacea is not only a skin condition. In many people it also affects the eyes, and troublesome flushing deserves its own attention.

## Eye Rosacea

Up to half of people with rosacea have eye symptoms, sometimes before any skin changes. Signs include:

- **Dry, gritty or burning eyes,** as if there is sand in them.
- **Red, bloodshot eyes** and watering.
- **Red, swollen eyelids** with crusting at the lash base.
- **Recurrent styes or chalazia** (eyelid lumps).
- **Sensitivity to light** and blurred vision that clears on blinking.

## Why It Matters

Untreated, eye rosacea can inflame the cornea (the clear front of the eye) and, rarely, damage vision. Persistent eye pain, marked light sensitivity or loss of vision needs urgent attention.

## Care and Treatment

- **Lid hygiene:** warm compresses and gentle lid cleaning daily.
- **Lubricating eye drops** for dryness.
- **Oral low-dose doxycycline** in moderate to severe cases, as part of treatment of rosacea.
- **Review by an eye doctor** for persistent symptoms, with the dermatologist and ophthalmologist working together.

## Flushing

Flushing is a sudden reddening of the face, neck and chest with a feeling of heat, lasting minutes. In rosacea, it is triggered by heat, alcohol, spicy food, stress and exercise.

**Managing it:**
- Identify and limit triggers.
- Cool the face and body quickly.
- Medicines can help persistent flushing, such as redness-reducing gels and, in selected patients, tablets that blunt flushing.

## When Flushing Needs Checking

Flushing with sweating, diarrhoea, palpitations, wheeze, weight loss or fainting is not typical rosacea and may need tests. Flushing from medicines and menopause is also common. See a doctor if your flushing is new, severe or has other symptoms.
