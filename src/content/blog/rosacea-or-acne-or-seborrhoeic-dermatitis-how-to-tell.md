Red, bumpy facial skin has many causes, and three common ones look alike. Getting the right label matters, because treatments differ and the wrong one can worsen the skin.

## Quick Comparison

| | Rosacea | Acne | Seborrhoeic dermatitis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age | Usually over 30 | Teens and adults | Any adult |
| Blackheads | No | Yes | No |
| Flushing | Yes | No | No |
| Visible vessels | Often | No | No |
| Scale and greasy flakes | No | No | Yes |
| Typical sites | Cheeks, nose, chin, forehead | Face, chest, back | Eyebrows, sides of nose, scalp |
| Stinging with products | Yes | Not typically | Mild itch |

## Rosacea

Persistent redness, easy flushing, visible small vessels and red bumps without blackheads, often with stinging or burning. See [rosacea](/conditions/rosacea).

## Acne

Blackheads and whiteheads, with inflamed spots and, in adults, often along the jawline. It may leave scars and marks. See [acne treatment](/services/acne).

## Seborrhoeic Dermatitis

Red patches with greasy, yellowish scale around the eyebrows, sides of the nose, behind the ears and on the scalp, with itch. See [seborrhoeic dermatitis](/blog/seborrhoeic-dermatitis-of-the-face-and-scalp-flares-and-control).

## They Can Overlap

Many adults have two of them. A person may have acne and seborrhoeic dermatitis, or rosacea with scalp dandruff.

## Why It Matters

Acne treatments such as benzoyl peroxide, retinoids and strong acids often burn rosacea. Steroid creams can calm all three briefly but worsen rosacea and acne over time. Antifungal creams help seborrhoeic dermatitis but not acne or rosacea.

## Other Conditions That Look Similar

Perioral dermatitis, contact allergy, lupus and steroid-induced rashes. A doctor can distinguish these by examination, and sometimes tests.

If redness, flushing or burning is the main feature, or acne treatments irritate your skin, see a dermatologist before trying more products. See our [rosacea service](/services/rosacea) and [dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis service](/services/dandruff).
