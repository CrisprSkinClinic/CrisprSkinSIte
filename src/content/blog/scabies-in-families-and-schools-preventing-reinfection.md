Scabies spreads easily in families, hostels and schools. Stopping it takes coordinated effort, not just treating the person who is itching.

## How It Spreads

Mites pass on through prolonged skin-to-skin contact, such as sharing a bed, hugging or caring for a child, and less often through clothes, towels and bedding. The first time you catch scabies, itching starts two to six weeks later, so people can carry and spread mites before they have any symptoms. This is why contacts must be treated even when well.

## Who Should Be Treated?

- Everyone who lives in the same home.
- Partners, and anyone who has shared a bed.
- Caregivers and childminders with close contact.
- Close friends or relatives with prolonged contact.

All are treated on the same day, and again a week later.

## Cleaning the Home

- Wash clothes, towels and bedding from the previous three days in hot water and dry them hot or in strong sun.
- Seal non-washable items such as soft toys and shoes in a bag for several days.
- Vacuum mattresses, sofas and carpets, and discard the vacuum bag.
- There is no need to disinfect the whole house, or use sprays.

## Schools and Hostels

Children can usually return after the first treatment. Schools should be told so that others can check for symptoms, since an outbreak needs a shared response. In hostels and care homes, treat everyone in the outbreak together, with staff guidance.

## Why Scabies Comes Back

- Contacts not treated, or treated on different days.
- Skipping the repeat after a week.
- Missed areas in application (nails, folds, between the toes).
- Bedding and clothing not cleaned.
- New contact from an untreated person.

## Looking After Itch and Sleep

Moisturise, use cool compresses and give an antihistamine at night if advised. Keep nails short, especially for children, to avoid infected scratches. See [impetigo in children](/blog/impetigo-in-children-treatment-school-and-spread) for a common complication.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor for itching in babies, in people with weak immunity, with crusted skin, or when itching persists beyond four weeks.
