There is a wide choice of scar treatments, and the best ones depend on the scar type. Here is what each does and where it fits.

## Silicone Gel and Sheets

**What it does.** Hydrates the scar and softens and flattens new raised scars. It is also used to prevent them after surgery.

**Best for.** New hypertrophic scars, and prevention in scar-prone people.

**Points.** Worn or applied daily for two to three months. Safe, simple, and evidence-based.

## Steroid Injections

**What it does.** Injected into the scar, they reduce overgrowth and soften and flatten raised scars.

**Best for.** Hypertrophic scars and keloids.

**Points.** Repeated every three to six weeks. Risks include thinning of the skin and lightening of the colour around the injection, which is why dose and technique matter.

## Cryotherapy (Freezing)

**What it does.** Freezes the scar from within or the surface, shrinking it.

**Best for.** Some keloids and hypertrophic scars, often with injections.

**Points.** Can lighten darker skin, so used carefully.

## Laser and Light

**What it does.** Vascular lasers reduce redness and thickness of raised scars. Fractional lasers improve texture of flat and atrophic scars.

**Best for.** Red or raised scars, and texture improvement.

**Points.** More risk of pigment change in darker skin, so the type and settings matter.

## Microneedling

**What it does.** Stimulates collagen, smoothing shallow scars and improving texture.

**Best for.** Shallow atrophic scars and stretch marks. See [microneedling for acne scars](/blog/microneedling-for-acne-scars-what-to-expect).

## Subcision

**What it does.** Releases the bands that pull rolling scars down, so they lift.

**Best for.** Rolling and some boxcar scars.

## Fillers

**What it does.** Temporarily lifts depressed scars.

**Best for.** Selected soft, depressed scars, with effects lasting months.

## Surgical Revision

**What it does.** Cuts out the scar and re-closes the wound so it heals finer, or reshapes a wide or badly placed scar.

**Best for.** Wide, stretched, contracted or badly positioned scars, once mature.

**Points.** Never used alone on a keloid. Surgery for scar-prone people is combined with injections and pressure.

## Combination Treatment

Most scars need a combination, for example subcision with microneedling for sunken scars, or surgery with steroid injections for keloids. Treatment is staged, with healing time between steps.

## What to Expect

Improvement, not erasing. A reasonable aim is a scar that is flatter, softer, lighter and less noticeable. Photographs track progress.
