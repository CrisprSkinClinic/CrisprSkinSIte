Seborrhoeic dermatitis is a common, long-lasting but harmless skin condition. It is dandruff's more inflamed relative, and it affects the face as well as the scalp.

## Where It Appears

The scalp, eyebrows, eyelid edges, sides of the nose, behind the ears, in the beard area and sometimes the chest. Skin looks red or pink with greasy yellowish scale, and may itch or burn.

## What Causes It

A normal skin yeast, Malassezia, living in oily areas, and the body's inflammatory reaction to it. It is not caused by poor hygiene. Flares follow stress, poor sleep, illness, cold dry weather, heavy sweating and some medicines. It is also more common in people with Parkinson's disease, HIV and certain other conditions.

## Treatment

- **Scalp:** medicated shampoos, used correctly. See [dandruff treatment: medicated shampoos](/blog/dandruff-treatment-medicated-shampoos-how-to-use-them).
- **Face:** an antifungal cream such as ketoconazole or ciclopirox, and a short course of a mild anti-inflammatory cream for a red flare.
- **Eyelids:** gentle lid cleaning, as advised by a doctor.
- **Beard:** an antifungal wash, and regular washing.

Strong steroid creams on the face for long periods are not advised, since they can thin the skin and cause rebound redness.

## Keeping It Under Control

It tends to come and go, so a maintenance routine of a shampoo or cream once or twice a week prevents most flares. Manage stress, sleep and avoid harsh products.

## Is It Something Else?

Rosacea, psoriasis, eczema and contact allergy can mimic it. See a doctor if the face does not settle. See [scalp psoriasis](/conditions/scalp-psoriasis).
