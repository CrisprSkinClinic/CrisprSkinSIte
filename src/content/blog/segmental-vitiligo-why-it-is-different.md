Most people with vitiligo have the non-segmental type, with patches that often appear on both sides of the body. Segmental vitiligo is a distinct pattern that behaves differently enough that it deserves its own plan. Recognising it early makes a real difference to the treatment path.

## What It Looks Like

In segmental vitiligo, depigmentation is confined to one segment or area of the body, such as one side of the face, one side of the trunk, or one limb. It does not cross the midline, and it often follows a band-like or patch-like distribution. It commonly begins in childhood or young adulthood, earlier than the more common type. Many patients have white hairs within the patch, which suggests the pigment cells in the hair follicle have also been affected.

## How It Behaves

The typical pattern is a period of spread over several months, sometimes up to a year or two, followed by stabilisation. After that, it often remains unchanged for years, and does not tend to continue appearing in new places the way the non-segmental type can. Some people with segmental vitiligo later develop non-segmental patches elsewhere, so ongoing awareness matters, but most remain confined.

## Why It Is Different

The exact reasons are not fully understood. The segmental pattern is thought to involve a more localised process than the generalised autoimmune activity seen in non-segmental vitiligo. A practical consequence is that segmental vitiligo tends to respond less well to creams and phototherapy than the non-segmental type, in part because the reservoir of pigment cells in hair follicles in the area is often depleted.

## Medical Treatment

[Creams](/blog/vitiligo-topical-treatments-guide) and [phototherapy](/blog/phototherapy-for-vitiligo-nbuvb-excimer) are still often tried, particularly early in the disease, and may help in some patients. In the active phase, treatment can be useful to try to limit spread. Topical treatment, targeted light treatment such as excimer, and sometimes short courses of other medicines are used depending on the case. However, patients and families should be prepared for the possibility that response may be limited.

## Why Surgery Often Works Well

Because segmental vitiligo tends to stabilise, it is often a good candidate for surgical repigmentation once stability is confirmed, commonly after a sustained period without change. [Surgical techniques](/blog/vitiligo-surgery-options-explained) that move pigment cells into the patch, such as suction blister grafting, cell suspension transfer, or hair follicle transplantation, can give good and lasting results in segmental vitiligo. The tendency of the disease to stay confined helps results last.

The choice of technique depends on the size and location of the patch. Visible sites such as the face and forehead often have high cosmetic value, and results can be very satisfying when patients are well selected.

## In Children

Because segmental vitiligo often begins in childhood, parents are commonly the ones asking about treatment. Early evaluation is worthwhile to confirm the diagnosis and to start sensible treatment during the active phase. Surgery is generally postponed until the child is older, since it requires cooperation and stable disease, and decisions are individualised. Emotional support for the child is important, as patches on the face can affect confidence at school.

## Camouflage and Sun Protection

While waiting for stability or surgery, camouflage cosmetics and daily sun protection are helpful. Sunscreen protects depigmented skin from burning, and reduces tanning of surrounding skin, which makes the contrast less noticeable.

## What to Expect From an Evaluation

We examine the pattern, check for white hairs, use a Wood's lamp to confirm extent, and ask about timing and any change. We also assess whether the disease appears [active or stable](/blog/stable-or-active-vitiligo-why-it-matters), and discuss which options fit your or your child's situation. If the patch is stable, we can talk about surgery realistically, including benefits, risks, and the technique we would consider.

## A Realistic Outlook

Segmental vitiligo can be frustrating because creams often disappoint. But the combination of its tendency to stabilise and the effectiveness of surgery in suitable patients means there is often a good path forward. If you or your child has a patch limited to one area, an early assessment helps classify it and plan treatment.
