Most vitiligo is the non-segmental type. Segmental vitiligo is different enough to deserve its own plan.

## How It Looks

Depigmentation confined to one side or segment of the body, such as one side of the face or one limb. It commonly starts in childhood or young adulthood.

## How It Behaves

It usually spreads over a limited period, often months, then stabilises, and does not tend to continue appearing in new areas the way the common type can.

## Why Creams and Light Often Disappoint

Segmental vitiligo tends to respond less well to medical treatment than non-segmental disease, partly because the hair follicle pigment cell reserves are often affected too.

## Why Surgery Often Works Well

Because segmental disease tends to stabilise, surgical repigmentation is often effective and lasting once stability has been confirmed.

## What to Do

If you or your child has a patch limited to one area, an early evaluation helps classify it and plan treatment. Early treatment can still be useful in the active phase, even though the longer-term solution may be surgical.
