Shingles often begins with burning, tingling or shooting pain on one side of the body, a day or several days before any rash. Then a band of red patches appears, which turns into clustered blisters and crusts over in a week or two.

## Why the First 72 Hours Matter

Antiviral tablets work best when started within about 72 hours of the rash. They shorten the illness, ease symptoms and lower the risk of long-lasting nerve pain. If you suspect shingles, do not wait to see whether it settles.

## What Else Helps

Pain relief, keeping blisters clean and dry, and loose clothing. Avoid bursting blisters. Do not apply home remedies or creams on open blisters, as they can cause infection and scarring.

## Warning Signs

See a doctor the same day if the rash involves the forehead, around or near the eye, or the tip of the nose, as the eye can be affected. A rash in or near the ear with facial weakness, or widespread blisters in someone with weakened immunity, also needs urgent care.

## Can It Spread?

Fluid from blisters can pass the chickenpox virus to someone who has never had chickenpox or the vaccine, causing chickenpox, not shingles. Keep blisters covered and stay away from newborns, pregnant women and people with weak immunity until they have crusted.
