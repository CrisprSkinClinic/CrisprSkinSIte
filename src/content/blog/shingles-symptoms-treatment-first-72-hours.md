Shingles often begins with burning, tingling or shooting pain on one side of the body, a day or several days before any rash. Then a band of red patches appears, which turns into clustered blisters and crusts over in a week or two.

## Why the First 72 Hours Matter

Antiviral tablets work best when started within about 72 hours of the rash. They shorten the illness, ease symptoms and lower the risk of long-lasting nerve pain. If you suspect shingles, do not wait to see whether it settles.

## What Else Helps

Pain relief, keeping blisters clean and dry, and loose clothing. Avoid bursting blisters. Do not apply home remedies or creams on open blisters, as they can cause infection and scarring.

## Warning Signs

See a doctor the same day if the rash involves the forehead, around or near the eye, or the tip of the nose, as the eye can be affected. A rash in or near the ear with facial weakness, or widespread blisters in someone with weakened immunity, also needs urgent care.

## Can It Spread?

Fluid from blisters can pass the chickenpox virus to someone who has never had chickenpox or the vaccine, causing chickenpox, not shingles. Keep blisters covered and stay away from newborns, pregnant women and people with weak immunity until they have crusted.

## Prevention

[The shingles vaccine](/blog/shingles-vaccine-who-should-get-it) greatly reduces the risk and severity. See our page on [herpes zoster](/conditions/herpes-zoster) and our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination).

## Stages of Shingles

**Before the rash (one to five days):** burning, tingling, itching or deep pain in a band on one side of the body or face, sometimes with fever, headache or tiredness. Many people think it is a muscle strain, a heart or a kidney problem.

**Active rash (about one week):** red patches turn into clusters of blisters that fill with fluid and then crust. Pain is often intense, and even light clothing can hurt.

**Healing (two to four weeks):** crusts fall off. Some people have temporary colour change or small scars.

**Afterwards:** nerve pain that continues beyond the rash is called post-herpetic neuralgia. It is more likely in older adults, and with severe pain at the start. Antivirals started early lower the chance.

## How We Confirm and Treat

Shingles is usually diagnosed by its appearance: a one-sided band of blisters that does not cross the midline. We start antiviral tablets without waiting for tests when the picture is clear and the rash is recent. If the picture is unusual, a swab test can confirm it. Pain relief is tailored, and nerve-pain medicines may be added when pain is severe. People with weakened immunity, a rash involving the eye or ear, or a rash that is spreading may need closer follow-up or admission.

## What You Can Do at Home

- Rest, and take pain relief as advised.
- Keep blisters clean and dry. Cool, damp compresses can soothe. Do not burst blisters.
- Wear loose, soft clothing.
- Do not apply creams that were prescribed for something else, such as steroids, on open blisters.
- Wash hands well, and cover the rash, to protect others.
- Avoid contact with newborns, pregnant women who have not had chickenpox, and people with weak immunity until the blisters have crusted.

## Common Myths

**"If the band meets around the waist, it can kill you."** This is an old story with no basis. Shingles follows one nerve on one side and does not circle the body.

**"Shingles means I have a weak body."** It means the virus has reactivated. Age, stress and illness play a part, and some healthy people get it.

**"I had it once, so I am safe."** Second attacks happen, which is why vaccination is advised afterwards.

## After Recovery

Once the rash has healed, ask about [the shingles vaccine](/blog/shingles-vaccine-who-should-get-it) to lower the chance of a repeat attack. If pain persists beyond a month, ask for a review, as treatment of nerve pain works better when started early.
