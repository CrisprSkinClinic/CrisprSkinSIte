Shingles is a painful blistering rash caused by the chickenpox virus, which stays dormant in nerve cells after childhood chickenpox. Later in life, or when immunity dips, it can wake up. The vaccine is the most effective way to prevent it.

## Who Should Take It

The recombinant (non-live) shingles vaccine is recommended for adults from 50 years. It is also advised for adults from 18 years who have weakened immunity, or who are about to start medicines such as [biologics](/blog/biologics-in-dermatology-explained), JAK inhibitors, methotrexate or long-term steroids.

## How It Is Given

It is a two-dose course. For most adults the second dose follows two to six months after the first. When immunity is weakened, the interval can be shortened to one to two months so protection is in place sooner.

## If You Have Already Had Shingles

Take it anyway once the rash has fully healed. Shingles can return, and the vaccine lowers that risk.

## What to Expect Afterwards

A sore arm, tiredness, aches or a mild fever for a day or two are common and show that the vaccine is working. Serious reactions are rare. Seek care for swelling of the face or lips, or difficulty breathing.

## Why It Is Worth It

The main danger of shingles is not the rash but the nerve pain that can follow and last for months, which is hard to treat. Prevention is far easier. Read [what to do in the first 72 hours](/blog/shingles-symptoms-treatment-first-72-hours) if you ever develop the rash.
