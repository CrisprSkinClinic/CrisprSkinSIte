Most moles are harmless, and the right answer for many is to leave them alone. Removal makes sense for certain reasons, and a dermatologist can help you decide.

## Medical Reasons to Remove a Mole

- A mole that has **changed** in size, shape, colour or texture.
- A mole that **bleeds, crusts, becomes sore** or does not heal.
- A mole that looks **different from your other moles**, or a new mole in an adult.
- A mole that **cannot be examined fully** because of its site, for example in the scalp or genital area.
- A mole that is **repeatedly irritated** and inflamed.
- A mole where examination leaves **doubt**.

In these situations, removal serves two purposes: it takes the mole away, and it allows the tissue to be tested. See [do removed moles need testing?](/blog/do-removed-moles-need-testing-histopathology).

## Cosmetic and Comfort Reasons

- A mole in a **visible place** that you dislike.
- A mole that **catches on clothes, jewellery, a razor or a bra strap.**
- A mole that makes you **self-conscious.**

Cosmetic removal is a personal choice, and it is reasonable. We still examine the mole first, to be sure it is benign.

## When It Is Better to Leave a Mole

- Stable moles that you do not mind.
- Moles in sites where removal would leave a worse scar than the mole, such as the chest or shoulders in keloid-prone people.
- Moles in children, where removal is usually delayed unless there is a medical reason, and where large congenital moles need specialist assessment. See [congenital melanocytic naevus](/conditions/congenital-melanocytic-naevus).

## What Is the Chance a Mole Is Cancer?

Very low for any one ordinary mole. But the cost of missing a skin cancer is high, which is why any change deserves examination. Sun protection and regular skin checks by a doctor are sensible, particularly if you have many moles, fair skin, a family history or a history of sunburn.

## Questions to Ask Before Removal

- What do you think this mole is?
- Which method will you use, and why?
- Will the removed tissue be tested?
- What scar can I expect?
- What happens if the report shows something unexpected?
- How do I look after the wound?

## Be Wary Of

Offers to remove moles with creams, lasers or "painless" methods without any examination, and clinics that do not send removed moles for testing.

## Pregnancy and Moles

Moles often darken in pregnancy, and this alone is not a worry. Any mole that changes in other ways should still be examined, and removal is usually delayed until after delivery unless urgent.
