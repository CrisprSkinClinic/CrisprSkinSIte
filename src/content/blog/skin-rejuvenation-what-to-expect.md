Skin rejuvenation covers a range of approaches aimed at improving tone, texture, and overall freshness. The right combination depends on your skin, your goals, and the time you can set aside for recovery.

## Start With Your Concern

Uneven tone, dullness, [early lines](/conditions/photoageing), and textural roughness each respond to different treatments, so naming your priority helps us choose well.

## Treatments Work Best in Combination

[Sun protection](/blog/sunscreen-and-visible-light-indian-skin) and a sound [skincare routine](/blog/skincare-routine-order-and-active-clashes) form the base. In-clinic treatments then build on it, and results are usually gradual across a series of sessions.

## Skin Type Matters

Pigmentation risk and healing differ across skin tones, so settings and treatment choices are individualized rather than standardized.

## Realistic Expectations

Rejuvenation refines and improves; it does not change who you are or freeze time. A clear conversation at the start about what is achievable leads to results you will be happy with.

## What a Typical Plan Looks Like

A plan usually starts with a consultation: we examine your skin, review past treatments and current medicines, and agree on a priority. Treatments are then sequenced. Some work on surface tone and texture, others on pigment or deeper laxity, and spacing the sessions gives the skin time to recover in between. Home care carries as much weight as the visit itself, so daily sunscreen, gentle cleansing and, where suitable, a [retinoid](/blog/retinol-vs-retinoids-how-to-start) are part of the plan from the first week.

## Downtime and Planning Around Your Life

Gentler treatments leave little visible recovery, while stronger ones can cause redness, peeling or temporary darkening for several days. Tell us about weddings, travel and work commitments so we can schedule sensibly instead of squeezing a treatment in just before an event. Avoid picking at flaking skin and avoid strong sun in the days afterwards.

## When We May Ask You to Wait

Some treatments are postponed if you have an active skin infection, cold sores, uncontrolled skin disease, a recent sunburn, or have recently used certain medicines such as isotretinoin. A tendency to scar or to darken after inflammation also changes what is safe for you, so please mention it. For related options, see our page on [skin rejuvenation](/services/skin-rejuvenation) and the notes on [chemical peel aftercare](/blog/chemical-peel-aftercare).

## Before You Come In

Bring a list of the skincare products you use and any procedures you have had before. Photographs of your skin in natural light a few months ago can also help us judge what has changed. Avoid new strong products and sun exposure in the days beforehand so that your skin is calm when we examine it.
