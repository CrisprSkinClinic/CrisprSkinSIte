Online videos and shop products make skin tag removal at home look easy. For most tags, a short visit to a clinic is safer, faster and gives a better result.

## Common Home Methods and Their Problems

**Tying with thread or dental floss.** The aim is to cut off the blood supply. It can be painful, slow, and a tag may become inflamed or infected. The thread can cut through skin unevenly, and the stalk can bleed when the tag falls off.

**Cutting with scissors, nail clippers or a blade.** Non-sterile tools cause infection, and can cause heavy bleeding, particularly with larger tags, which have a blood supply. It is easy to cut too deep or leave the base behind.

**Skin tag removal creams and pens.** These contain strong acids or caustic substances that burn the skin. Burns can scar, leave dark or pale marks and cause infection. Many products do not work, and some have not been properly tested.

**Freezing kits.** Over-the-counter freezing kits are less cold than clinic freezing, and frequently fail or burn nearby skin.

**Natural remedies** such as apple cider vinegar, tea tree oil, garlic or banana peel. Vinegar and strong oils can burn skin. There is no good evidence that any of these work.

## The Bigger Risk: It May Not Be a Skin Tag

Not every small bump on a stalk is a skin tag. Warts, moles, small cysts and other growths can look similar, and a few are not harmless. Removing them at home can hide a problem, cause incomplete removal and delay a diagnosis. A dermatologist can tell the difference, and test anything unusual.

## Risks Around Delicate Areas

Tags on the eyelids, near the eyes, in the groin and around the nipples are close to delicate tissue. Home removal here can cause serious injury and should never be attempted.

## What a Clinic Offers Instead

- A quick examination to confirm it is a skin tag.
- A choice of snip, cautery or freezing, with numbing where needed.
- Sterile technique, and control of bleeding.
- Aftercare and a check that the site is healing.
- Testing of any growth that is not typical.

## If You Have Already Tried at Home

Keep the area clean, do not pick, and see a doctor if there is pain, pus, spreading redness, heavy bleeding or a burn. Treat burns as wounds and protect the area from the sun to reduce marks.

## When a Tag Is Best Seen First

See a dermatologist if a tag is large, bleeding, changes colour, is dark or firm, grows fast, or if you have many tags suddenly. See [why skin tags appear](/blog/why-skin-tags-appear-and-do-they-come-back).
