Skin tag removal is quick and low-risk in a clinic. The three main methods differ in speed, bleeding and marks, and the best one depends on the size and site of the tag.

## 1. Snip Excision

**How.** Sterile fine scissors cut the tag at its base. A numbing cream or a tiny injection is used for larger tags, and a little pressure or a touch of cautery stops any bleeding.

**Best for.** Tags with a thin stalk, in most areas.

**Points.** The quickest method. Bleeding is minimal, and healing takes about a week, leaving a small pink dot.

## 2. Cautery (Electrosurgery)

**How.** A fine electrical tip cuts or burns the tag at its base and seals the tiny blood vessels at the same time.

**Best for.** Small tags, tags with a wide base and those near the eyelids, where bleeding must be tightly controlled.

**Points.** A small scab forms and falls off in one to two weeks. Mild stinging is felt after numbing wears off. Care is needed in darker skin, to avoid a pale or dark mark.

## 3. Cryotherapy (Freezing)

**How.** Liquid nitrogen is applied to the tag for seconds, freezing it. It darkens and falls off within a week or two.

**Best for.** Small tags in areas with thicker skin.

**Points.** Simple and needs no cutting, but may need to be repeated, and can lighten or darken skin around the tag, which is a real concern on the face and in darker skin.

## Choosing the Method

| Tag | Often chosen |
|---|---|
| Small tag with a thin stalk | Snip, or cautery |
| Tag on the eyelid | Snip or cautery, with great care |
| Many tiny tags in the neck | Cautery or snip, in one session |
| Larger or broader tag | Snip with cautery |
| Small tag on thick skin | Freezing |

## What About Numbing?

Most people need only a numbing cream, or a very small injection, and the removal itself takes seconds. Large or many tags may need more injections.

## Aftercare

Keep the area clean and dry, apply the ointment given and let any scab fall by itself. Avoid picking. Use sunscreen on exposed sites, since the new skin darkens easily.

## Possible Problems

Bleeding, infection, a pale or dark mark, a scar (rare), and regrowth if the base was not removed. These are uncommon in a clinic.
