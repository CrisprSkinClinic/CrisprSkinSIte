A split earlobe is more common than most people realise, and the repair is one of the most satisfying small procedures in dermatology. This article explains how it works, the different approaches, and what you can realistically expect.

## How Earlobes Split

The earlobe is made of skin and fat with very little supporting cartilage. A heavy earring wears through the piercing over years, enlarging the hole until the lower edge gives way, or the lobe tears suddenly when an earring is pulled or caught. Over time, the edges of the tear heal separately, each lined with a thin rim of skin, leaving a cleft or a lobe with an enlarged, elongated hole.

## Why It Needs Surgery

Because each side of an old tear is lined with its own skin, the two sides cannot knit back together on their own. The cleft stays, even if the lobe is left alone for years. Repair works by removing this lining so that raw edges can be joined, much like freshening the edges of a wound before closing it.

## The Procedure Step by Step

**1. Anaesthesia.** The lobe is numbed with a small injection of local anaesthetic. You are awake but should feel no pain.

**2. Freshening the edges.** The rim of lined skin along the cleft, or around the enlarged hole, is trimmed away to create clean, raw edges.

**3. Layered closure.** The two sides are brought together and stitched in layers with fine sutures. A layered closure supports the lobe and gives a flatter, stronger scar.

**4. Dressing.** A light dressing is applied, and you receive aftercare instructions.

## The Main Techniques

Different tears call for different closures.

**Straight-line closure.** The simplest approach: the edges are freshened and joined in a straight line. It works well for clean splits, but a straight scar through the lobe can sometimes notch or pull over time.

**Shaped or staggered closure.** The edges are cut so the scar follows a zigzag, stepped, or curved line. This breaks up the scar and reduces the chance of notching as the scar contracts, and it is often preferred for complete splits.

**Techniques that preserve a piercing hole.** If you want to wear earrings through the repaired lobe, the repair can be designed to leave a small opening, or a spot suited to re-piercing later, away from the scar line.

**Reshaping for stretched lobes.** Where the lobe is elongated, a segment of tissue may be reduced so the lobe returns to a more natural length and the hole is repaired at the same time.

We choose according to the type of tear, the thickness of the lobe, and whether you want to wear earrings again.

## Healing

The lobe will be swollen and tender at first. Stitches usually come out after about a week, depending on the technique. Keep the area clean and dry, avoid pulling or sleeping on that side where possible, and follow the instructions you are given. The scar is pink at first and usually fades to a fine line over months.

## Re-Piercing

The repaired lobe needs time before it can take earrings again, commonly several weeks to a few months, and the new piercing is usually placed slightly away from the scar line. See [earlobe repair recovery and re-piercing](/blog/earlobe-repair-recovery-and-re-piercing) for timing and tips.

## Risks

Possible problems include infection, a notch or irregularity along the repair line, widening or opening of the repair if the lobe is pulled, asymmetry between the two ears, and scarring. People who tend to form keloids need special care, as a repair can trigger one. We discuss these risks in advance and give guidance to keep them low.

## Who Is a Good Candidate

Most people with a split, notched, or stretched lobe are suitable. We check your health history and scarring tendency first. If you have had keloids, are prone to thick scars, or have an active infection or other skin problem on the ear, we plan accordingly or advise waiting.

## Realistic Expectations

Repair restores the lobe's shape and continuity, but a fine scar remains and a repaired lobe is a little more vulnerable than an untouched one.
