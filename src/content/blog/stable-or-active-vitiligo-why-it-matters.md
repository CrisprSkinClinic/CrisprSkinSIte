Two people with similar-looking patches can need very different treatment, because one has active disease and the other has stable disease. Knowing which category you are in is one of the most important things we establish at the first visit, and it changes the order and type of treatment, including whether surgery is even an option.

## What "Active" Vitiligo Means

Active vitiligo means the immune process is still destroying pigment cells, so the disease is progressing. Signs include new patches appearing, existing patches enlarging, and patches appearing in areas of friction, pressure, injury, or sunburn, which is called the Koebner phenomenon. Patches may have blurred, poorly defined edges, and in some people there is a pale or slightly inflamed rim around them. A patient may also report that their vitiligo has recently changed or spread, or that it has been getting worse over recent weeks to months.

## What "Stable" Vitiligo Means

Stable vitiligo means pigment loss has stopped progressing. There are no new patches, existing patches are not enlarging, and no new patches appear at sites of injury. The edges tend to be sharp and well defined. Stability is usually judged over a period, commonly six to twelve months, though definitions vary between experts, and this is partly why careful history and records matter.

## Why Vitiligo Can Change

Vitiligo is not a fixed condition. It can be active for a while, then settle, and may flare again later. Stress, illness, sunburn, injury, or other triggers have been linked to flares in some people, though in many, there is no obvious cause. A patch can also be stable while another site becomes active, so assessment considers the whole picture.

## How We Judge Stability

There is no single test, so we combine several sources of information. We take a detailed history of when patches appeared and how they have changed. We examine the borders and look for signs of recent activity. A Wood's lamp can reveal the true extent of depigmentation, including areas not obvious in normal light. We also use photographs taken at intervals, since comparing images over time is one of the most reliable ways to see change. Patients are often asked to take their own photographs in the same light and position.

## Why It Matters for Treatment

**If the disease is active,** the priority is to calm the immune process and limit further loss. Options include [phototherapy](/blog/phototherapy-for-vitiligo-nbuvb-excimer), [topical treatment](/blog/vitiligo-topical-treatments-guide), and in rapidly progressing cases a short, supervised course of oral medicine to halt progress, with careful monitoring. [Surgery](/blog/vitiligo-surgery-options-explained) is avoided, because grafted skin and donor skin can be affected by ongoing disease.

**If the disease is stable,** the aim shifts to restoring pigment. Topical treatment and phototherapy can encourage repigmentation, and for patches that remain resistant, surgical repigmentation becomes a realistic option.

## Why Surgery Needs Stability

The surgical techniques used in vitiligo depend on transferring healthy pigment cells to a patch. If the disease is still active, the immune system may attack the transferred cells, or new patches may appear at the donor site. Waiting for a sustained period of stability gives the best chance of a lasting result.

## What You Can Do

Protect your skin from sunburn and avoid repeated friction or injury over affected areas where you can, since these can trigger new patches in susceptible people. Do not undergo cosmetic procedures such as tattooing, aggressive peels, or laser on active areas without advice, because injury to the skin can provoke new patches. Photograph your patches every few weeks under the same lighting and bring them to your visits, so that we can see the trend.

## Stability Can Be Reassessed

Status is not permanent. We review it over time, and treatment changes accordingly. A person whose disease was active at the first visit may be stable a year later, and then become a candidate for different treatments.

If you are unsure whether your vitiligo is spreading, an assessment gives a clear answer and a plan matched to it, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.
