Two people with similar-looking patches can need very different treatment, because one has active disease and the other has stable disease.

## What Active Means

New patches, enlarging patches, or patches appearing at sites of friction or injury suggest the immune process is still going. The priority is to calm it and limit further loss.

## What Stable Means

No new patches and no enlargement over a sustained period, often six to twelve months. Treatment can focus on restoring pigment.

## How We Judge It

History, serial photographs, examination, and sometimes a Wood's lamp. Review at intervals shows whether things are changing.

## Why It Matters for Surgery

Surgery is considered only for stable disease, because new patches can appear in grafted skin if the disease is still active.

## Practical Steps

Protect skin from sunburn and injury where possible, avoid unnecessary friction on affected areas, and keep a record of any change. Bring photographs to visits so we can see the pattern over time.
