Many people use a cream bought over the counter or given by a pharmacy for an itchy ring-shaped rash. Often it contains a steroid, sometimes combined with an antifungal and an antibacterial. It feels like it works. It usually makes tinea worse.

## What Goes Wrong

Steroids suppress the skin's immune response and reduce redness and itch within days. The fungus, meanwhile, thrives. The rash may fade at the edge but spread outward, lose its typical ring shape, and relapse the moment the cream is stopped. Doctors call this tinea incognito.

## Other Harm

Prolonged steroid use on the groin, face or body can cause thinning of the skin, stretch marks, visible small blood vessels, acne-like spots and dark marks, some of which are permanent.

## Why It Happens

Steroid creams are cheap, quick-acting and widely available. A rash that is itchy and red seems to need one. A proper diagnosis is needed to know when it does not.

## What to Do Instead

- Stop applying steroid or combination creams on a ring-shaped rash unless your doctor has prescribed them.
- Have the rash examined, and, where needed, a skin scraping tested.
- Use an antifungal treatment for the full period advised.

## Already Used Them for Months?

Do not stop abruptly if they were used for a long time on the face, as the skin may flare. See a dermatologist, who will plan the switch.

## How to Recognise the Problem

Tinea incognito, the altered rash after steroid use, often looks like a widespread red or brown scaly area with less obvious ring edges, perhaps with small pimple-like bumps on the border. The itch may be controlled for a few hours or days after application, and return with a vengeance later. Others have burning and a greasy sheen, and thin skin that bruises easily.

## What Is in the Tube?

Many over-the-counter creams combine a steroid, an antifungal and sometimes an antibiotic. The steroid lowers inflammation, and the antifungal in the mixture may be at a dose or combination that is not suited to treating established ringworm. Packaging may not clearly state the steroid, so check the label for names ending in "-sone", "-olone" and "-onide", such as clobetasol, betamethasone or mometasone. If you are unsure, show the tube to your doctor.

## Why Steroids Help Fungus

Steroids suppress immune cells that normally limit fungal growth in the skin. The environment under a steroid-treated rash is warmer, more moist and less defended. This allows fungus to invade deeper, spread more widely and survive treatment.

## Side Effects of Misusing Strong Steroids

- Thin, shiny, fragile skin that tears or bruises.
- Stretch marks, especially in the groin, thighs and armpits.
- Visible small blood vessels.
- Acne-like or perioral (around the mouth) rashes on the face.
- Lightening or darkening of skin colour.
- Infection spreading more widely.
- Rebound flare on stopping.

Some of these changes, especially stretch marks, are permanent.

## What a Dermatologist Does

We take a careful history, including every cream you have used. We examine the skin, and may take a scraping for testing. Treatment includes stopping the steroid cream, which sometimes needs to be gradual, treating the fungus with the right medicines for long enough, and managing skin damage. Recovery of skin takes weeks or months.

## When Steroid Creams Are Appropriate

Steroids are valuable medicines for eczema, psoriasis, allergic reactions and other conditions, in the right strength, for the right period, and with the right instructions. The problem arises when they are used on an undiagnosed rash. See [how much to apply](/blog/how-much-topical-medicine-to-apply-fingertip-unit-guide) for the safe use of prescribed steroids.

Never apply a cream that was prescribed for someone else, or for an older problem, to a new rash. Get a diagnosis first. See [why tinea treatment fails](/blog/tinea-treatment-why-it-fails-and-compliance).
