A common question is whether treatment is forever. For pattern hair loss, the honest answer is that the benefit lasts only as long as the medicine does.

## What Happens

When you stop finasteride, DHT rises back to its previous level. Over six to twelve months, the hair you gained or preserved thins again, and loss resumes at its natural pace. After stopping minoxidil, the extra growth fades in a few months, sometimes with a temporary shed.

## You Do Not Lose More Than You Would Have

Stopping returns you to where you would have been without treatment. It does not make hair worse than baseline, but you lose the years of protection.

## Reasons People Stop

Side effects, planning for a baby, cost, or a feeling that it is "not working". Please talk to your doctor first. Often a dose change, a different formulation or a review fixes the problem.

## If You Are Planning a Pregnancy

Finasteride matters if a partner could be exposed, so discuss it. For women taking medicines for hair loss, the plan must change in pregnancy. Our guide on [women's options](/blog/medical-treatment-for-female-pattern-hair-loss) explains more.

## Cutting Back

Some people reduce to the lowest effective dose after years of stable results. This should be supervised.

## After a Transplant

Medicines protect native hair around transplanted areas, so they are usually continued. See [medicines after a hair transplant](/blog/medical-therapy-with-hair-transplant).

## Timeline After Stopping

**Minoxidil:** within one to three months, some hairs enter the shedding phase, and by six months, hair density returns to what it would have been without treatment. An early shed can feel dramatic, and recovery is possible if you restart.

**Finasteride:** DHT returns to baseline within a couple of weeks, and visible loss follows more slowly, commonly over six to twelve months, as follicles shrink again.

## Is There a Way to Plan a Pause?

Some men stop for a family reason, a medical issue or personal choice. If you are stopping:

- Take photographs before stopping, so you can compare.
- Review in three to six months to see what has changed.
- Think about restarting before significant loss accumulates, since regrowth is greater when follicles are not yet very miniaturised.

## If You Stopped Because of Side Effects

Discuss it. Possible options include a lower or alternate-day dose, a switch to topical finasteride, using minoxidil alone, or a trial off the drug to see if symptoms resolve. Persistent symptoms, particularly low mood or sexual problems that last for weeks after stopping, should be assessed by your doctor.

## Stopping After a Transplant

Transplanted hair is largely permanent, since it comes from the DHT-resistant donor area. But natural hair around it can continue to thin if medicines are stopped, creating an uneven look over years. This is why long-term medical therapy is generally advised. See [medicines after a hair transplant](/blog/medical-therapy-with-hair-transplant).

## Life Events

**Planning a baby:** discuss finasteride and dutasteride with your doctor. For women, oral minoxidil, spironolactone and finasteride need to be stopped before and during pregnancy, and topical minoxidil is usually avoided.

**Surgery or illness:** short interruptions are usually fine, and your doctor will advise.

**Cost or inconvenience:** tell us. A simpler regimen or a different formulation may help.

## What Not to Do

Do not stop abruptly because of one bad month of shedding, since early shedding can occur on minoxidil. Do not swap to online products from unverified sources, and do not double up to "catch up".

## The Larger Picture

Hair loss treatment is like blood pressure treatment: benefit continues while you take it. Decide with your doctor what trade-offs suit you, and review them yearly. See [medical treatment for hair loss](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment).
