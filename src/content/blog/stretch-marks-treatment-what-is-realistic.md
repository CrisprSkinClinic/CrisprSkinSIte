Stretch marks (striae) are very common, affecting most women in pregnancy and many teenagers and men who put on weight or muscle quickly. They are harmless, but many people want them to look less obvious. Knowing what treatment can and cannot do avoids disappointment.

## Why They Form

When the skin stretches faster than it can adapt, the middle layer (dermis) tears, and collagen is rearranged. They are more likely in those with a family history, in pregnancy, in growth spurts, with rapid weight gain or loss, with body building and with long-term steroid use (creams or tablets).

## Two Stages

- **New (striae rubrae):** red, pink or purple, slightly raised and sometimes itchy. They respond best to treatment.
- **Old (striae albae):** white or silvery, flat or slightly sunken. They respond less.

## What Helps New Marks

- **Prescription retinoid cream** (tretinoin) can improve new marks, but must **not be used in pregnancy or while trying to conceive**, and breastfeeding needs advice.
- **Vascular or pulsed dye laser** to reduce redness.
- **Microneedling** to boost collagen.
- **Gentle moisturising** helps itch, though creams do not prevent stretch marks.

## What Helps Old Marks

- **Fractional laser** and **microneedling** can improve texture and appearance modestly over several sessions.
- **Chemical peels** and radiofrequency-based treatments in selected cases.
- Treatments for colour in darker skin, since stretch marks can be darker than the surroundings.

## What Does Not Help Much

Cocoa butter, oils and many creams sold for prevention have not been shown to prevent stretch marks reliably. Surgery such as tummy tuck removes marks on the lower abdomen, but is a major operation for other reasons.

## What Results to Expect

Treatment lowers visibility, often by twenty to fifty percent in suitable marks, with several sessions needed. Complete removal is not realistic. Newer marks do better, and older marks improve less.

## Reducing the Chance

- Gain or lose weight gradually.
- Stay hydrated and eat well.
- Avoid unnecessary long-term steroid creams on the skin, especially in the groin, armpits and thighs.
- Moisturise for comfort, understanding that it may not prevent marks.

## After Pregnancy

Wait until you have finished breastfeeding for treatments that carry any risk, and ask for advice. Treatments such as laser and microneedling can be done after the skin settles.
