Skin cancer is often assumed to be a problem of fair-skinned people in sunny countries. It can affect anyone, and early detection makes a large difference.

## Who Is at Risk

Fair skin carries higher risk, but skin cancer occurs in all skin tones, and in darker skin it is often found later, which can worsen outcomes. In darker skin, some skin cancers arise on the palms, soles, and nails rather than sun-exposed sites.

## Prevention

[Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen](/blog/sunscreen-and-visible-light-indian-skin), shade, protective clothing, and avoiding the strongest midday sun all reduce risk. Avoid tanning beds.

## What to Look For

A new growth that does not heal, a sore that bleeds or crusts repeatedly, [a mole that is changing](/blog/mole-warning-signs), or a dark streak under a [nail](/services/nail-disorders) that is new or widening. Painless changes still count.

## How Often to Check

Look over your skin every month or so, including the scalp, soles, and between the toes, and ask someone to check your back. Those with many moles or a family history should be examined more regularly.

## Early Detection Works

Most skin cancers found early are highly treatable. If you notice something suspicious, [book an examination](/services/warts-moles-skin-tags) rather than waiting to see what happens.
