Many people with deeper skin tones assume they do not need much sun protection. For pigmentation, the opposite is often true.

## Why It Matters

Light, including visible light and not just UV, can stimulate pigment cells. In people prone to [melasma](/conditions/melasma) or dark marks after acne, repeated exposure keeps pigmentation active or brings it back.

## What to Look For

A broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher. For melasma and persistent pigmentation, tinted formulations, which contain pigments that help block visible light, are often preferred.

## How Much and How Often

Most people apply too little. As a rough guide, use about two finger-lengths for the face and neck, and reapply every two to three hours when outdoors.

## Indoors and Cloudy Days

Daylight comes through windows and clouds. If you are prone to pigmentation, daily application is a sensible habit even when mostly indoors.

## Beyond Sunscreen

Hats, shade, and avoiding peak sun add protection. A good plan also treats the [pigmentation](/services/pigmentation) itself, which we can guide you on after an evaluation.
