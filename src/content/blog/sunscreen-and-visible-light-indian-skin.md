Many people with deeper skin tones assume they do not need much sun protection. For pigmentation, the opposite is often true.

## Why It Matters

Light, including visible light and not just UV, can stimulate pigment cells. In people prone to [melasma](/conditions/melasma) or dark marks after acne, repeated exposure keeps pigmentation active or brings it back.

## What to Look For

A broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher. For melasma and persistent pigmentation, tinted formulations, which contain pigments that help block visible light, are often preferred.

## How Much and How Often

Most people apply too little. As a rough guide, use about two finger-lengths for the face and neck, and reapply every two to three hours when outdoors.

## Indoors and Cloudy Days

Daylight comes through windows and clouds. If you are prone to pigmentation, daily application is a sensible habit even when mostly indoors.

## Beyond Sunscreen

Hats, shade, and avoiding peak sun add protection. A good plan also treats the [pigmentation](/services/pigmentation) itself, which we can guide you on after an evaluation.

## Sunscreen Types and Textures

Mineral sunscreens, which contain zinc oxide or titanium dioxide, sit on the skin and can leave a white cast on deeper tones, while newer tinted versions blend more easily. Chemical sunscreens absorb into the skin and usually feel lighter. Neither type is "better" in general. The best sunscreen is the one you will actually apply every day, so try a few textures and pick the one that suits your skin.

## Sensitive and Acne-Prone Skin

Look for labels such as "non-comedogenic" and fragrance-free formulations if your skin is oily or reactive. Gel and fluid textures are often more comfortable in humid weather. If a product stings your eyes or breaks you out, switch rather than stopping sunscreen altogether.

## Do Not Forget the Rest of the Body

Ears, neck, the backs of hands and any area exposed to daily sun deserve protection too. Lips need a lip balm with SPF. Clothing, such as full sleeves, offers reliable cover.

## When Pigmentation Is Already There

If you already have dark patches, sunscreen stops them worsening but will not remove them by itself. For general sun safety, see [sun safety and skin cancer awareness](/blog/sun-safety-and-skin-cancer-awareness).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Applying too little, skipping the neck and ears, forgetting to reapply, and relying on makeup with SPF alone are the usual gaps. Another mistake is changing sunscreens too often, so give a product a fair trial of a few weeks unless it irritates your skin.
