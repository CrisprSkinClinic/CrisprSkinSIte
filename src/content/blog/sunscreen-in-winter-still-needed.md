It is tempting to skip sunscreen when the sun feels gentle. Skin does not agree.

## UVA Does Not Take a Holiday

UVA rays, which cause pigmentation and premature ageing, are present all year and pass through glass and clouds. Visible light, which matters for Indian skin, is there too.

## Melasma and Dark Marks

For melasma and post-acne marks, daily protection is the foundation of treatment. Our guides on [why sun protection matters for melasma](/blog/melasma-sunscreen-and-triggers) and [sunscreen for Indian skin](/blog/sunscreen-and-visible-light-indian-skin) explain why.

## Choosing One for Dry Skin

Look for broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher, in a cream formula that also moisturises. Tinted formulas help with visible light. Apply a generous amount to the face and neck, and reapply every two to three hours if outdoors.

## Under Moisturiser or Over?

Moisturise first, then apply sunscreen as the last step of the morning routine.

## Snow, Hills and Travel

Travelling to cold, high-altitude places increases UV exposure, so protection matters even more there.

## What Sunscreen Does Year-Round

Sunscreen protects against UVB, which causes burning, and UVA, which penetrates deeper, causes tanning, pigmentation and ageing, and is present all year at nearly constant strength. In Chennai, the UV index is high through most of the year, even in the cooler months, and rarely drops to levels where protection is unnecessary.

## Who Benefits Most

- People with **melasma**, post-acne marks or other pigmentation, since sun and visible light are major drivers.
- People treated with **retinoids, peels or lasers**, whose skin is more sensitive.
- People who spend time on the road, on two-wheelers, or near windows.
- Those with **vitiligo**, since unprotected patches burn easily.
- Anyone concerned about fine lines and long-term skin damage.

## How to Choose

- **SPF 30 or above** and **broad-spectrum** (it protects against UVA too).
- For pigmentation, choose a **tinted** formula with iron oxides, which also blocks visible light.
- Texture matters for use: gels and fluids for oily skin, creams for dry skin, mineral formulas for sensitive skin.
- Look for "non-comedogenic" if you get acne.

## How to Apply It

Use about a quarter teaspoon, or two finger-lengths, for the face and neck. Apply it as the last step of the morning skincare routine, ten to fifteen minutes before going out. Reapply every two to three hours if outdoors, or after sweating. For the amounts used by studies, see [how much to apply](/blog/how-much-topical-medicine-to-apply-fingertip-unit-guide).

## Winter-Specific Tips

Choose a moisturising sunscreen when the air is dry. Do not forget ears, neck, the backs of hands and lips (use SPF lip balm). On hill trips, remember that UV increases with altitude and snow reflection.

## Myths

**"I get vitamin D from the sun, so I should skip sunscreen."** Brief, incidental exposure is enough for most people, and deficiency is better handled by testing and supplements.

**"Dark skin does not need sunscreen."** Darker skin burns less but still gets pigmentation, skin cancers and ageing from UV.

**"Sunscreen is only for the beach."** The commonest exposure is everyday, through short, repeated outdoor time.
