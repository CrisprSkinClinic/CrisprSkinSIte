The supplement shelf is full of promises. Here is a reality check.

## Supported When a Deficiency Exists

Iron, vitamin D, vitamin B12 and zinc help when levels are truly low, and your doctor can test for them. In those cases, correcting them can reduce hair shedding and improve overall health.

## Limited or Unproven

- **Biotin:** helps in rare biotin deficiency. In other people, there is little evidence for hair or nail growth. High doses can interfere with thyroid and heart blood tests, so tell your doctor if you take it.
- **Collagen:** some small studies suggest modest improvement in skin hydration, but results are mixed. See [collagen supplements](/blog/collagen-supplements-what-evidence-says).
- **Antioxidants and "glow" blends:** the evidence of benefit on skin appearance is weak.
- **Omega-3:** may help general health, but is not a skin treatment.

## Risks

High doses of vitamin A, selenium and some herbal products can cause hair loss, liver injury or other harm. More is not better.

## Better Spending

Daily sunscreen, a good moisturiser, treatment of acne or pigmentation, and medicines for [pattern hair loss](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment) do more than most supplements.

## If You Want to Take One

Choose one with a clear reason, check the dose, and review after three months. Tell your doctor about everything you take.
