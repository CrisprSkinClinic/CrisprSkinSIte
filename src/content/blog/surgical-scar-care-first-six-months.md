A good scar depends partly on the operation and partly on what you do afterwards. Simple care makes a visible difference.

## How a Scar Matures

- **Weeks 0 to 2:** the wound is closing. Stitches or glue are present and the area is tender.
- **Weeks 2 to 12:** the scar is pink or red and may be slightly raised and firm. This is the active building phase.
- **Months 3 to 6:** redness fades gradually and the scar softens.
- **Months 6 to 18:** the scar flattens, lightens and becomes less visible. It reaches its final look at around a year or more.

A scar that looks worst at two or three months is often still improving.

## First Two Weeks

- Follow the dressing instructions you were given.
- Keep the wound clean and dry, and do not soak it.
- Avoid stretching, lifting or exercising in a way that pulls on the wound.
- Do not pick at scabs or glue.
- Stitches come out at the time your surgeon decides, and removing them early or late can affect the scar.

## From Two Weeks: Once the Wound Is Closed

1. **Use silicone gel or sheets** as advised, for about two to three months, to support softening and flattening.
2. **Massage gently** with a moisturiser once the wound is fully sealed, as your doctor advises.
3. **Protect from the sun.** New scars darken easily. Cover with clothing or sunscreen for at least a year.
4. **Avoid tension.** For scars over joints, avoid stretching them while they are healing.
5. **Do not smoke,** since it slows healing and makes scars worse.
6. **Eat well,** with enough protein and vitamins for tissue repair.

## What Is Normal

Itching, mild tenderness, firmness and redness in the first months are normal. A raised pink scar at two months is not necessarily a problem.

## When to Ask for Help

See your surgeon or dermatologist if a scar:
- Becomes thick, raised, red and spreading beyond the wound.
- Itches intensely or becomes painful.
- Opens, oozes, or shows signs of infection.
- Is getting wider.

Early treatment of a raised scar, for example with steroid injection, is far more effective than waiting. See [scar types explained](/blog/scar-types-hypertrophic-keloid-and-atrophic-explained).

## Scar-Prone People

If you have formed keloids or thick scars before, tell your surgeon before the operation, so preventive measures such as silicone, pressure or injections can be planned. See [keloid treatment](/blog/keloid-treatment-what-works-and-why-keloids-return).
