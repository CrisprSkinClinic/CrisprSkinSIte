A round, scaly patch where hair has broken off is one of the commonest reasons parents bring a school-age child to a dermatologist. When it is fungal, it is called tinea capitis, or scalp ringworm, and it needs a different treatment from ringworm on the skin.

## What It Looks Like

Tinea capitis is most common between the ages of three and fourteen. Signs include:

- One or more round or irregular patches of scale and hair loss.
- Hairs broken a few millimetres above the scalp, sometimes seen as "black dots".
- Mild itching, or none at all.
- Sometimes a swollen, boggy, tender lump with pus called a kerion, which is the body reacting strongly to the fungus. It needs urgent treatment to avoid permanent scarring.
- Enlarged glands at the back of the neck.

Similar-looking problems include [dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis](/blog/dandruff-myths), scalp psoriasis, alopecia areata and head lice, so confirmation matters.

## How It Is Diagnosed

We look at the patch, may use a special light, and take a few hairs and scale for a microscope test (KOH) and, where needed, a fungal culture. A culture helps choose the right medicine and shows whether the fungus came from an animal, soil or another person.

## Why Creams Alone Do Not Work

The fungus lives inside the hair shaft and follicle, below where a cream can reach. Treatment therefore needs an antifungal medicine taken by mouth, usually for six to eight weeks, with the dose chosen for the child's weight. A medicated shampoo used two or three times a week lowers the amount of fungus shed on to combs, pillows and other children, but does not replace tablets. Your doctor will tell you which tablet and whether any blood tests are needed.

Steroid creams must be avoided, because they make the fungus spread and mask the picture. See [steroid creams and ringworm](/blog/steroid-creams-and-tinea-why-they-make-it-worse).

## Stopping the Spread

- Do not share combs, hats, pillowcases, towels or hair accessories.
- Wash these items in hot water and dry them in sunlight, and soak combs in warm soapy water.
- Check siblings and other household members, and pets, since cats and dogs can carry the fungus.
- Children can generally return to school once treatment has started, with a note from the doctor. Schools differ, so ask.
- Wash hands after touching the scalp.

## Will the Hair Grow Back?

In most children the hair grows back normally within a few months after the infection is cleared. A kerion that is not treated promptly can leave a scar and permanent bald patch, which is why early treatment matters.

## Helping a Child Finish the Course

Tablets need to be taken to the end, even when the scalp looks clear after two or three weeks. Stopping early is the commonest reason for relapse. Use a calendar, give the dose with food if advised, and return for the review visit. See [how long to continue tinea treatment](/blog/tinea-how-long-to-continue-treatment).

## When to See a Doctor

Book a visit for any round scaly bald patch in a child, a painful swollen lump on the scalp, or hair loss that is spreading.
