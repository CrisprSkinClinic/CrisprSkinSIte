"The rash is gone, so can I stop?" is the question that decides whether tinea returns.

## Why Visible Improvement Is Misleading

Itch and redness settle within days, but the fungus lives in the upper layer of skin and hair for longer. If you stop at this stage, remaining fungus regrows, and the infection returns, sometimes in a form that is harder to treat.

## Typical Durations

These are general guides. Your doctor sets the exact plan.

- **Body and groin tinea:** several weeks of treatment, and often one to two weeks beyond the clearing of skin.
- **Stubborn or widespread infection:** longer, with tablets and creams together.
- **Foot tinea:** several weeks, with attention to toe webs and soles.
- **Scalp tinea (mainly children):** tablets for weeks, as creams cannot reach the hair roots.
- **Nails:** many weeks to months, because nails grow slowly. See [nail fungus](/conditions/onychomycosis).

## Practical Tips for Staying on Track

- Set a phone reminder for tablets and cream.
- Use a calendar and mark each day.
- Take tablets with food if advised, to improve absorption.
- Do not skip because supplies ran out. Ask for a refill.
- Check with your doctor before taking other medicines, as some antifungals interact.

## Tablet Safety

Antifungal tablets are generally safe, but your doctor may check your liver, or review other medicines. Tell them if you have liver disease or are pregnant.

## When It Does Not Clear

If patches are not clearly better in two weeks, or keep coming back, do not repeat the same cream. Book a review. See [why tinea treatment fails](/blog/tinea-treatment-why-it-fails-and-compliance) and the page on [fungal infections](/services/fungal-infections).

## What Is Happening in the Skin During Treatment

Dermatophytes feed on keratin in the top layer of skin, hair and nails. Antifungal medicines stop fungal growth, but the infected cells have to shed and be replaced by healthy ones before the skin is truly clear. The outer skin layer renews over roughly four weeks, thicker skin of the soles and palms takes longer, and nails take many months. This is why visible recovery can precede true cure.

## Site-by-Site Guide

**Groin and body (tinea cruris and corporis):** several weeks, depending on severity and whether tablets are used. Widespread disease often needs longer.

**Feet (tinea pedis):** several weeks, particularly for the thick, dry "moccasin" type. Keep toe-webs dry.

**Hands (tinea manuum):** often one hand and both feet ("two feet, one hand" pattern), needing prolonged treatment.

**Face and beard (tinea faciei and barbae):** prompt treatment helps to avoid hair follicle involvement.

**Scalp (tinea capitis):** tablets for weeks, usually with a medicated shampoo, and screening of family members.

**Nails (onychomycosis):** fingernails often take around six weeks of oral treatment, toenails around twelve weeks, though full growth of a clear nail takes many months. See [nail fungus](/conditions/onychomycosis).

## Why "A Few Days" Is Not Enough

Short courses might suppress symptoms, but leave live fungus. After stopping, it regrows, often in a more resistant form. Patients then think the medicine "does not work", when the real problem was the duration.

## Signs the Treatment Is Working

- Itching falls within one to two weeks.
- The red edge of the ring stops spreading and flattens.
- The skin colour gradually normalises. Darker marks can last for months, even after cure.
- No new lesions appear.

## Signs It Is Not Working

- New patches appear despite treatment.
- The rash is not improving by two weeks.
- It seems to improve and relapse quickly.
- Thick, scaly or pus-filled lesions develop.

Return for review, since a change of medicine, a skin scraping test or a check of other factors may be needed.
