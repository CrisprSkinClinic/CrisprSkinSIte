Treating the skin is only half the job. Fungus lives in clothes, towels, bedding and shoes, and passes between family members. Fixing the environment prevents relapse.

## Clothing and Towels

- Wash clothes and towels in hot water, and dry them in sun or iron them. Wash during treatment, not only at the end.
- Change underwear and socks daily.
- Choose loose cotton in humid weather, and avoid tight synthetic clothing, especially in the groin.
- Keep your own towel. Do not share, and avoid using the same towel for feet and body.

## Footwear

Rotate shoes so they can dry. Use cotton or moisture-wicking socks, and dry between the toes. Antifungal powder in shoes may help.

## Skin Care

Dry skin folds carefully after bathing. Keep nails short, so scratching does not spread infection. Treat foot or nail fungus at the same time, since it can reinfect the groin and body.

## Family and Pets

Check family members for itchy ring-shaped patches, and treat everyone together. Some ringworm comes from pets, so check them if patches persist.

## Weight, Sweat and Diabetes

Excess sweating, being overweight and diabetes increase recurrence. Controlling blood sugar is part of treatment.

## Gym and Swimming

Wear sandals in showers and pools, and avoid sharing equipment without a barrier.

## Keep Taking the Medicine

Prevention steps do not replace treatment. See [how long to continue](/blog/tinea-how-long-to-continue-treatment), [why treatment fails](/blog/tinea-treatment-why-it-fails-and-compliance), and our page on [tinea infections](/conditions/tinea-infections).

## A Home Cleaning Checklist

During treatment, once a week or at the start and end:

- Wash towels, bedsheets, innerwear and clothes worn near the infected area at the hottest water temperature the fabric allows, then dry in strong sunlight or iron.
- Disinfect bathroom mats and the floor of shared showers, as fungal spores persist.
- Clean the inside of shoes, using an antifungal powder or spray, and let them dry in sunlight.
- Replace old, worn insoles.
- Wash combs, hairbrushes and razors with hot water and soap.

## Daily Habits

**After bathing:** dry skin folds thoroughly, particularly the groin, under the breasts, between the toes and the armpits.

**Clothing:** loose cotton underwear and clothes. Avoid wearing the same sweaty clothes twice, or tight jeans for long hours, especially in humid weather.

**Footwear:** alternate pairs. Do not wear wet shoes.

**Body powders:** use medicated or plain powder in folds only when skin is dry, as advised by a doctor.

## Reducing Sweat and Friction

Lose weight if advised, since skin folds trap sweat. Change clothes after exercise promptly. Use anti-chafing products if recommended.

## Pets and the Environment

Cats and dogs can carry ringworm, often with patchy hair loss or scaly skin. If you have a pet with suspicious patches, a vet should check them. Fungus can also be picked up from the soil, gyms and barber shops, so keep towels personal and bring your own.

## Who Is More Prone to Recurrence?

People with diabetes, obesity, those on steroids or immune-lowering medicines, people with heavy sweating, those who live in crowded or humid environments, and people who have had prior incomplete treatment. If you are in one of these groups, longer follow-up and stricter prevention are wise. See [vaccines and immune-lowering medicines](/blog/vaccines-before-immunosuppressants-biologics-checklist) for planning when starting such treatment.

## What About Nails and Feet?

Treat athlete's foot and nail infection along with other areas, since they act as a reservoir. See [nail fungus](/conditions/onychomycosis). Trim nails short and use separate clippers.

## Telling the Family

Fungal infection is not a matter of poor hygiene, and it is common. Explaining this helps relatives agree to be checked and treated. A household that treats together has far fewer recurrences.
