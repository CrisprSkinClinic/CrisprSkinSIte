Parents of children with eczema often worry that vaccines will trigger flares, or that they should be postponed. In almost all cases, neither is true.

## Eczema Is Not a Reason to Delay

Children with eczema should follow the normal schedule. Infections are in fact more serious in children with eczema, so protection matters more. Vaccines do not cause eczema.

## When a Short Delay May Help

If your child is very unwell with a fever, or has a severe, infected or weeping flare, speak to your paediatrician about timing. Ordinary mild eczema needs no delay.

## Before the Visit

Moisturise as usual. Tell the doctor which areas are affected so a clear patch of skin is chosen for the injection.

## After the Injection

Expect a little redness or soreness. Keep the area clean, avoid scratching, and keep up the usual moisturiser routine. Fever, a spreading rash or extreme irritability should be reported.

## Egg and Other Allergies

Mild food allergies are generally not a barrier to routine vaccines. Tell your paediatrician about any serious allergic reactions.

## Keep Up the Skin Care

Read our guide to [managing childhood eczema day to day](/blog/managing-childhood-eczema), and see the page on [childhood atopic dermatitis](/conditions/childhood-atopic-dermatitis). For routine childhood vaccines, please follow your paediatrician's schedule.
