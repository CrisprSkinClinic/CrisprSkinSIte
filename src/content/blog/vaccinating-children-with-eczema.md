Parents of children with eczema often worry that vaccines will trigger flares, or that they should be postponed. In almost all cases, neither is true.

## Eczema Is Not a Reason to Delay

Children with eczema should follow the normal schedule. Infections are in fact more serious in children with eczema, so protection matters more. Vaccines do not cause eczema.

## When a Short Delay May Help

If your child is very unwell with a fever, or has a severe, infected or weeping flare, speak to your paediatrician about timing. Ordinary mild eczema needs no delay.

## Before the Visit

Moisturise as usual. Tell the doctor which areas are affected so a clear patch of skin is chosen for the injection.

## After the Injection

Expect a little redness or soreness. Keep the area clean, avoid scratching, and keep up the usual moisturiser routine. Fever, a spreading rash or extreme irritability should be reported.

## Egg and Other Allergies

Mild food allergies are generally not a barrier to routine vaccines. Tell your paediatrician about any serious allergic reactions.

## Keep Up the Skin Care

Read our guide to [managing childhood eczema day to day](/blog/managing-childhood-eczema), and see the page on [childhood atopic dermatitis](/conditions/childhood-atopic-dermatitis). For routine childhood vaccines, please follow your paediatrician's schedule.

## Why Parents Worry, and What the Research Shows

It is understandable to worry when a child's skin is already fragile. Studies of children with eczema have not found that routine vaccines cause new eczema or make existing eczema worse. Some children have a short flare in the days after an injection, which is usually coincidental or related to the mild fever. The risks of the diseases the vaccines prevent, such as whooping cough, measles and chickenpox, are far greater, and skin infections can be more serious in children with eczema.

## Preparing for the Visit

- **Treat flares first.** If your child has an angry, weeping or infected patch, ask your doctor to calm it before the appointment, since this makes the visit easier.
- **Moisturise on schedule.** A well-hydrated skin barrier tolerates the injection better.
- **Plan the site.** Ask the vaccinator to choose clear skin on the thigh or arm.
- **Bring your child's eczema treatment plan.** It helps the doctor understand what is going on.

## After the Visit

A small red lump or soreness for a couple of days is normal. Keep the area clean and avoid scratching. Do not apply steroid creams to the injection site unless advised. If your child has a fever, give the medicine your doctor recommends at the right dose for their weight, and keep up the moisturiser.

## Special Situations

**Child on strong eczema medicines:** some tablets or injections that lower immunity change which vaccines are suitable, especially live vaccines. Tell the paediatrician and the dermatologist about every medicine.

**Eczema herpeticum:** a painful clustered blistering infection that can occur in children with eczema, usually from cold-sore virus exposure. It needs urgent medical care. Fever with painful, punched-out blisters over eczema is a warning sign.

**Egg allergy:** most routine childhood vaccines are not a problem in children with egg allergy. Discuss any past serious reaction with your paediatrician.

## The Bottom Line

Keep to the vaccine schedule your paediatrician recommends. The skin plan and the vaccine plan work side by side.
