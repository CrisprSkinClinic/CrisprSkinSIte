Skin reactions after vaccines are common and almost always mild. Knowing what is expected helps you avoid unnecessary worry.

## Common and Harmless

- Redness, soreness or slight swelling at the injection site for a few days.
- A small firm lump that fades over weeks.
- Tiredness, a mild temperature or aches for a day or two.

These show your body is responding.

## A Delayed Local Reaction

Some people get a large, red, itchy patch around the site four to ten days later, especially after certain vaccines. It looks alarming but settles on its own. A cool compress and an antihistamine, if advised, help.

## Rash After a Vaccine

A mild rash can follow vaccines, and some appear a week or more later, particularly after live vaccines such as measles. Most settle without treatment. A rash that spreads, blisters or comes with fever needs review.

## Seek Urgent Care If

You have swelling of the lips, tongue or face, wheeze or breathlessness, dizziness, or widespread hives soon after the injection.

## Does This Mean No More Vaccines?

Not usually. A doctor can tell you whether the reaction was an allergy or something harmless. [Hives](/blog/hives-when-to-worry) from other causes are common as well. See our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination).

## A Closer Look at Common Reactions

**Soreness, redness and swelling at the site.** Peaks at one to two days and fades within a week. Cool compresses and movement of the arm help.

**A firm lump (nodule).** Some vaccines that contain aluminium, which helps the immune response, can leave a small firm lump that lasts weeks or months. It is harmless, but tell your doctor if it grows, becomes painful or drains.

**Delayed large local reaction.** A hand-sized red, itchy patch that appears four to ten days after, particularly after certain vaccines. It resembles cellulitis but is usually a delayed allergy-type reaction, and typically has no fever. It settles in a few days. If you are unsure, have it examined.

**Fever and body aches.** A normal immune response. Paracetamol at the correct dose is usually enough.

## Rashes After Specific Vaccines

- **Measles-containing vaccines:** some people get a faint measles-like rash and fever about a week or two later. It is not infectious.
- **Chickenpox vaccine:** a few small spots may appear near the site, or elsewhere, a few weeks later.
- **Shingles vaccine:** a sore arm and fatigue are common, but rash is uncommon.
- **Any vaccine:** hives or itchy welts are sometimes seen within hours and settle with an antihistamine, but should be reported.

## Allergy: How to Recognise It

A true allergic reaction usually begins within minutes to a couple of hours: widespread hives, swelling of the lips, face or tongue, wheeze, vomiting, light-headedness. This needs immediate emergency care. Vaccine clinics keep emergency medicines ready, which is why you are asked to wait afterwards.

## What to Do Next Time

If you had a mild local reaction, you can usually continue with future doses. If you had hives, facial swelling or breathing trouble, speak to a doctor before the next dose. Allergy testing can sometimes identify the cause, and safer ways to continue may exist.

## Other Skin Problems That Look Like Vaccine Reactions

Insect bites, contact dermatitis from skin-cleaning solutions, viral rashes in children, and flares of eczema or [chronic hives](/blog/hives-when-to-worry) can coincide with a vaccine. A dermatologist can tell them apart, and avoid labelling a vaccine as the cause when it was not.
