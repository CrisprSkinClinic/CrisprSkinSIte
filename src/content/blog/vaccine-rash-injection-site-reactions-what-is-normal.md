Skin reactions after vaccines are common and almost always mild. Knowing what is expected helps you avoid unnecessary worry.

## Common and Harmless

- Redness, soreness or slight swelling at the injection site for a few days.
- A small firm lump that fades over weeks.
- Tiredness, a mild temperature or aches for a day or two.

These show your body is responding.

## A Delayed Local Reaction

Some people get a large, red, itchy patch around the site four to ten days later, especially after certain vaccines. It looks alarming but settles on its own. A cool compress and an antihistamine, if advised, help.

## Rash After a Vaccine

A mild rash can follow vaccines, and some appear a week or more later, particularly after live vaccines such as measles. Most settle without treatment. A rash that spreads, blisters or comes with fever needs review.

## Seek Urgent Care If

You have swelling of the lips, tongue or face, wheeze or breathlessness, dizziness, or widespread hives soon after the injection.

## Does This Mean No More Vaccines?

Not usually. A doctor can tell you whether the reaction was an allergy or something harmless. [Hives](/blog/hives-when-to-worry) from other causes are common as well. See our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination).
