People with psoriasis, vitiligo or alopecia areata often avoid vaccines out of fear of a flare. The evidence is reassuring.

## Myth: Vaccines Cause Autoimmune Skin Disease

Large studies have not shown that routine vaccines cause these conditions. Infections are a much more common trigger for flares than vaccines.

## Myth: You Should Avoid Vaccines If You Have Psoriasis

Not so. Non-live vaccines are recommended as usual. People on [treatment ladder](/blog/psoriasis-treatment-ladder) medicines, such as methotrexate or biologics, need a little planning about live vaccines and timing, but should not skip protection.

## Vitiligo and Alopecia Areata

There is no good evidence that vaccines worsen [vitiligo](/blog/vitiligo-misconceptions) or [alopecia areata](/blog/living-with-alopecia-areata). Treatment decisions, including JAK inhibitors, call for a review of your vaccination status.

## A Rare Flare After Vaccination

Occasionally someone notices a flare after a vaccine. A coincidence, or the infection-like response, is usually the explanation, and it is not a reason to avoid future doses. Discuss it with your doctor.

## What to Do

Keep vaccinations up to date, tell the vaccinator your diagnosis and medicines, and book a review before starting new immune-lowering treatment. See our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination).
