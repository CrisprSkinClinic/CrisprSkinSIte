Medicines that lower immunity treat psoriasis, eczema, hidradenitis and other skin disease very well. They also change how vaccines behave. A few weeks of planning before starting can prevent avoidable infections.

## Live and Non-Live Vaccines

Live vaccines are generally avoided once treatment begins. If one is needed, it is usually given at least four weeks beforehand. Non-live vaccines are safe at any time, but produce a better response when given at least two weeks before treatment. Urgent treatment should never be held up unnecessarily.

## What We Usually Review

- **Shingles (recombinant, non-live)** for adults, including those from 18 years with weakened immunity. See [who should take it](/blog/shingles-vaccine-who-should-get-it).
- **HPV**, if not already completed.
- **Hepatitis B**, with a blood test to check immunity. Read [more on hepatitis B](/blog/hepatitis-b-vaccination-before-skin-procedures).
- **Influenza and pneumococcal** vaccines, which your physician usually provides.
- **Chickenpox and measles immunity**, because live vaccines would need to be given early.
- **COVID-19 and tetanus boosters**, as advised.

## Also Part of Screening

Blood tests for hepatitis B and C, and tests for tuberculosis, are usually done before biologics. Our page on [biologics](/services/biologics) explains this.

## Household Contacts

Family members can usually have routine vaccines as advised.
