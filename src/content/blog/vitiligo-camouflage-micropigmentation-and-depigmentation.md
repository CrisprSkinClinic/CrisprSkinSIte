Even with good treatment, some patches respond slowly or not at all. There are other options, and a good plan includes them.

## Camouflage

Specially formulated cosmetics and body make-up can cover patches, and a self-tanning product can sometimes blend skin tone. These are a legitimate complement to medical treatment, not a failure of it.

## Micropigmentation

Medical tattooing places pigment into the skin and is sometimes used for small, stable patches, particularly on the lips. Colour match is hard to maintain, and results can change over time, so it is considered carefully.

## Depigmentation

For very extensive vitiligo, where most of the body is already affected and repigmentation is not realistic, some patients choose to lighten the remaining pigmented skin to achieve a uniform tone. It is permanent and means lifelong sun protection, so it is discussed only after other options and with time to reflect.

## Sun Protection

Whatever the plan, daily sunscreen protects depigmented skin from burning and reduces contrast with surrounding skin.

## Emotional Wellbeing

Vitiligo can affect confidence, relationships, and mood. Talk to us about how you are coping, and consider support groups or counselling if it is weighing on you. Looking after the person matters as much as treating the patches.
