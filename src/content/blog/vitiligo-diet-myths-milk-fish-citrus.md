Few skin conditions attract as many food rules as vitiligo. Patients are told to avoid milk, curd, fish, citrus fruit, sour foods or non-vegetarian food together with milk.

## What the Evidence Shows

There is no good evidence that these foods cause vitiligo, or make it spread or worse. Vitiligo is an autoimmune condition, in which the immune system damages pigment cells. Diet is not the cause.

## The Cost of Myths

Cutting out milk, fish or fruit removes protein, calcium, vitamin C and healthy fats, and adds stress. In children, this can affect nutrition.

## What Is Worth Doing

- Eat a balanced diet with plenty of fruit and vegetables.
- Have deficiencies checked, such as vitamin D, B12 and folate, if your doctor advises. Vitiligo is more often seen with thyroid problems, so thyroid testing is often done.
- Protect skin from sunburn, which can trigger new patches.

## Supplements

Antioxidants and vitamin D have been studied, but evidence for helping vitiligo is limited. They are not a replacement for treatment.

## What Does Help

Medical treatment such as topical therapy and narrowband UVB. ## Where These Beliefs Come From

Food restrictions in vitiligo are passed down in families and communities, often based on beliefs about "incompatible foods", such as milk with fish, or on the idea that sour foods "damage" pigment. Unfortunately, they are not backed by scientific evidence, and many patients feel guilty, thinking that they brought vitiligo on themselves. Vitiligo is an autoimmune condition, in which the immune system attacks melanocytes, and genetics play a large role.

## What Research Has Actually Shown

Studies looking at diet and vitiligo are limited. Some small studies suggest that people with vitiligo may have lower levels of certain antioxidants, vitamin D or B12, but whether this is a cause or a consequence is unclear. No food has been shown to trigger or worsen vitiligo in controlled studies. Patients who avoid specific foods do not show better outcomes.

## What Does Contribute to Vitiligo Activity

- Skin injury, including friction, burns and tattoos (the Koebner phenomenon).
- Sunburn.
- Stress and illness.
- Associated autoimmune disease, especially thyroid disease.
- Certain chemicals, such as phenols in some products.

## A Sensible Approach to Nutrition

A varied diet rich in fruit, vegetables, pulses and whole grains supports health. Check for thyroid disease, and for deficiencies of vitamin D, B12 and folate when relevant. Take supplements only if levels are low and advised by your doctor.

## Antioxidant Supplements

Some trials combined antioxidants with phototherapy. Results were mixed. They are not a replacement for treatment, and should be discussed with a doctor.

## Why It Matters to Get This Right

Cutting out milk, fish and fruit can cause nutritional gaps, particularly in children. Anxiety about food adds to the emotional burden of vitiligo. Understanding that diet is not to blame can lift guilt, and lets patients concentrate on treatments that work.

## What Helps Repigmentation

Topical steroids, tacrolimus, narrowband UVB phototherapy and, in selected stable cases, surgical options.
