Several surgical methods can repigment stable vitiligo. They all take pigment cells from healthy skin, but they differ in how much skin they can treat, how natural the colour looks and how long recovery takes.

## 1. Non-Cultured Epidermal Cell Suspension

**How it works.** A very thin shaving of normal skin, usually from the thigh, is taken. Enzymes release the pigment cells and the surrounding skin cells into a liquid. The patch is gently abraded to prepare it, and the suspension is spread across it and covered with a dressing.

**Best for.** Medium to large patches, and patches on flat areas. A small donor patch can treat an area several times larger.

**Points.** Colour match is usually good, texture is smooth, and the donor area heals as a graze. The procedure needs a laboratory-style setup and skilled handling, and takes a few hours.

## 2. Suction Blister Grafting

**How it works.** A suction device raises blisters on normal skin over about two hours. The thin roofs of the blisters are cut away and laid on the prepared patch, then dressed.

**Best for.** Small to medium patches, especially on the face, eyelids and other thin skin.

**Points.** Colour and texture are excellent, and the donor area usually heals without a scar. It is slower than other methods, and the area that can be treated is limited by the number of blisters.

## 3. Punch Grafting

**How it works.** Small cylinders of normal skin (1 to 1.5 mm) are taken with a punch and placed in small holes made in the patch. The graft grows outwards.

**Best for.** Small, stable patches, and areas such as the lips and fingertips where other methods are hard.

**Points.** It is simple and cheap, but the colour can look dotted at first, and a cobblestone texture can occur. The donor area has tiny scars.

## 4. Other Techniques

Thin split-thickness grafts and hair follicle grafting are used by some surgeons in selected cases, with their own pros and cons. We discuss any that apply to you.

## How We Choose

We consider the size and site of the patch, the donor skin available, your skin colour and tendency to scar, and your preferences. For patches on the face, blister grafting or cell suspension is often preferred. For larger areas, cell suspension. For tiny patches in difficult sites, punch grafts.

## What Is the Same for All

- Local anaesthetic, and an awake, comfortable procedure.
- Dressings for about a week, with the area kept still and dry.
- Repigmentation over three to six months.
- Sun protection and often narrowband UVB afterwards to support colour spread.
