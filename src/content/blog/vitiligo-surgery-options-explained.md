Surgical treatment for vitiligo moves pigment-producing cells from healthy skin into a depigmented patch. It is for stable disease only.

## Who Is a Candidate

Patches that have been stable, usually for around a year, and have not repigmented with medical treatment. Segmental vitiligo is often a good fit. Active, spreading disease is not suitable.

## Main Techniques

**Punch grafting** places tiny skin grafts into small patches. **Suction blister grafting** lifts a thin layer of skin from a donor site for a smooth cosmetic result. **Cell suspension transfer** uses a suspension of cells from a small donor sample to treat larger areas. **Hair follicle transplant** transfers pigmented follicles and can help certain sites. **Thin skin grafts** are used selectively.

## Choosing the Technique

It depends on the size and location of the patch, and your skin type. Some sites, such as lips and the backs of joints, are more challenging.

## Risks and Limitations

Mismatched colour, scarring, uneven pigment, or failure of the graft to take can occur, and new patches can develop if the disease reactivates. A test area is sometimes done first to check how your skin responds.

## After Surgery

Healing takes several weeks, and phototherapy or sun exposure is often added to improve the result. We discuss everything in advance so expectations are realistic.
