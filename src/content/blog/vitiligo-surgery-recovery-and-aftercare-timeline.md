Recovery after vitiligo surgery is gentle, but patience and aftercare make a big difference to the result. Here is what to expect.

## Day of Surgery

You go home the same day with dressings on both the treated patch and the donor area. Some soreness is normal, and simple painkillers are enough. Keep the treated area still, so the grafted cells are not disturbed.

## First Week

- **Keep dressings dry and in place.** Avoid showering the treated area, and do not remove the dressing until the day you are asked to return.
- **Limit movement near the area.** For patches on joints, a splint or support may be advised.
- **Raise the limb** if it is swollen.
- **Medicines:** a short course of antibiotics or painkillers if prescribed.
- **Avoid:** sweating, exercise, swimming, rubbing and sun.

At about one week the dressing is removed in clinic. The area looks pink and raw or crusty, and the donor area looks like a graze.

## Weeks 2 to 4

- The crust falls off by itself. Do not pick.
- Moisturise as advised. Protect the area from sunlight, and avoid friction.
- The donor area heals, often leaving a pink mark that fades.
- Gentle activity can resume, and strenuous exercise a little later.

## Weeks 4 to 12

Colour begins to appear, often as tiny dots (perifollicular) or a spreading rim around grafts. Narrowband UVB or controlled sun exposure may be started to encourage spread, if your doctor advises. Follow-up visits track progress with photographs.

## Months 3 to 6

Repigmentation continues and often reaches its best by about six months. Some patches fill in completely, some partly. Final colour match is judged now, and any touch-up is planned, not earlier.

## Looking After the Result

- Use sunscreen on treated skin, to prevent sunburn and uneven tanning.
- Avoid injury and friction to the area, since vitiligo can appear at injury sites.
- Continue medical treatment for vitiligo if advised.
- Report new white patches promptly.

## Warning Signs

Contact the clinic for spreading redness, pus, severe pain, fever or a dressing that has come off early.

## Common Concerns

**"The patch looks worse at first."** That is expected, since healing is under way. Judge the result after three to six months.

**"Colour appears unevenly."** Early spotty repigmentation fills in over time.

**"The donor area looks pale."** It often fades, but may stay slightly different.
