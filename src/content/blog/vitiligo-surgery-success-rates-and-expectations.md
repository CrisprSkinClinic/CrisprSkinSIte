Patients considering vitiligo surgery want one answer: will it work? The honest answer is that it works well for carefully selected patients, and less well for the rest. Understanding why helps you decide.

## What Studies Show

In published studies of stable vitiligo, good repigmentation (usually defined as more than seventy to ninety percent colour return) is reported in a majority of well-selected patients, with figures ranging widely between methods, sites and centres. Results are best in segmental and stable non-segmental vitiligo on the face, trunk and limbs, and weaker on the hands, feet and knuckles.

Exact numbers depend on how success is measured, and no centre can guarantee a result. We give an estimate for your patches, and a test patch can sharpen it.

## What Improves the Odds

- **Proven stability** before surgery.
- **Segmental vitiligo** or well-defined stable patches.
- **Sites such as the face and trunk.**
- **Careful preparation** of the patch, and good technique.
- **Aftercare:** keeping dressings on and the area still.
- **Light treatment afterwards** (narrowband UVB) to help colour spread.
- **Not smoking,** which impairs healing.

## What Lowers the Odds

- Active or unstable vitiligo.
- Patches on the hands, feet, fingertips, lips and bony prominences.
- A history of new patches at injury sites.
- A very large area, or too little donor skin.
- Poor aftercare, infection or injury.

## What a Realistic Result Looks Like

- **Complete or near-complete repigmentation** in some patches.
- **Partial repigmentation,** with a patchy or speckled look that may improve with further sessions.
- **Colour mismatch:** the new colour may be slightly lighter or darker than the surroundings.
- **Texture changes,** such as cobblestoning with punch grafting.
- **No response** in a minority, and occasionally new patches elsewhere.

## What Surgery Cannot Do

It does not cure vitiligo, prevent new patches or change the underlying immune activity. Vitiligo can come back, so follow-up and continued medical treatment matter.

## How We Measure Results

We take photographs before and at fixed intervals after surgery, and judge colour return at three and six months. Further touch-up sessions are planned only when the first result has settled.
