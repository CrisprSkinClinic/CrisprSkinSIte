Vitiligo surgery can restore colour to white patches, but only when the right patient is chosen. Careful selection matters more than the choice of technique.

## The Key Requirement: Stability

Vitiligo surgery moves pigment cells into a patch. If the vitiligo is still active, the new cells can be attacked, and new patches may appear. For this reason, surgery is advised only when the disease is **stable**.

Stability is usually judged by:
- No new patches.
- No spreading of the existing patches.
- No change in the size of the patches.
- A period of about twelve months with no change, sometimes shorter in segmental vitiligo.

Doctors also look for the signs of activity: blurred or "confetti" edges, new white hairs, and the history of patches appearing after injury. See [stable or active vitiligo?](/blog/stable-or-active-vitiligo-why-it-matters).

## Good Candidates

- **Segmental vitiligo,** which usually stops spreading early and tends to respond well to surgery. See [segmental vitiligo](/blog/segmental-vitiligo-why-it-is-different).
- **Stable non-segmental vitiligo** that has not responded to medicines and light treatment.
- **Patches in sites that respond well,** such as the face, neck, trunk and limbs.
- People with realistic expectations who can follow aftercare.
- Adults, and older adolescents in selected cases.

## Who Should Wait

- **Active vitiligo,** until it has been quiet for the required time.
- **A strong koebner response,** meaning new patches appear at sites of injury, which makes surgery risky.
- **Recent new patches** or fast spread.
- **Unrealistic expectations,** such as a complete cure.

## Who Is Less Suited

- A history of keloids or thick scarring.
- Patches on the hands, feet, knuckles, lips and other bony or moist areas, which respond less well.
- Very extensive vitiligo, where medical treatment and, in some cases, depigmentation of the remaining colour may be discussed. See [vitiligo camouflage and depigmentation](/blog/vitiligo-camouflage-micropigmentation-and-depigmentation).
- Uncontrolled health conditions that impair healing.

## Making Vitiligo Stable First

Medicines such as topical treatments, oral treatment in active disease and narrowband UVB phototherapy can help bring vitiligo under control. This both prepares for surgery and, for many patients, delivers the result without surgery. See [phototherapy for vitiligo](/blog/phototherapy-for-vitiligo-nbuvb-excimer).

## The Test Patch

When stability is uncertain, or when a large area is planned, we may do a small test graft first. If it repigments well and no new patches appear in the following three to six months, the larger procedure is more reliable.

## Questions We Ask

How long since the last change? Have patches appeared after cuts, burns or friction? What treatments have been tried, and for how long? Is there a family history of vitiligo or autoimmune disease? Do you form keloids? Are you on any medicines?
