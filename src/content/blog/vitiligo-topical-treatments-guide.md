For limited vitiligo, creams are usually where treatment begins. Different creams suit different sites, and using the right one matters.

## Topical Corticosteroids

These reduce the local immune activity that destroys pigment cells. They can be effective for limited patches, but must be used in short, supervised courses because prolonged use can thin the skin, cause stretch marks, and cause visible blood vessels, especially on the face and in folds.

## Calcineurin Inhibitors

Tacrolimus and pimecrolimus act on the same immune process without thinning the skin, so they are preferred for the face, neck, and skin folds, and can be used for longer periods. Mild burning or stinging in the early days is common and usually settles.

## Vitamin D Analogues

These are sometimes used alongside other treatments, though on their own they are generally less effective.

## Newer Topical Agents

Topical JAK inhibitors have shown repigmentation in studies, mainly on the face. Approval and availability vary between countries, and we will tell you what is practically accessible.

## Making Creams Work

Consistency over months matters, and sunlight exposure or phototherapy often improves response. Response is best on the face and neck and slowest on the hands and feet. We review at regular intervals and change the plan if there is no response after an adequate trial.
