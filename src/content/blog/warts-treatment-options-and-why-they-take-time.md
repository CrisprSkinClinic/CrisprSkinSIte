Warts are caused by the human papillomavirus, and they are among the most stubborn small skin problems. Treatment is a matter of persistence.

## Why Warts Are Slow

The virus lives in the top layer of skin and hides from the immune system. Treatment aims to destroy infected skin and prompt the body to recognise the virus. Even after clearing, virus may remain nearby, and warts can return.

## Without Treatment

Many warts disappear on their own, within months to two years, especially in children. We often wait for small, painless warts, and treat those that hurt, spread or bother you. See [viral warts](/conditions/viral-warts).

## Treatment Options

- **Salicylic acid:** a daily paste or plaster to soften and peel the wart, used for weeks to months, with filing of dead skin. It is the safe first step for most.
- **Cryotherapy:** liquid nitrogen freezes the wart, repeated every two to three weeks. It stings, and may blister.
- **Cantharidin and similar topical treatments** applied in clinic, in selected patients.
- **Cautery or curettage** for stubborn or large warts, with local anaesthetic.
- **Other prescription treatments** for resistant warts, chosen by a dermatologist.

Plantar warts on the feet are hardest, since pressure pushes them in. Padding and regular paring help comfort.

## What Helps at Home

- Use salicylic acid consistently.
- Pare the surface with a disposable file or pumice, used only on the wart.
- Cover with a plaster, and keep feet dry.
- Do not share files and towels.

## What to Avoid

Cutting or picking warts, using strong acids not meant for skin, and unproven home remedies. These can scar and spread the virus.

## Prevention

Wear sandals in communal showers, keep feet dry and do not pick. The HPV vaccine does not prevent common warts. See [what the HPV vaccine does and does not do](/blog/hpv-vaccine-what-it-prevents-and-what-it-does-not).

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist for painful warts, warts on the face or genitals, many warts, warts in people with weak immunity or any wart that changes, bleeds or looks unusual.
