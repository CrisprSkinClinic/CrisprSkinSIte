Wet wrap therapy is a simple but effective treatment for severe or stubborn eczema flares, in children and adults. It calms inflammation, soothes itch and helps medicines work.

## How It Works

A layer of moisturiser (and, if prescribed, a mild steroid) is applied to the skin and covered with a damp layer of cotton fabric, then a dry layer on top. The damp layer keeps the skin moist, helps the creams absorb and acts as a barrier against scratching.

## When It Is Used

- A severe flare that has not responded to usual treatment.
- Eczema with intense itch that disturbs sleep.
- Dry, cracked, very inflamed patches.
- As a short course (about three to seven days) to bring a flare under control.

It is generally not used when skin is infected, until the infection is treated.

## How to Do It

1. **Bathe** in lukewarm water for about ten minutes, and gently pat dry.
2. **Apply** the prescribed medicine (if any) to red patches, then a thick moisturiser to all the skin.
3. **Dampen** a layer of clean cotton clothing or tubular bandages with warm water, and wring out until just damp.
4. **Put on** the damp layer, then a dry layer on top (pyjamas or bandage).
5. **Leave it on** for several hours or overnight, as advised.
6. **Re-moisturise** when you remove them, and wash the fabric between uses.

## Safety Points

- Use the steroid strength and amount your doctor advises, since wraps increase absorption.
- Watch for infection: pus, new crusts, increasing redness or fever.
- Keep the room warm so the child does not get cold.
- Stop if the skin becomes cold, blistered or more sore.
- Wraps are for short courses, then back to daily moisturising.

## What to Expect

Many people see calmer, less itchy skin within days. Daily care afterwards prevents relapse. If flares keep returning despite wraps, a step up in treatment is considered. See [moderate to severe atopic dermatitis](/conditions/moderate-to-severe-atopic-dermatitis).
