One of the most common reasons acne treatment doesn't work isn't that the treatment itself was wrong -- it's that it was abandoned before it had a real chance to work.

## The Six-to-Eight-Week Rule

Most acne treatments, whether topical or oral, need six to eight weeks of consistent use before you can fairly judge whether they're working. Skin cell turnover and the processes acne treatments target simply operate on that kind of timeline. Expecting visible improvement in a week sets you up to abandon something that would have worked.

## Why Switching Products Constantly Backfires

We often see patients who've tried five different products in two months, each for a week or two, because none of them seemed to be "doing anything." In most cases, none of them had been given enough time to actually work, and constantly switching also increases the risk of [irritation](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier), which can look like a new problem layered on top of the original acne.

## Acne Type Matters for Treatment Choice

Acne isn't one uniform condition -- comedonal acne (blackheads and whiteheads), inflammatory acne (papules and pustules), and cystic or nodular acne all respond to somewhat different treatment approaches. Using a mismatched product for your specific acne type is another common reason treatment feels like it's not working.

## Early Flare Is Not Failure

Some treatments, especially retinoids, can make skin look worse for the first few weeks as clogged pores come to the surface. This is common and temporary, which is why we warn you in advance and why stopping early can mean missing the benefit. If skin becomes very red, sore or flaky, though, tell us so that we can adjust the plan instead of pushing through.

## Habits That Help Treatment Work

Wash gently twice a day, do not pick or squeeze spots, and use a non-comedogenic moisturizer and sunscreen. Take any oral treatment exactly as prescribed, and keep your follow-up visits, since adjustments are normal. Avoid piling on extra products, because they can irritate skin and obscure what is actually working. Our guide to [building a skincare routine](/blog/skincare-routine-order-and-active-clashes) can help keep it simple.

## Hormones, Diet and Stress

For some people, particularly women with breakouts along the jaw that flare before periods, [hormonal acne](/conditions/hormonal-acne) needs a specific approach. Stress, poor sleep and certain foods can play a role for some people, though they are rarely the whole story. We look at the full picture instead of blaming a single cause.
