When humidity drops, skin loses water faster. In Chennai, the trigger is often air-conditioning, cooler mornings or travel to colder places. The fix is simple, and it is mostly about habits.

## Bathe Smarter

Use lukewarm, not hot, water, and keep showers to about ten minutes. Use a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser only where needed, not over the whole body.

## Moisturise Within Minutes

Pat dry, and apply moisturiser while the skin is still slightly damp. Thick creams and ointments hold moisture better than thin lotions. Look for ceramides, glycerin, petrolatum or urea.

## Reapply on Problem Areas

Hands, shins, elbows and heels need more. Moisturise after washing your hands, and consider a rich hand cream at night.

## Humidity and Clothing

Do not sit with air-conditioning blowing directly on you. Cotton next to the skin is kinder than wool. Wash clothes with a mild, fragrance-free detergent and rinse well.

## Do Not Skip Sunscreen

See [sunscreen in winter](/blog/sunscreen-in-winter-still-needed).

## When Moisturiser Is Not Enough

Itching that wakes you, red scaly patches or cracks that bleed may be [eczema](/blog/eczema-flares-in-winter) or another problem. See our page on [winter dry skin](/conditions/winter-xerosis) and our [winter skin care service](/services/winter-skin-care).

## A Morning and Evening Routine You Can Follow

**Morning**
1. Rinse the face with lukewarm water, or use a gentle cleanser if skin feels oily.
2. Apply a moisturiser suited to your skin type. Light cream for the face; richer cream for body and hands.
3. Apply broad-spectrum sunscreen on exposed skin.

**Evening**
1. Bathe in lukewarm water for about ten minutes.
2. Pat dry, leaving a little dampness, and apply moisturiser within three minutes.
3. Apply a thicker layer to elbows, shins, heels and hands. A thin film of petroleum jelly over very dry patches seals in moisture overnight.

## Choosing Products

- **Cleansers:** soap-free, fragrance-free, mild. Foaming and antibacterial soaps strip oils.
- **Moisturisers:** look for glycerin, ceramides, urea or petrolatum. Gels and thin lotions suit oily skin, and creams and ointments suit dry skin. See [creams, ointments, gels and lotions explained](/blog/creams-ointments-gels-lotions-topical-formulations-explained).
- **Exfoliants and retinoids:** reduce frequency or pause during very dry spells, so the barrier can recover.
- **Fragrance and essential oils:** common sources of irritation in dry skin.

## Common Mistakes

- Long, steaming-hot showers, which feel good and strip skin.
- Applying moisturiser to completely dry skin, which is less effective than on damp skin.
- Scrubbing flaky skin, which makes the barrier worse.
- Switching to a new product every week.
- Forgetting hands, which are washed repeatedly. Keep a small tube at every sink.

## Special Areas

**Face:** avoid hot water and harsh toners. A plain moisturiser with ceramides or glycerin is enough.

**Hands:** moisturise after each wash. At night, apply thick cream and wear cotton gloves.

**Lips:** petroleum jelly or a plain lip balm. See [cracked heels and chapped lips in winter](/blog/cracked-heels-and-chapped-lips-in-winter).

**Scalp:** gentle shampoo, and see [winter hair fall and dry scalp](/blog/winter-hair-fall-and-dry-scalp).

## How Much Moisturiser Is Enough?

Most people under-apply. A reasonable guide for adults with dry skin is about a fingertip unit for each area of the body the size of two palms. See [how much to apply](/blog/how-much-topical-medicine-to-apply-fingertip-unit-guide).

## When to Stop Self-Treating

If skin is cracked and bleeding, intensely itchy, red and scaly in patches, or not improving with two weeks of a good routine, see a doctor. Underlying eczema, a fungal infection or thyroid disease can be the cause, and each needs a different treatment.
