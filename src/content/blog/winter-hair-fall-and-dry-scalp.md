Many people notice more flakes and hair on the pillow when the weather cools. Often it is ordinary, but it is worth understanding.

## Dry Scalp vs. Dandruff

Dry scalp shows as fine, white flakes with tightness, and the rest of the skin is dry too. Dandruff is more oily, with larger yellowish flakes and redness. Read [dandruff myths](/blog/dandruff-myths) for how they differ.

## Why Shedding Can Increase

Hair has natural growth cycles, and some people shed a little more in certain seasons. Hot water, harsh shampoos and scratching make the scalp unhappy. Heavy shedding that lasts months, or follows illness, stress or crash dieting, may be [telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium).

## What Helps

Wash with lukewarm water, use a gentle shampoo suited to your scalp, and do not scrub. Avoid very hot hair dryers. A light, non-greasy scalp moisturiser may help dry scalp.

## When to Get It Checked

See a dermatologist if shedding is heavy, you are seeing a wider parting or thinning, or the scalp is red and itchy, because scalp psoriasis and seborrhoeic dermatitis look similar. ## Is Seasonal Shedding Real?

Studies from several countries suggest that people shed slightly more hair in particular seasons, with peaks often reported in autumn. The change is small, usually less than a third more hairs, and doesn't cause visible thinning in most people. A true, heavy increase in shedding needs another explanation.

## Dry Scalp, Dandruff or Something Else?

**Dry scalp:** fine, white, powdery flakes, usually with dry skin elsewhere, and relief after moisturising. Often seasonal.

**Dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis:** larger, yellowish, greasy flakes with redness and itch, especially at the hairline, eyebrows and behind the ears. It tends to flare in winter and with stress. See [seborrhoeic dermatitis of the scalp](/conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-scalp).

**Scalp psoriasis:** thick, silvery plaques with sharp edges that may extend beyond the hairline. See [scalp psoriasis](/conditions/scalp-psoriasis).

**Fungal infection (tinea capitis):** patchy hair loss with scale, mainly in children.

Treatment is different for each, which is why identifying the pattern matters.

## Caring for the Scalp

- Wash with lukewarm water and a mild shampoo, two to three times a week for dry scalp, more often for oily or flaky scalp.
- Condition the lengths, not the roots.
- Do not scratch or pick at flakes.
- If your doctor has recommended a medicated shampoo, leave it on for the advised minutes before rinsing.
- Limit heat styling, tight hairstyles and chemical treatments.

## Hair Fall Checklist

Ask yourself:
- Has shedding lasted more than three months?
- Have you lost weight, been ill, or started a new medicine recently?
- Do you see a wider parting, receding temples or a thinner crown?
- Are there bald patches or a painful scalp?

Any "yes" deserves an examination. [Telogen effluvium](/conditions/telogen-effluvium) from illness or diet usually recovers, while [pattern hair loss](/conditions/androgenetic-alopecia) is progressive and benefits from early [medical treatment](/services/hair-loss-medical-treatment).

## What Doesn't Help

Expensive tonics that promise to stop winter hair fall, oiling the scalp heavily (which can worsen dandruff), and frequent hair colouring. See [dandruff myths](/blog/dandruff-myths).
