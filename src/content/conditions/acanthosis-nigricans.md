Acanthosis nigricans (AN) is a change in the skin, not a disease of its own. High insulin levels stimulate skin cells to multiply and thicken, which produces the dark, velvety look. It is therefore a visible marker of insulin resistance, which may come years before diabetes.

Many patients scrub their necks for years, and it does not help, because the colour is within the skin, not on it. Read [what the dark patches mean](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-what-dark-velvety-patches-mean), [which tests we recommend](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-insulin-resistance-and-tests), and [how it is treated](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-treatment-and-neck-darkening). For young people, see [acanthosis nigricans in children and teens](/blog/acanthosis-nigricans-in-children-and-teens).

## When to Be Concerned

Patches that appear suddenly, spread quickly, involve the lips, palms or mouth, or come with unexplained weight loss should be examined promptly.
