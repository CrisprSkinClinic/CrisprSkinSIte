Acne is one of the most common skin conditions, affecting teenagers and, increasingly, adults. It develops when hair follicles become blocked with oil and dead skin cells, and when bacteria and inflammation follow. It is not caused by poor hygiene, and scrubbing harder tends to make it worse.

## The Main Types

Acne is not one uniform condition. **Comedonal acne** consists of blackheads and whiteheads. **Inflammatory acne** adds red bumps and pustules. **Nodular or cystic acne** involves deep, painful lumps and carries the highest risk of scarring. Many people have a mixture. [Hormonal acne](/conditions/hormonal-acne), common in adult women along the jawline and chin, has its own pattern and often needs a different approach.

## Why Treatment Seems to Fail

The most common reason is not that the treatment was wrong, but that it was abandoned too early. Most acne treatments need six to eight weeks of consistent use before response can be judged, and frequently switching products raises the risk of irritation. See [why acne treatment takes time](/blog/why-acne-treatment-takes-time). The second common reason is a mismatch between the product and the acne type.

## What Evaluation Involves

We identify your acne type and severity, ask about hormonal patterns, family history, medicines, and what you have already tried, and look for signs of scarring or pigmentation. This shapes a plan and a realistic timeline, so you know what to expect and when to review.

## Treatment

Treatment is matched to severity. Mild acne often responds to topical therapy. Moderate-to-severe, widespread, or scarring acne may need oral treatment, with the appropriate monitoring. Supportive skincare matters too: a gentle routine, sun protection, and ingredients such as [niacinamide](/blog/niacinamide-for-oily-and-acne-prone-skin) may help some people, but they do not replace prescribed treatment where it is needed.

## Marks and Scars

Dark marks left after pimples are [post-inflammatory pigmentation](/conditions/post-inflammatory-hyperpigmentation) and usually fade over months, with treatment able to speed this up. True scars, which are changes in the skin's texture, are a separate problem, covered under [acne scar types](/conditions/acne-scar-types). Treating acne early and avoiding picking are the best ways to limit both.

## When to Seek Help Sooner

See us promptly for deep, painful, or nodular lesions, acne that is leaving scars, acne that is affecting your confidence or mood, or acne that has not improved after a full course of consistent treatment. Acne also needs a different assessment if it appears suddenly in adulthood with other changes such as irregular periods or excess hair growth.
