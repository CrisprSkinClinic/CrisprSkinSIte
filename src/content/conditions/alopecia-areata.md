Alopecia areata occurs when the immune system mistakenly attacks hair follicles, causing sudden, well-defined patches of hair loss, most commonly on the scalp but occasionally elsewhere on the body. It's distinct from [pattern hair loss](/conditions/androgenetic-alopecia) and requires a different diagnostic and treatment approach, usually confirmed on examination alone or with dermoscopy.

The course of alopecia areata is [genuinely unpredictable](/blog/living-with-alopecia-areata): some patches regrow spontaneously without treatment, others respond well to treatment, and the condition can recur, sometimes in new areas, over months or years. It's somewhat more common in people with a personal or family history of other autoimmune conditions, such as thyroid disease. For newer options in severe disease, see [alopecia areata: newer treatment options](/blog/alopecia-areata-newer-treatment-options).

## What It Is

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks hair follicles, causing sudden, round or oval patches of hair loss with smooth, normal-looking skin. It can affect the scalp, beard, eyebrows and body. It is not contagious, and it is not caused by stress alone, though stress can be a trigger in some people.

## Patterns

- **Patchy:** one or a few round patches.
- **Ophiasis:** a band of loss around the sides and back of the scalp.
- **Totalis and universalis:** loss of all scalp hair or all body hair. See [alopecia totalis and universalis](/conditions/alopecia-totalis-universalis).
- **Diffuse:** thinning over the scalp.

Small "exclamation mark" hairs at the edge of a patch and nail pitting are clues.

## Outlook

Many people regrow hair, sometimes without treatment, though relapses are common. The follicle stays alive, so regrowth is always possible. Early onset, extensive loss and nail changes make recovery harder.

## Treatment

- **Steroid injections** into patches, repeated every four to six weeks.
- **Topical steroids** and **topical immunotherapy.**
- **Short oral steroid courses** in selected cases.
- **JAK inhibitor tablets** for severe disease in suitable adults and adolescents, with monitoring. See [alopecia areata: newer treatment options](/blog/alopecia-areata-newer-treatment-options).
- **Camouflage** options.

## Related Conditions

People with alopecia areata have a higher chance of thyroid disease, vitiligo and eczema, so your doctor may check for them. See [living with alopecia areata](/blog/living-with-alopecia-areata) and our [alopecia areata service](/services/alopecia-areata).
