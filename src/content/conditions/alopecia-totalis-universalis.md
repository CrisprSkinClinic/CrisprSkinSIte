Alopecia totalis and alopecia universalis are more extensive forms of [alopecia areata](/conditions/alopecia-areata) -- totalis involving complete loss of scalp hair, and universalis involving complete loss of body hair, including eyebrows and eyelashes. These represent a more significant, and generally more treatment-resistant, end of the alopecia areata spectrum.

Given the greater extent of involvement, [more advanced systemic treatment options](/blog/jak-inhibitors-safety-and-monitoring) are typically considered, discussed openly in terms of their potential benefits and the monitoring they require. We also recognize the significant emotional impact this level of hair loss can have, and support patients through a condition that is as challenging emotionally as it is medically.

## What These Terms Mean

Alopecia totalis is the loss of all scalp hair. Alopecia universalis is the loss of all hair on the scalp and body, including eyebrows, lashes and body hair. Both are severe forms of alopecia areata, an autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks hair follicles. The follicles stay alive, so hair can regrow.

## Course and Outlook

The pattern is unpredictable. Some people regrow hair spontaneously, even after years, and many have cycles of loss and regrowth. Chances are lower with long-standing, extensive loss, early onset in childhood, and nail changes. Because the follicle remains intact, new treatments offer real hope.

## Treatment

Options are chosen by age, extent and duration, and by what has already been tried:

- **Topical and injected steroids,** mainly for patchy disease.
- **Topical immunotherapy,** used by specialist centres.
- **JAK inhibitor tablets,** a newer, more effective option for selected adults and adolescents, with monitoring and an honest discussion of risks. See [alopecia areata: newer treatment options](/blog/alopecia-areata-newer-treatment-options).
- **Camouflage:** wigs, scalp pigmentation and eyebrow solutions, which restore confidence while treatment proceeds.

## Emotional Wellbeing

Loss of all hair can be distressing, and support matters. Counselling and peer groups help. Sun protection is important for an unprotected scalp.
