Most people with [chronic hives](/conditions/urticaria-acute-chronic) respond to antihistamines, sometimes at higher doses than standard under medical supervision. A smaller group continues to have symptoms despite this, and this is not a sign they have done anything wrong.

For these patients, [add-on options exist](/blog/hives-not-responding-to-antihistamines), including injectable treatment that targets a specific part of the allergic pathway. Treatment is chosen after assessment, and disease often settles over time, so the plan is reviewed regularly.

## What "Resistant" Means

Chronic hives that keep coming despite a standard daily dose of a non-sedating antihistamine are called antihistamine-resistant. Many people respond when the dose is raised up to four times the usual dose, under a doctor's guidance, which is an accepted, guideline-based step. Only if that fails are other treatments considered.

## Finding Out Why

Most chronic hives have no outside trigger, and the cause is often autoimmune. A doctor may check blood counts, inflammation markers, thyroid tests and sometimes infection or autoimmunity tests, and review medicines, especially painkillers such as aspirin and anti-inflammatories that can worsen hives. Extensive allergy testing is rarely helpful, and food avoidance diets have limited evidence. See [hives and food](/blog/hives-and-food-do-low-histamine-diets-work).

## Treatment Steps

1. **Standard-dose non-sedating antihistamine,** taken daily.
2. **Increase to up to four times the dose,** as directed.
3. **Add-on injectable treatment (omalizumab)** for hives that persist despite step 2. It is highly effective for many, and given as a monthly injection.
4. **Short courses of steroid tablets** only for severe flares, not for long-term control.
5. **Other medicines** such as ciclosporin for selected, very resistant cases, with monitoring.

## Self-Care

Avoid known aggravators: heat, tight clothing, pressure, alcohol and aspirin-type painkillers if they worsen it. Keep a simple diary. Take the antihistamine every day, since stopping and starting is a common reason for poor control.

## When to Seek Urgent Help

Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing, needs emergency care.

## Outlook

Most chronic hives settle within one to five years, and treatment keeps symptoms controlled meanwhile.
