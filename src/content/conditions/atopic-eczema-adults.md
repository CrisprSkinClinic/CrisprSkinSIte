Atopic eczema in adults is often a continuation of a [childhood pattern](/conditions/childhood-atopic-dermatitis), though it can also first appear later in life. It tends to follow a cycle of flares and calmer periods, with the skin's [weakened barrier](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier) making it prone to dryness, itching, and irritation.

Treatment combines flare control with long-term barrier care. Because adult eczema can overlap with [contact dermatitis](/conditions/contact-dermatitis) and other conditions, an accurate diagnosis is the first step toward a plan that holds up over time.

## Adult Atopic Eczema Is Different From Childhood Eczema

Eczema in adults tends to affect the flexures (inner elbows and backs of knees), the neck, face and eyelids, and the hands. The skin is dry, thickened and very itchy, with scratch marks. It may begin in childhood and continue, or appear for the first time in adulthood.

## Triggers

Dry air and hot water, stress and poor sleep, irritants such as soaps and detergents, sweat, wool, infections, allergens such as dust mites and pets, and, rarely, foods. See [food allergy and eczema](/blog/food-allergy-and-eczema-when-to-test-and-when-not-to).

## Complications

Skin infection, eye problems (dryness, itch, conjunctivitis), sleep loss and low mood. Painful clustered blisters can signal a herpes infection of eczema, which needs urgent care.

## Treatment Plan

1. **Moisturise** daily, with thick creams or ointments, and after bathing.
2. **Anti-inflammatory creams** at the first sign of flare, in the right strength and for the right length of time.
3. **Steroid-sparing creams** for face, eyelids and long-term control.
4. **Light therapy** for widespread eczema.
5. **Tablets or injectable treatments** for moderate to severe disease. See [moderate to severe atopic dermatitis](/conditions/moderate-to-severe-atopic-dermatitis).
6. **Treat infections** promptly.

## Self-Care

Short lukewarm showers, mild cleansers, soft cotton, cool bedrooms, stress management, and short nails to reduce scratch damage. See [eczema triggers](/blog/eczema-trigger-identification).

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if eczema affects sleep or work, is widespread, is not controlled with moisturiser and prescribed creams, or if you see weeping, crusts or blisters. See our [eczema service](/services/eczema).
