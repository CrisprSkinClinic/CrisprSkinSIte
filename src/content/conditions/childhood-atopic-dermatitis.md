Childhood atopic dermatitis is the most common form of eczema in children, typically appearing as dry, intensely itchy, inflamed patches that present differently by age -- often on the face and scalp in infants, and in the creases of elbows and knees in older children. It's frequently linked to a personal or family history of other atopic conditions like asthma or hay fever.

Many children see it improve significantly as they grow, though the timeline varies considerably between children. In the meantime, consistent management -- a [sustainable daily moisturizing routine](/blog/managing-childhood-eczema) alongside appropriate treatment for flares -- provides genuine, practical control of the condition. For eczema that continues into adulthood, see [atopic eczema in adults](/conditions/atopic-eczema-adults).

## How It Shows in Children

In babies, eczema appears as red, dry, itchy patches on the cheeks, scalp and outer limbs. In toddlers and older children it moves to the creases of the elbows and knees, neck, wrists and ankles. Itching is intense, especially at night, and scratching damages the skin.

## What Causes It

A mix of an inherited weak skin barrier and an overactive immune response. Children with eczema are more likely to have asthma, hay fever and food allergy. Triggers include dry air, heat and sweat, soaps, wool, infections and, occasionally, foods. See [food allergy and eczema](/blog/food-allergy-and-eczema-when-to-test-and-when-not-to).

## Daily Care

1. **Bathe** briefly in lukewarm water, with a soap-free cleanser.
2. **Moisturise** straight after the bath, and again through the day.
3. **Use prescribed creams** at the first sign of a flare, in the amount advised.
4. **Dress** in soft cotton, and keep nails short.
5. **Avoid** overheating and known triggers.

See [managing childhood eczema](/blog/managing-childhood-eczema) and [baby and child skin in winter](/blog/baby-and-child-skin-in-winter).

## Infections and Vaccines

Eczema can become infected, and cold-sore virus can cause a serious flare, needing urgent care. Children with eczema should have their usual vaccines. See [vaccinating a child with eczema](/blog/vaccinating-children-with-eczema).
