A congenital melanocytic naevus is a pigmented birthmark present from birth, ranging widely in size from small spots to larger areas of pigmented skin. Most small and medium congenital naevi are monitored over time as part of routine [paediatric skin care](/services/pediatric-dermatology), with an appropriate follow-up schedule based on size and appearance.

Larger congenital naevi carry a somewhat higher, though still generally low, long-term monitoring consideration, and are discussed individually in terms of an appropriate follow-up plan, and occasionally surgical options, based on their specific size and features. See also [moles in general](/conditions/melanocytic-naevi-moles) and our guide to [birthmarks for parents](/blog/birthmarks-for-parents).

## What It Is

A congenital melanocytic naevus (CMN) is a mole present at birth or appearing in the first months. Sizes range from small (under 1.5 cm) to giant, covering a large part of the body. Colour is brown to black, often with hair growing from it.

## Why Size Matters

Small CMNs are usually harmless. The risk of problems rises with size: larger and giant naevi carry a higher lifetime risk of melanoma and, rarely, of brain involvement (neurocutaneous melanosis) when they are on the spine or head. This is why large naevi need specialist assessment.

## What to Do

- Have the naevus examined by a dermatologist, ideally in infancy.
- Photograph it regularly, so changes can be tracked.
- Protect it from the sun.
- Any change such as a new lump, ulcer, bleeding or rapid growth needs prompt review.
- For large or giant naevi, specialist care and sometimes imaging are advised.

## Treatment

Many small naevi are simply watched. Removal may be considered for appearance, for a site where changes cannot be watched, or for medical reasons, with decisions made case by case, balancing scars and risk. Large naevi may need staged surgery by specialists.

## Parents' Questions

Is it dangerous? Usually not for small naevi. Should it be removed? Not necessarily. See [should a mole be removed?](/blog/should-a-mole-be-removed-medical-vs-cosmetic-reasons) and [birthmarks](/services/birthmarks).
