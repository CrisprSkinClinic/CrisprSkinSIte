Contact dermatitis occurs when the skin reacts to direct contact with a specific substance -- either through an allergic reaction (allergic contact dermatitis) or direct irritation (irritant contact dermatitis) -- ranging from cosmetics and jewellery to plants, fragrances, and certain fabrics. The pattern and location of the reaction often provide a useful clue toward the likely category of trigger.

Resolving contact dermatitis long-term depends far more on identifying and avoiding the specific trigger than on ongoing treatment. Where the trigger isn't obvious from history and examination alone, [patch testing](/blog/patch-testing-explained) offers a more systematic way of confirming exactly what's responsible. When the hands are mainly affected, see [hand eczema](/conditions/hand-eczema).

## Two Kinds

**Irritant contact dermatitis** is direct skin damage from soaps, solvents, acids, frequent washing or friction. It can happen to anyone, with enough exposure. **Allergic contact dermatitis** is a delayed immune reaction to a specific substance, appearing one to three days after contact, in people who have become sensitised.

## Common Allergens

Nickel (jewellery, buckles, jeans studs), fragrances, preservatives in cosmetics and creams, hair dye chemicals (PPD), rubber, adhesives, some plants and topical medicines. In India, hair dye, bindis, footwear leather and cosmetics are frequent culprits.

## How It Looks

Red, itchy, scaly or blistering skin in the area of contact, often with sharp edges that follow the shape of the cause, such as a watch band or a necklace.

## Diagnosis

A careful history of products and work, and, for suspected allergy, **patch testing**: small amounts of allergens are placed on the back for 48 hours and read at 48 and 96 hours.

## Treatment

1. **Identify and avoid** the cause.
2. **Calm the skin** with a short course of steroid cream.
3. **Repair the barrier** with plain moisturiser.
4. **Switch to fragrance-free,** hypoallergenic products.

## Outlook

Allergic dermatitis can return whenever contact recurs, so avoidance is the cure. See [hand eczema](/conditions/hand-eczema), [damaged skin barrier](/conditions/damaged-skin-barrier) and our [skin allergy service](/services/skin-allergy).
