Heel skin is thick and has few oil glands, so it dries easily. Pressure from standing spreads the heel and splits the dry skin. Treat it early, before cracks are deep.

Our guide on [cracked heels and chapped lips in winter](/blog/cracked-heels-and-chapped-lips-in-winter) has a step-by-step routine. People with diabetes should have any heel crack looked at promptly.

## Types and Severity

**Mild:** rough, dry and flaky heels with no cracks.

**Moderate:** visible fissures that do not reach the deeper tissues, with thick, yellowish edges.

**Severe:** deep, painful fissures that bleed or ooze, sometimes with signs of infection.

## What Else Can Cause Thick, Cracked Heels

- **Athlete's foot (moccasin type):** dry scaling on the soles and heels, often with one hand also affected. See [tinea infections](/conditions/tinea-infections).
- **Eczema or psoriasis of the soles.**
- **Keratoderma:** inherited or acquired thickening of the soles.
- **Diabetes and nerve damage:** dry skin from reduced sweating.
- **Hypothyroidism.**
- **Medicines and chemicals.**

If cracks keep returning despite good care, see a doctor to look for such causes.

## Treatment Details

Softening agents such as urea (10 to 40 percent), salicylic acid or lactic acid break down thick skin. Paring by a doctor or podiatrist removes hard edges. Fissures may be covered with protective dressings or skin glue. Infection is treated with antiseptics or antibiotics, and fungus with antifungal treatment. For persistent thick skin, stronger options can be considered by your dermatologist.

## Special Advice for People With Diabetes

Even a small fissure can become infected and slow to heal. Inspect your feet every day, do not walk barefoot, avoid cutting calluses with blades, and see a doctor promptly for any crack, blister or sore. See our service on [fungal infections](/services/fungal-infections) for related foot problems.

## Prevention

Daily moisturiser, well-fitting closed footwear, moisture-wicking socks, avoiding prolonged barefoot walking and treating foot fungus early keep heels healthy through the year. See [cracked heels and chapped lips in winter](/blog/cracked-heels-and-chapped-lips-in-winter).
