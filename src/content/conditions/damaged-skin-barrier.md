The outer layer of the skin works as a barrier, keeping moisture in and irritants out. Harsh products, over-exfoliation, and [piling on strong active ingredients](/blog/skincare-routine-order-and-active-clashes) can break it down, leaving skin that stings, flakes, and reacts unpredictably.

Most mild cases recover when the routine is stripped back to basics for a few weeks. Skin that does not improve, or that keeps flaring, deserves an examination to check for an underlying condition, because [eczema](/services/eczema) or an [allergic reaction to a product](/conditions/contact-dermatitis) can mimic a simple barrier problem.

## What the Barrier Does

The outer layer of skin works like a brick wall, with skin cells as bricks and fats as mortar, keeping water in and irritants out. When it is damaged, skin stings, tightens, flakes and reacts to products that were previously fine.

## Common Causes

Over-exfoliation, too many active ingredients at once (acids, retinoids, vitamin C), harsh or foaming cleansers, very hot water, low humidity and air-conditioning, sun exposure, and underlying eczema or rosacea. A routine of many steps is a frequent culprit. See [skincare routine: order and clashes](/blog/skincare-routine-order-and-active-clashes).

## How It Shows

Burning or stinging with products, redness, dry flaky skin, a shiny tight look, sudden breakouts, and sensitivity to water and sunscreen.

## Repair Plan

1. **Strip the routine back** for two to four weeks: a gentle cleanser, a plain moisturiser and sunscreen only.
2. **Pause** acids, retinoids, scrubs, vitamin C and fragranced products.
3. **Use a barrier cream** with ceramides, glycerin or petrolatum.
4. **Use lukewarm water** and pat dry.
5. **Reintroduce** one product at a time, a week apart, starting with the gentlest.

## When It Is More Than a Barrier Problem

Persistent redness, flushing or pimples may be rosacea, eczema or contact allergy to a product. See a doctor if skin does not improve in two to four weeks. See [eczema](/services/eczema).

## Prevention

Keep routines simple, introduce actives slowly, and avoid combining several strong products. See [a winter dry skin routine that works](/blog/winter-dry-skin-routine-that-works).
