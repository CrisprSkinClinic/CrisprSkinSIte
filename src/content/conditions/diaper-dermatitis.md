Diaper dermatitis is a common [irritation](/conditions/contact-dermatitis) caused by prolonged contact with moisture and friction in the diaper area, generally responding well to basic measures like frequent diaper changes, gentle cleansing, and a protective barrier cream. Most cases improve within a few days with consistent care.

Rash that's more severe, spreading, or not improving with basic measures can involve a secondary fungal or bacterial infection, which needs a different, targeted treatment alongside continued barrier care -- [an evaluation](/services/pediatric-dermatology) helps distinguish straightforward diaper rash from these less common but treatable complications. For other common rashes in children, see [common childhood skin conditions](/blog/common-childhood-skin-conditions).

## Why It Happens

Diaper (nappy) dermatitis is inflammation of the skin under a diaper. Urine and stool irritate the skin, moisture softens it, and friction damages it. Yeast (candida) and bacteria can take advantage. It is very common, and affects most babies at some stage.

## Types

- **Irritant:** shiny red skin in the convex areas, sparing the creases.
- **Candida (yeast):** bright red with small satellite spots and involvement of the skin folds. Common after antibiotics.
- **Mixed or bacterial:** crusts, pustules or sores.
- **Other conditions:** psoriasis and seborrhoeic dermatitis can look similar.

## Prevention and Home Care

1. **Change often,** and promptly after stool.
2. **Clean gently** with water or a mild cleanser, and pat dry, without rubbing.
3. **Give nappy-free time** to air the skin.
4. **Apply a thick barrier cream** (zinc oxide or petrolatum) at each change.
5. **Avoid** scented wipes, powders and tight diapers.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor if there is no improvement in three to four days, if there are pustules, blisters, sores, bleeding, fever, or a bright red rash with satellite spots, which needs an anti-yeast cream. Do not use steroid creams on babies without advice.
