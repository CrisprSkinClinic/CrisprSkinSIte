Expression lines, also called dynamic wrinkles, form where repeated facial movement creases the skin: across the forehead, between the eyebrows, and at the outer corners of the eyes. At first they appear only with movement. Over years, as the skin loses elasticity, they can remain visible at rest.

## Why Treatment Differs From Other Wrinkles

Not every line has the same cause. Expression lines are driven by muscle movement, so they respond to treatments that relax those muscles. Lines caused mainly by volume loss, sun damage, or skin thinning respond better to other approaches, which is why an assessment matters before treatment. See [Botox vs. fillers](/blog/fillers-vs-botox) for how the two differ.

## How Treatment Works

A small amount of a muscle-relaxing medicine is placed into specific muscles. Results build over several days and typically last a few months, so many people choose periodic maintenance. The dose and placement determine how natural the result looks, and a conservative starting approach, as described in [getting natural-looking Botox results](/blog/botox-natural-results), lets movement remain while lines soften.

## Where Technique Matters

The forehead and the area around the eyes are where dosing and placement make the biggest visible difference. Over-treating the forehead can flatten expression or lower the brows, while under-treating the frown area may leave the lines unchanged. Experience and a conservative first session help avoid both.

## Prevention and Skin Health

Daily sun protection and a sound skincare routine do more for skin quality over the long term than any single procedure. They also slow the progression from dynamic lines to static ones. See [photoageing](/conditions/photoageing) for how sun exposure shapes the skin.

## Realistic Expectations

Treatment softens lines and slows their deepening, but it does not stop ageing or fix every change in the face. We discuss what bothers you most and what is realistic, and we do not recommend treatment for everyone who asks.

## Safety

Treatment is generally well tolerated when performed by a trained doctor, though temporary bruising, headache, or minor asymmetry can occur. Tell us about your medical history, medicines, and any plans for pregnancy before treatment.
