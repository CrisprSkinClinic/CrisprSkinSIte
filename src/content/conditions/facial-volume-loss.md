As we age, the face changes in more ways than wrinkles. Fat pads shrink and shift, bone support recedes, and skin thins. The result is hollowing in the cheeks and temples, deeper grooves around the mouth and under the eyes, and a loss of the smooth contour of younger faces. These changes are often what makes someone look tired, even when they feel well.

## Why Volume Matters

Many people focus on lines, but volume loss is often the underlying cause of how a face looks. Treating a single groove without considering the volume above it can look unnatural. That is why we assess the whole face first. For the differences between treatments, see [Botox vs. fillers](/blog/fillers-vs-botox).

## How Fillers Are Used

Hyaluronic acid-based fillers are placed to restore volume or definition in specific areas, such as the cheeks, tear troughs, or jawline. Fillers add volume directly and do not affect muscle movement. Results are visible immediately and last for a period that varies by product and area, so treatment is repeated as needed.

## A Conservative Approach

Overcorrection is more noticeable than undercorrection, particularly in the lips and under the eyes. We prefer to start conservatively and build gradually, based on how the first treatment looks, rather than aim for a dramatic change in one visit. Natural-looking results come from a plan, not a large volume.

## Combining Treatments

Many patients have both volume loss and expression lines. Fillers and muscle-relaxing treatment address different problems and can be combined where both are present. See [expression lines](/conditions/expression-lines), and our article on an [individualized approach to anti-ageing](/blog/anti-ageing-individualized-approach).

## Skin Quality and Prevention

Fillers do not replace sun protection and a good skincare routine, which preserve the skin that fillers sit under. See [photoageing](/conditions/photoageing).

## Risks and Safety

Temporary swelling, bruising, and tenderness are common. Less common risks include lumps, asymmetry, and, rarely, serious complications involving blood vessels, which is why treatment should be done by a trained doctor with knowledge of facial anatomy. We discuss risks and suitability before treatment, and tell you plainly if we recommend waiting or choosing a different approach.
