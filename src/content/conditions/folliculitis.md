Folliculitis is inflammation of hair follicles. It looks like small pimples with a hair in the centre, and appears anywhere there is hair, most often on the beard area, chest, back, buttocks and thighs.

## Types

- **Bacterial (Staph) folliculitis:** tender pustules, the commonest type.
- **Pseudofolliculitis (razor bumps):** ingrown hairs after shaving, common in curly hair. See [razor bumps](/blog/razor-bumps-pseudofolliculitis-barbae-causes-and-treatment).
- **Yeast (Malassezia) folliculitis:** itchy, uniform bumps on the chest, back and upper arms, often mistaken for acne.
- **Hot-tub folliculitis:** from unclean water.
- **Boils (furuncles) and carbuncles:** deeper, painful infections.

## Treatment

Gentle washing with an antibacterial wash, topical antibiotics for limited disease, antifungal treatment for yeast folliculitis, and oral antibiotics for widespread or deep infection. Boils respond to warm compresses, and when they come to a head, a doctor can drain them. Do not squeeze them. See [folliculitis and boils](/blog/folliculitis-and-boils-causes-and-treatment).

## Recurrent Boils

A doctor checks for diabetes, anaemia and bacterial carriage, and may advise a decolonisation routine. See [recurrent boils and staph prevention](/blog/recurrent-boils-and-staph-prevention). Deep, repeated lumps in the armpits and groin may be hidradenitis. See [hidradenitis suppurativa](/conditions/hidradenitis-suppurativa).

## Prevention

Loose clothes, shower after sweating, clean razors, shave along the direction of hair growth, do not share towels, and keep skin folds dry.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor for boils on the face, spine or groin, fever, spreading redness or recurrent episodes. See [common skin infections](/services/common-skin-infections).
