Shingles happens when the chickenpox virus, which stays dormant in nerve cells after childhood, wakes up decades later. It causes a painful, blistering rash that follows a band on one side of the body. Most people recover in a few weeks, but some are left with burning nerve pain that lasts months.

Read our guide to [the first 72 hours of shingles](/blog/shingles-symptoms-treatment-first-72-hours) and to [who should get the shingles vaccine](/blog/shingles-vaccine-who-should-get-it).

## Urgent Warning Signs

A rash on the forehead, around the eye or on the tip of the nose can threaten vision. A rash in the ear, with facial weakness, or widespread blisters in someone with weakened immunity also needs urgent care.
