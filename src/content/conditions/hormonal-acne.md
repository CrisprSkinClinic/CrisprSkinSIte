Hormonal acne is a distinct pattern of [acne](/conditions/acne-vulgaris) linked to hormonal fluctuations, most commonly affecting adult women along the jawline and chin, often flaring in a pattern that correlates with the menstrual cycle. It can also be associated with underlying hormonal conditions such as PCOS.

Because the underlying driver is hormonal rather than purely related to oil production or bacteria on the skin surface, hormonal acne often responds better to treatment that specifically addresses this component, considered alongside [standard topical therapy](/services/acne) as part of a complete plan. ## Recognising Hormonal Acne

Hormonal acne typically appears in adults, especially women, along the jawline, chin and neck, as deep, tender bumps that heal slowly and flare before periods. It can start in the twenties and continue into the thirties and forties, and often resists ordinary acne creams.

## What Drives It

Oil glands in the lower face respond strongly to androgen hormones. Cycle changes, PCOS, stress, sleep loss and stopping hormonal contraception can all play a part. See [PCOS signs on the skin](/blog/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs-checklist).

## Evaluation

Doctors ask about periods, weight, excess hair, hair thinning and family history, and suggest tests if signs point to PCOS or another hormonal condition. See [when to test for PCOS](/blog/hormonal-acne-and-pcos-when-to-test).

## Treatment

- **Topical:** retinoids, benzoyl peroxide and azelaic acid.
- **Hormone-directed tablets:** certain contraceptive pills and spironolactone in suitable women.
- **Antibiotic tablets** for short periods.
- **Isotretinoin** for severe or scarring acne.
- **Marks and scars:** peels, and scar treatment when needed. See [acne scars](/services/acne-scars).

Each medicine has precautions, and some cannot be used in pregnancy. See [hormonal acne medicines explained](/blog/hormonal-acne-medicines-explained) and [jawline acne causes and treatment](/blog/hormonal-acne-jawline-causes-and-treatment).

## Timeline

Expect improvement in two to three months, and best results by six. Maintenance is often needed to prevent relapse. See our [hormonal skin and hair service](/services/hormonal-skin-conditions) and [acne service](/services/acne).
