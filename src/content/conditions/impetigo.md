Impetigo is a superficial skin infection caused by Staphylococcus aureus or Streptococcus. It is very contagious, spreads by touch and shared towels, and is most common in children, especially in warm, humid weather.

## Types

- **Non-bullous (common):** small blisters that burst quickly, leaving honey-coloured crusts, around the nose and mouth and on exposed skin.
- **Bullous:** larger fluid-filled blisters that break and leave a rim, more common in babies.
- **Ecthyma:** a deeper, ulcerated form.

## Treatment

Remove crusts gently after soaking in warm water, then apply the antibiotic ointment prescribed, usually for five to seven days. Widespread impetigo, bullous impetigo, fever or no improvement needs antibiotic tablets. Do not use steroid creams on infected skin, and do not scratch. See [impetigo in children](/blog/impetigo-in-children-treatment-school-and-spread).

## School and Spread

Children are usually advised to stay home until treatment has been under way for about a day and the sores are drying. Keep nails short, wash hands, and give separate towels and bedding to the child. Cover sores where possible.

## Complications

Cellulitis, spread to deeper skin and, rarely after strep infection, kidney inflammation. See a doctor for fever, spreading redness, swelling or dark urine.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor for any suspected impetigo, especially in babies and in children with eczema, and for recurrent episodes. See [common skin infections](/services/common-skin-infections) and our [paediatric dermatology service](/services/pediatric-dermatology).
