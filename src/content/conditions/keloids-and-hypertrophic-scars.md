Hypertrophic scars and keloids are both raised, firm scars caused by an overgrowth of collagen during healing. They differ in how they behave.

## Hypertrophic Scar or Keloid?

- **Hypertrophic:** stays within the original wound, appears within weeks, and often flattens and fades over one to two years. Responds well to treatment.
- **Keloid:** grows beyond the wound edges, may keep growing for months or years, and rarely improves by itself. It often returns after surgery.

## Why Some People Scar This Way

Genes, darker skin and young age make overgrowth more likely. Triggers include any wound: ear piercings, acne, surgery, burns, vaccinations and even minor injuries. Wounds under tension, such as on the chest and shoulders, are at higher risk.

## Treatment

New raised scars respond best to early treatment with silicone and steroid injections. Established keloids usually need a combination: injections repeated for months, freezing in some, laser for redness, and, for large keloids, surgery combined with injections, pressure and sometimes radiation by specialists. See [keloid treatment: what works and why keloids return](/blog/keloid-treatment-what-works-and-why-keloids-return) and [keloids on the earlobe](/blog/keloids-on-the-earlobe-what-to-know).

## Prevention

Treat acne early, avoid unnecessary piercings or elective surgery in keloid-prone areas, protect new scars from the sun and tension, use silicone after surgery, and tell any surgeon about your history. See [caring for a surgical scar](/blog/surgical-scar-care-first-six-months).

## Realistic Outcomes

Most scars can be made flatter, softer and more comfortable. Recurrence of keloids is common, so follow-up matters. See [scar types explained](/blog/scar-types-hypertrophic-keloid-and-atrophic-explained) and our [scar revision service](/services/scar-revision).
