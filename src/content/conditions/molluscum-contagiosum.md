Molluscum contagiosum is a common viral skin infection in children, presenting as small, smooth, pearly bumps, often with a characteristic central dimple. It spreads through direct skin contact or shared items, which is why it often appears in small clusters among children in close contact with one another.

Because molluscum commonly resolves on its own within months to about a year without treatment, [watchful waiting](/blog/viral-skin-infections-school) -- combined with simple hygiene guidance to limit spread -- is often the most appropriate approach, and this is explained clearly to parents as a legitimate medical choice rather than a lack of care. Active removal remains an option where the child is bothered by the lesions or a parent prefers it. Warts follow a similar pattern; see [viral warts](/conditions/viral-warts).

## What It Is

Molluscum contagiosum is a common viral skin infection of young children, causing small, firm, dome-shaped, pearly bumps with a central dimple. They appear on the trunk, armpits, folds and limbs, often in groups, and spread by skin contact, shared towels and scratching. In adults it can be sexually transmitted.

## Course

Each bump lasts weeks to months, and the infection usually clears by itself within six to twelve months, sometimes up to two years or more. It is harmless, but can itch, and surrounding eczema is common (molluscum dermatitis).

## Treatment

Many children need no treatment, since lesions resolve alone and treatments can be uncomfortable. Treatment may be advised for spreading disease, bumps in sensitive areas, itching or inflammation, children with eczema and adults. Options include gentle topical treatments, cryotherapy and curettage (for older children), done with care and often with numbing cream. If lesions become red and sore, there may be infection.

## Preventing Spread

Do not scratch or squeeze bumps, cover them, avoid sharing towels, and wash hands. Children can usually continue school and swimming, with covered lesions if advised.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist for lesions near the eyes, spreading quickly, infected bumps, in children with eczema or in adults with many lesions. See [molluscum and warts at school](/blog/viral-skin-infections-school) and our [viral skin infections service](/services/viral-skin-infections).
