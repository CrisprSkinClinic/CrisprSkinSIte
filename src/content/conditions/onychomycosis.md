Onychomycosis is a fungal infection of the nail, causing discoloration, thickening, and sometimes separation from the nail bed. Because several other conditions -- including [nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis) and trauma -- can produce similar-looking nail changes, [confirming the diagnosis](/blog/nail-changes-when-to-see-a-doctor) with a nail sample sent for microscopy or culture is an important step before committing to what is typically a months-long treatment course.

Nail growth is inherently slow -- fingernails take months and toenails can take the better part of a year to grow out fully -- so visible improvement during treatment is gradual by nature, and this timeline is explained clearly upfront so progress is judged accurately.

## Staying on Treatment

Nails grow slowly, so treatment continues for months. Stopping early is the commonest reason for failure. See [how long to continue antifungal treatment](/blog/tinea-how-long-to-continue-treatment).

## Timeline

For how long treatment takes and what to expect month by month, see [fungal nail infection: treatment timeline](/blog/fungal-nail-infection-treatment-timeline-and-what-to-expect).

## Recognising Fungal Nails

Nails turn yellow, white or brown, thicken, crumble and may lift from the nail bed, with debris underneath. Toenails are affected far more than fingernails, and athlete's foot often goes along with it.

## Why Diagnosis Matters

Many nail problems look like fungus, including nail psoriasis, injury, eczema and lichen planus. Treatment lasts months, so confirmation by a nail clipping examined under the microscope or cultured is worthwhile. See [nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis).

## Treatment

- **Topical nail lacquers or solutions** for early, mild disease or when tablets are unsuitable.
- **Antifungal tablets,** the most effective option, taken for around six weeks for fingernails and about twelve weeks for toenails, with liver safety checks and a medicine review.
- **Nail trimming** and thinning to help treatment penetrate.
- **Treating athlete's foot** at the same time.

## What to Expect

Because toenails grow about a millimetre a month, a clear nail takes twelve to eighteen months to grow out, even when the fungus is gone. Infection can come back, so prevention measures matter.

## Prevention

Keep feet dry, wear breathable socks and shoes, use sandals in communal showers, do not share clippers, and treat shoes with antifungal powder.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor for a nail that changes colour or shape, particularly with diabetes or if painful. See [nail disorders](/services/nail-disorders) and [tinea infections](/conditions/tinea-infections).
