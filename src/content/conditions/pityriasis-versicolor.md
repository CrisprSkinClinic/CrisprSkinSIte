Pityriasis versicolor is caused by overgrowth of a yeast that normally lives on the skin. It produces fine-scaled patches whose colour differs from the surrounding skin, which is why it is often confused with [vitiligo](/conditions/vitiligo) or other pigmentation problems.

[Treatment](/services/fungal-infections) clears the infection quickly, but the pigment difference can take weeks to even out after the yeast is gone. That delay is normal and not a sign that treatment failed. Recurrence is common; see [why fungal infections keep coming back](/blog/fungal-infection-recurrence).

## What Causes It

A yeast called Malassezia lives on everyone's skin. In hot, humid weather, with sweating or oily skin, it multiplies and interferes with pigment. It is not caused by poor hygiene, and it is not strongly contagious.

## Appearance

Round or oval patches on the upper back, chest, neck and arms, either paler or darker than the surrounding skin, with a fine bran-like scale. The patches are more obvious after sun exposure, because affected skin does not tan. Itching is usually mild.

## Diagnosis

Usually by examination, with a skin scraping under the microscope to see the yeast. A special light can help. It is important to tell it from vitiligo, which has smooth, chalk-white patches without scale.

## Treatment

Anti-yeast shampoos or washes left on the skin for the advised time, antifungal creams for small areas, and tablets for widespread or repeated cases. Colour takes weeks to months to even out after the yeast is cleared.

## Recurrence

Relapse is common in hot, humid climates. A once-a-month wash in the warm months, loose breathable clothing and treating dandruff help. See our [fungal infections service](/services/fungal-infections).
