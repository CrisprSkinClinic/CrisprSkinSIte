Plaque psoriasis is the most common form of psoriasis, a chronic autoimmune condition that causes skin cells to build up rapidly, forming thick, red, scaly patches most often seen on the elbows, knees, and lower back. It is [not contagious](/blog/psoriasis-not-contagious), a persistent and unfortunate misconception that can affect how patients are treated socially.

As a chronic, relapsing-remitting condition, plaque psoriasis is managed with a long-term, sustained treatment plan rather than positioned around a single permanent cure, with topical therapy, phototherapy, or [systemic treatment](/blog/psoriasis-treatment-ladder) considered based on extent and severity. Where disease is extensive, see [moderate-to-severe psoriasis](/conditions/moderate-to-severe-psoriasis).

## What It Looks Like

Plaque psoriasis is the commonest type: well-defined, raised, red or purplish patches covered with silvery-white scale, most often on the elbows, knees, scalp and lower back. Plaques can itch or burn, and crack. Nails and joints may be involved. See [scalp psoriasis](/conditions/scalp-psoriasis), [nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis) and [psoriatic arthritis](/conditions/psoriatic-arthritis).

## What Causes It

Psoriasis is an immune-driven condition in which skin cells renew too fast, forming plaques. It runs in families and is not contagious. Triggers include throat infections, stress, skin injury, some medicines, smoking and alcohol, obesity, and cold, dry weather.

## Treatment Ladder

1. **Topical treatments:** steroid creams, vitamin D analogues, tar and salicylic acid, used for mild disease.
2. **Phototherapy:** narrowband UVB for widespread plaques.
3. **Tablets:** methotrexate, acitretin, ciclosporin and other systemic medicines.
4. **Biologics and targeted medicines** for moderate to severe disease. See [biologics and advanced therapy](/services/biologics).

See [the psoriasis treatment ladder](/blog/psoriasis-treatment-ladder).

## Living With Psoriasis

Moisturise, avoid picking and skin injury, stop smoking, limit alcohol, keep a healthy weight and manage stress. See [psoriasis and diet](/blog/psoriasis-diet-weight-alcohol-gluten-evidence) if available, and [psoriasis in winter](/blog/psoriasis-in-winter-why-flares) if available.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if plaques are widespread, painful or affect your life, if you have joint pain or nail changes, or if creams are not working. See our [psoriasis service](/services/psoriasis).
