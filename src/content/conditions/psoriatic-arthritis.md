A proportion of people with [psoriasis](/conditions/plaque-psoriasis) develop inflammation in their joints, known as psoriatic arthritis. It can appear years after the skin disease, and occasionally before it. Untreated, it can cause lasting joint damage, so early recognition matters.

Treating the skin and joints together is often the most effective approach, and [some medicines address both](/blog/biologics-in-dermatology-explained). If you have psoriasis and persistent joint symptoms, [mention them at your visit](/services/psoriasis) so they can be assessed properly. Nail changes can also be an early clue; see [nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis).

## What It Is

Psoriatic arthritis is an inflammatory arthritis that affects some people with psoriasis, sometimes years after the skin disease, and occasionally before it. Untreated, it can permanently damage joints, so early recognition matters.

## Symptoms to Notice

- Morning stiffness lasting more than thirty minutes that eases with movement.
- Swollen, painful joints, especially the fingers and toes.
- A whole finger or toe swollen like a sausage (dactylitis).
- Heel pain or pain at tendon attachments (enthesitis).
- Back or buttock pain that is worse at rest and better with exercise.
- Nail pitting or lifting, which raises the risk. See [nail psoriasis](/conditions/nail-psoriasis).

## Diagnosis

A doctor examines joints and nails, reviews symptoms and often arranges blood tests and X-rays or scans. A rheumatologist usually confirms the diagnosis and shares care with the dermatologist.

## Treatment

Treatment aims to relieve pain and prevent joint damage. It ranges from anti-inflammatory painkillers and disease-modifying tablets such as methotrexate, to biologic injections for moderate to severe disease, which can treat skin and joints together. See [biologics and advanced therapy](/services/biologics) and [the psoriasis treatment ladder](/blog/psoriasis-treatment-ladder).

## Self-Care

Keep active with gentle exercise, protect joints, maintain a healthy weight, avoid smoking and limit alcohol. Tell your doctor about any new joint pain.

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor promptly if you have psoriasis and persistent joint stiffness, swelling or pain. Starting treatment early gives the best chance of protecting the joints. See [psoriasis](/services/psoriasis).
