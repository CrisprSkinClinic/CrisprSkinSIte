Rosacea is a chronic inflammatory condition of the face. The exact cause is not known, but it involves sensitive blood vessels, an overactive skin immune response, sun damage and sometimes skin mites (Demodex). It is not caused by poor hygiene or alcohol, though alcohol can worsen flushing.

## Subtypes

- **Redness and flushing type** with visible vessels.
- **Bumps and pimples type,** often confused with acne.
- **Thickened skin type (phymatous),** usually of the nose.
- **Eye rosacea.** See [rosacea and the eyes](/blog/rosacea-eyes-flushing-and-when-to-see-a-doctor).

## Why It Gets Missed

In brown skin, redness is less obvious and may appear as dusky, warm, tender skin. People treat it as acne, buy strong products and steroid creams, and make it worse.

## Treatment

1. **Triggers and care:** identify triggers, use a gentle routine and daily sunscreen. See [rosacea triggers and skin care](/blog/rosacea-triggers-and-everyday-skin-care).
2. **Topical medicines:** azelaic acid, metronidazole and ivermectin for bumps, and brimonidine or oxymetazoline for redness.
3. **Oral medicine:** low-dose doxycycline for moderate disease, and sometimes others.
4. **Light and laser** for persistent redness and visible vessels.
5. **Severe or resistant cases:** specialist treatment, including low-dose isotretinoin.

See [rosacea treatment options](/blog/rosacea-treatment-options-creams-tablets-and-laser).

## Avoid

Steroid creams on the face, abrasive scrubs and alcohol-based toners.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if redness persists beyond a few weeks, if acne treatments are not working, if eyes are red and gritty, or if a nose is thickening. See our [rosacea service](/services/rosacea).
