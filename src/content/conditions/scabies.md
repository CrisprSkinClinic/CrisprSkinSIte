Scabies is caused by a tiny mite, Sarcoptes scabiei, which burrows into the top layer of skin. It spreads through prolonged skin contact and shared bedding, and is common in families, hostels and crowded settings. It is not a sign of poor hygiene.

## Diagnosis

Usually from the pattern of itch and the distribution of bumps and burrows, with a dermatoscope or a skin scraping if needed. Scabies is often mistaken for eczema or allergy, and treated with steroid creams, which hide the signs and let it spread.

## Treatment

Permethrin cream is applied to the whole body below the neck (and the scalp and face in children and the elderly), left on for the time advised, then washed off and repeated after a week. Tablets (ivermectin) are an alternative, taken twice a week apart, but not suitable for pregnant women and small children. Everyone in the household and close contacts must be treated together. See [scabies treatment step by step](/blog/scabies-treatment-step-by-step-and-why-the-itch-lasts).

## After Treatment

Itching and bumps can last two to four weeks, since the skin is still reacting to dead mites. Moisturisers and antihistamines help. New burrows or new itching after two weeks suggest reinfestation or treatment failure, and need review.

## Preventing Reinfestation

Wash clothes, bedding and towels at the hottest suitable temperature, and dry them in the sun or on high heat, or seal them in a bag for several days. Vacuum mattresses and sofas. See [scabies in families and schools](/blog/scabies-in-families-and-schools-preventing-reinfection).

## When to See a Doctor

See a doctor for night-time itching that others share, rashes between the fingers, itching in babies or people with weak immunity, or infected sores. See [common skin infections](/services/common-skin-infections).
