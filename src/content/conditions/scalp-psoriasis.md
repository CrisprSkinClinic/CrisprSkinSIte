Scalp psoriasis causes thick, well-defined, scaly patches on the scalp that can extend beyond the hairline, and is frequently mistaken for [severe dandruff](/blog/dandruff-myths) since both cause visible flaking. The scale in scalp psoriasis tends to be thicker and more tightly adherent than typical dandruff, and standard anti-dandruff shampoos are usually far less effective, since the underlying cause is different.

Treatment typically requires medicated formulations specifically suited to scalp application, and where psoriasis is present elsewhere on the body as well, scalp-specific treatment is coordinated as part of a [broader management plan](/services/psoriasis) rather than treated in isolation. Greasy, yellowish scale points instead to [seborrheic dermatitis](/conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-scalp).

## What It Looks Like

Scalp psoriasis causes red, raised, well-defined patches with thick silvery scale, often extending beyond the hairline onto the forehead, ears and neck. It can be mild, with fine scaling, or thick and crusted, and it itches or burns. It is often mistaken for dandruff, but plaques are thicker and sharper-edged. See [seborrhoeic dermatitis of the scalp](/conditions/seborrheic-dermatitis-scalp).

## Hair and Scalp Psoriasis

Hair loss can occur temporarily with heavy scaling and picking, and usually regrows when psoriasis is controlled. Do not pick or pull scale off, since this can cause bleeding and new plaques.

## Treatment

1. **Soften and remove scale:** salicylic acid or oil applied for a few hours or overnight, then washed out.
2. **Medicated shampoos:** tar, ketoconazole or salicylic acid shampoos, left on for the time advised.
3. **Steroid scalp lotions or foams,** often combined with vitamin D analogues, in the right strength for the right time.
4. **Phototherapy** for widespread scalp disease, with devices that part the hair.
5. **Tablets and biologics** for severe cases, which also treat skin elsewhere. See [biologics and advanced therapy](/services/biologics).

## Self-Care

Gentle washing, a soft brush, not scratching, sun in moderation, and avoiding triggers such as stress and smoking. Winter makes it worse. See [psoriasis in winter](/blog/psoriasis-in-winter-why-flares) and [winter hair fall and dry scalp](/blog/winter-hair-fall-and-dry-scalp).

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if flaking does not respond to anti-dandruff shampoo, if scale is thick, if there is hair loss or if you have joint pain. See our [psoriasis service](/services/psoriasis).
