The earlobe is soft tissue with a limited blood supply and little structural support, so it does not tolerate force well. A heavy earring wearing through the piercing over years, or a sudden tear from a snag or pulled earring, can leave a split or stretched lobe. It is very common, and it is treatable.

## Types

**Complete split.** The lobe has torn through the bottom edge, creating a cleft. This often results from an earring being pulled, and the edges heal separately with a rounded, scarred margin.

**Partial split or enlarged hole.** The piercing has widened and elongated, sometimes into a slit, but the lobe's lower edge is intact.

**Stretched lobe.** The whole lobe has become longer and thinner from long-term weight, with an enlarged piercing.

## Why Old Splits Need Surgery

Once the edges of a tear have healed separately, a thin lining of skin grows along each edge. Because of that lining, the two sides will not simply knit together, and a cleft remains. To repair it, the lining has to be removed so fresh raw edges can be joined. That is what the repair involves.

## Fresh Tears

A recent tear, where the lobe has just been torn, is a different situation. Early assessment can sometimes allow the edges to be repaired promptly. Clean the area, control bleeding with gentle pressure, and see us as soon as you can.

## How Repair Works

Under local anaesthetic, the edges are refreshed and the lobe is closed in layers with fine stitches. Different closure techniques, including straight-line and shaped closures, are used depending on the type of tear and whether a new piercing is wanted later. See [split earlobe repair explained](/blog/split-earlobe-repair-explained) and our service page on [ear lobe repair](/services/ear-lobe-repair).

## Recovery and Earrings

Stitches usually come out after about a week. The lobe is protected while it heals, and re-piercing waits until the repair is well settled, commonly several weeks to a few months. See [recovery and re-piercing](/blog/earlobe-repair-recovery-and-re-piercing).

## Scarring

The repair leaves a fine scar, which usually fades. People who tend to form keloids or thick scars need special attention, because the repair can provoke one. If you have had a keloid before, tell us in advance.

## Prevention

Choose lighter earrings, avoid pulling on them, take them out for sport or sleep, and avoid very heavy designs for long wear. See [preventing torn and stretched earlobes](/blog/preventing-torn-and-stretched-earlobes).

## Other Lobe Concerns

Not every lump or mark on the earlobe is a tear-related problem. Small growths can be [skin tags](/conditions/skin-tags) or [moles](/conditions/melanocytic-naevi-moles), and an examination sorts them out.
