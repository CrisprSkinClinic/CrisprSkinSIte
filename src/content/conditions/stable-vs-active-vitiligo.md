Vitiligo does not behave the same way in every person, or even at every point in the same person's life. Some patients have a patch that has not changed in years. Others notice new spots every few weeks. This difference, between active and stable disease, is one of the most important things we establish at the first visit, because it decides which treatments are sensible and in what order.

## What Active Vitiligo Means

Active vitiligo means pigment loss is still progressing. The immune process is still destroying pigment cells, so new patches appear or existing ones enlarge. Typical features include recent appearance of new patches, patches that have grown over weeks to months, and the appearance of patches at sites of friction, pressure, injury, or sunburn, which is called the Koebner phenomenon. In some patients, borders appear blurred or have a slightly pale or inflamed margin. Some patients describe the vitiligo as "getting worse" in recent months.

## What Stable Vitiligo Means

Stable vitiligo means the disease has settled. There are no new patches, existing patches are not enlarging, and no new patches appear at sites of injury. Borders tend to be sharply defined. Stability is usually judged over a sustained period, often six to twelve months, though experts define it slightly differently, which is why careful records matter.

## Can It Switch?

Yes. Vitiligo can be active for a period, settle, and flare again later. Stress, illness, sunburn, skin injury, or other triggers have been linked to flares in some people, though in many there is no obvious cause. A person can also be stable at one site and active at another. Status is therefore reassessed over time rather than decided once.

## How We Judge Stability

There is no single test. We combine several sources of information. We take a careful history of when patches appeared and how they have changed. We examine the borders for signs of activity and use a Wood's lamp to see the true extent. Photographs at regular intervals are among the most reliable tools, since gradual change is hard to notice day to day, and we encourage patients to take their own photographs in the same lighting and position. We also look for features that suggest higher risk of spread.

## Treatment When Vitiligo Is Active

The priority is to calm the immune process and limit further loss. Options include narrowband UVB phototherapy, topical treatment, and in rapidly progressing cases a short, supervised course of oral medicine to help stop spread, with careful monitoring because of possible side effects. Protecting skin from sunburn and injury is also part of the plan. Surgery is avoided, and other skin procedures on active areas should be approached with care.

## Treatment When Vitiligo Is Stable

Once the disease is settled, attention shifts to restoring pigment. Topical creams and phototherapy can encourage repigmentation, often in combination. For patches that remain resistant, surgical repigmentation becomes a realistic option, since the transferred pigment cells are less likely to be attacked.

## Why Surgery Needs Stability

Surgical techniques for vitiligo depend on moving healthy pigment cells into a patch. If the disease is still active, the immune system can attack the transferred cells, or new patches can appear at the donor site. Waiting for a sustained period of stability gives the best chance of a lasting result, and sometimes a small test graft is done first to check how your skin behaves.

## What You Can Do

Protect your skin from sunburn and avoid repeated friction or injury over affected areas where possible. Do not have tattoos, aggressive peels, or other procedures on active areas without advice, because injury to the skin can provoke new patches in people with active disease. Take photographs of your patches every few weeks, in the same light, and bring them to your visits.

## Follow-Up

Assessment of stability is not a one-time event. We review at intervals, and your plan changes as your status changes. A patient whose vitiligo was active at the first visit may be stable a year later, and then become a candidate for different options.

If you are unsure whether your vitiligo is spreading, an assessment gives a clear answer and a plan matched to it.
