Stretch marks are very common, affecting most women in pregnancy and many teenagers and adults after fast growth or weight change. They are harmless, but many people want them to look less obvious.

## Why They Form

When skin stretches faster than it can adapt, the middle layer tears and collagen is rearranged. Hormones, genes and steroid exposure make it more likely.

## New and Old Marks

New marks (striae rubrae) are red or purple and respond best to treatment. Old marks (striae albae) are white and slightly depressed, and improve less.

## What Can Help

Prescription retinoid creams can improve new marks, but are avoided in pregnancy and breastfeeding. Vascular laser reduces redness. Fractional laser, microneedling and radiofrequency treatments can soften texture over several sessions. Expect modest results, typically a twenty to fifty percent improvement, not complete removal. Creams and oils sold for prevention have little evidence. See [stretch marks: what treatment can realistically do](/blog/stretch-marks-treatment-what-is-realistic).

## Reducing the Chance

Gain or lose weight gradually, avoid long-term steroid creams on skin folds, and moisturise for comfort.

## When to See a Doctor

See a dermatologist if stretch marks appear suddenly in large numbers without growth, weight change or pregnancy, particularly with other symptoms, since hormonal or steroid causes may be involved. See our [scar revision service](/services/scar-revision).
