Telogen effluvium is a common cause of diffuse hair shedding, occurring when a significant stressor -- physical or emotional -- pushes a larger-than-usual proportion of hair follicles into their resting (telogen) phase simultaneously. Because hair typically sheds two to three months after entering this resting phase, the shedding itself often appears well after the actual triggering event, which can make the connection less obvious to patients.

Common triggers include significant illness, surgery, high fever, [childbirth](/conditions/postpartum-hair-loss), major stress, [rapid weight loss](/blog/weight-loss-medicines-and-hair-shedding), or starting or stopping certain medications. In most cases, telogen effluvium is self-limiting and resolves within six to twelve months once the underlying trigger has passed, though [an evaluation](/services/hair-loss) helps confirm this diagnosis and rule out other causes of diffuse thinning.

If your shedding began after a high fever or illness, see [hair fall after fever](/blog/hair-fall-after-fever-dengue-typhoid-covid-recovery). After delivery, see [hair fall after pregnancy](/blog/hair-fall-after-pregnancy-timeline-nutrition-and-when-to-treat).

## What Is Happening

Telogen effluvium is a temporary increase in hair shedding. A stress to the body pushes many hairs from the growth phase into the resting phase together, and about two to three months later they all shed. It is the most common cause of sudden, diffuse shedding.

## Common Triggers

High fever and infections (dengue, typhoid, COVID-19), childbirth, major surgery, rapid weight loss or crash dieting, iron or protein deficiency, thyroid disease, new or stopped medicines and severe emotional stress.

## What It Looks Like

Large amounts of hair on the pillow and in the shower, with the scalp looking normal and an overall thinning, especially at the temples and crown. Total baldness does not occur.

## Diagnosis

A doctor reviews recent illness, diet and medicines, performs a pull test, and may check ferritin, thyroid tests, vitamin D and B12. Pattern hair loss and alopecia areata can coexist or mimic it. See [androgenetic alopecia](/conditions/androgenetic-alopecia).

## Treatment

Treat the cause and wait. Shedding usually settles in three to six months, and regrowth follows over six to twelve months. Eat enough protein and iron, avoid crash diets, and be gentle with hair. Medicines are rarely needed. See [hair fall after fever](/blog/hair-fall-after-fever-dengue-typhoid-covid-recovery) and [nutrition and hair loss](/blog/nutrition-and-hair-loss-iron-vitamin-d-protein).
