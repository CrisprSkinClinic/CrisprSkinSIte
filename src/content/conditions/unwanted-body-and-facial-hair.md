Body and facial hair vary widely between people, and much of that variation is normal. Family and ethnic background strongly influence how much hair someone has and where. Wanting less of it is a perfectly reasonable preference, and for most people there is no underlying medical problem.

## When It May Mean Something Else

In women, hair that becomes noticeably thicker or darker in a male-like pattern, such as on the face, chest, or abdomen, can sometimes reflect a hormonal condition, most commonly polycystic ovary syndrome. Clues include irregular periods, acne, and thinning of scalp hair. If hair growth is new, rapidly increasing, or comes with other changes, it deserves a medical evaluation, and treating the cause may matter more than removing the hair. This is called [hirsutism](/conditions/hirsutism), and the [hormonal skin service](/services/hormonal-skin-conditions) covers it. See also [hormonal acne](/conditions/hormonal-acne) and [female pattern hair loss](/conditions/female-pattern-hair-loss), which can coexist.

## Laser Hair Reduction

We use the word "reduction" deliberately. Laser treatment targets pigment in the hair follicle and works only on hair that is in its active growth phase, so several sessions are needed to catch hair as it cycles. Most people see a substantial and lasting reduction, with any regrowth typically finer and sparser, but it is not a promise of complete, permanent removal for everyone. See our article on [what to expect from laser hair reduction](/blog/laser-hair-reduction-expectations).

## Who Benefits Most

Laser works best on darker, coarser hair. It is less effective on very light, fine, or grey hair, regardless of the equipment, and results depend on both hair and skin type. We assess these at consultation and give a realistic session estimate rather than a generic number.

## Other Considerations

Shaving, waxing, and plucking can cause ingrown hairs and irritation, which laser reduction may lessen. People with darker skin need careful settings to reduce the risk of pigment change. Tell us about medicines, a history of keloid scarring, recent sun exposure, and any skin conditions in the area.

## What to Expect at Consultation

We examine the area, ask about the pattern and timing of hair growth, and assess your skin and hair type. If there are features suggesting a hormonal cause, we discuss appropriate evaluation first. Where laser is suitable, we explain the likely number of sessions and what results are realistic for you.
