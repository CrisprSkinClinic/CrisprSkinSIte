Surgery for vitiligo works by moving pigment-producing cells from healthy skin to a depigmented patch. It is considered only after the disease has been stable for a sustained period and medical treatment has not given enough repigmentation.

Different techniques suit different patches, depending on size, location, and skin type. Outcomes vary, and the donor and treated areas heal over several weeks. Sun protection and follow-up are part of the plan, and phototherapy is sometimes combined to improve the result. We discuss benefits, risks, and realistic expectations before any decision.
