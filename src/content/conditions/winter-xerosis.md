Skin is a barrier that holds in water. When the air is dry, that water evaporates faster, and the surface becomes rough and prone to cracks and itch. The medical term is xerosis.

Our [simple winter routine](/blog/winter-dry-skin-routine-that-works) is the first step for most people. If it does not help in two weeks, read about [eczema flares in winter](/blog/eczema-flares-in-winter).

## Why Dry Skin Itches

Dry skin has tiny cracks in its barrier, which let irritants in and activate nerve endings that sense itch. Scratching worsens the barrier, creating an itch-scratch cycle. The condition, called xerotic eczema or eczema craquelé when advanced, shows a pattern of fine cracks resembling a dry riverbed, mostly on the shins.

## Who Is Most Affected?

Older adults, whose oil production and skin water content fall with age. People with a history of eczema, asthma or hay fever. Those who bathe in hot water or wash frequently, healthcare workers and others who use sanitiser, and those who work in air-conditioned or dry environments. Some medicines, such as diuretics and statins, and conditions such as hypothyroidism and diabetes, also contribute.

## When Dry Skin Is a Sign of Something Else

Persistent, generalised itch without a rash, particularly with tiredness, weight loss, or yellow skin or eyes, calls for blood tests to look for thyroid, liver, kidney, iron or blood disorders. See a doctor if you have such symptoms.

## Treatment Layers

1. **Gentle cleansing:** soap-free wash, lukewarm water, short showers.
2. **Moisturise generously:** ointments or thick creams once to twice a day, plus after washing hands. See [how much to apply](/blog/how-much-topical-medicine-to-apply-fingertip-unit-guide).
3. **Humidify:** a humidifier or bowl of water near an air conditioner helps in dry rooms.
4. **Treat inflammation:** a short course of a mild steroid or non-steroid cream when skin is red and itchy.
5. **Manage itch:** keep nails short, cool the skin, and discuss antihistamines for sleep if needed.

## Protecting Skin Over the Season

Prepare in advance. Start a thicker moisturiser at the first sign of dryness, avoid long hot showers, wear cotton and wash new clothes before use. See [a winter dry skin routine that works](/blog/winter-dry-skin-routine-that-works).
