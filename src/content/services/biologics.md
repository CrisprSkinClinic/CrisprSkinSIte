Most skin conditions respond well to standard treatment. A smaller group has disease that remains active, widespread, or disabling despite good basic care. For these patients, newer targeted medicines, including biologics, have changed what is achievable.

Biologics are medicines that block one specific signal driving inflammation. They are used for selected patients with conditions such as moderate-to-severe psoriasis, severe atopic dermatitis, chronic hives that do not respond to antihistamines, and hidradenitis suppurativa. Which medicine, if any, is suitable depends on your condition, health, and circumstances, and availability and approved uses differ between medicines.

## What to Expect at Your Visit

We review your diagnosis and what has been tried so far, because the first step is often to optimise standard treatment rather than to escalate. If advanced therapy is appropriate, we discuss the options, arrange screening, and explain monitoring before anything is started.

## A Measured Approach

These treatments are powerful tools used within a longer plan. Our aim is the simplest treatment that keeps you well, with honest discussion of benefits, risks, cost, and alternatives, so that the plan is one you can sustain.

## Vaccines Before Treatment

Vaccines work best, and live vaccines are only safe, when given before you start. See our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination) and the [pre-treatment vaccine checklist](/blog/vaccines-before-immunosuppressants-biologics-checklist).

## Who Is Considered for a Biologic

People with moderate to severe psoriasis, atopic dermatitis, hidradenitis suppurativa or chronic hives who have not been controlled with standard treatments, or who cannot take them, may be considered. The decision weighs how much the disease affects your life, your health, other medicines, pregnancy plans and what has already been tried. Many people do well without a biologic, and we say so when that is the case.

## What We Check Before Starting

- **Infections:** tuberculosis screening (blood test and chest X-ray), hepatitis B and C and, where relevant, HIV.
- **General health:** blood counts, liver and kidney tests, and review of heart disease, cancers and neurological conditions.
- **Vaccines:** shingles, hepatitis B, pneumococcal, influenza and others, ideally before starting. See [vaccines before immunosuppressants](/blog/vaccines-before-immunosuppressants-biologics-checklist) and our [vaccination service](/services/vaccination).
- **Pregnancy plans** and breastfeeding.

## What Starting Looks Like

Most biologics are given as an injection under the skin, at home after training, every few weeks to months. Some are given by infusion. You are shown how to inject, how to store the medicine and what to do if you develop fever or an infection.

## How Fast They Work and What to Expect

Improvement often begins within weeks, and the full effect takes around three to four months. A good response means clearer skin, less itching and better sleep and quality of life. A minority do not respond to the first biologic, and a switch to another class is common and effective.

## Safety and Monitoring

Biologics lower specific parts of the immune response, so infections such as colds, skin infections and, rarely, serious infections can be more frequent. Injection-site reactions and headaches are common. Rare effects depend on the medicine. We see you regularly, check blood tests when needed and give you clear instructions on when to call.

## Stopping and Long-Term Plans

Skin disease often returns when a biologic is stopped, though some people can reduce the dose or spacing once well controlled. Decisions about stopping are made with you, not on your own.
