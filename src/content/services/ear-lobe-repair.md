Earlobes are delicate. A heavy earring, a snag on clothing, a pulled earring, or years of stretching can split the lobe, leaving a cleft or an enlarged hole. It is a common problem, and often an embarrassing one, but it is usually straightforward to repair.

## Types of Problem

A **complete split** means the lobe has torn right through, so the two halves are separate. A **partial split** leaves a deep notch or an enlarged, elongated piercing hole. **Stretched lobes** are elongated gradually by long-term wear of heavy earrings. Each is repaired slightly differently, and we describe them in more detail in [split earlobe](/conditions/split-earlobe).

## How the Repair Works

Under local anaesthetic, the lining that has grown along the edges of the tear is removed so that fresh edges can heal together. The lobe is then closed in layers with fine stitches, usually shaped so that the scar does not pull the lobe out of line. Depending on the type of tear, different closure techniques are used, and we choose one that gives a smooth lobe and, if you want, allows for re-piercing later. See [split earlobe repair explained](/blog/split-earlobe-repair-explained) for the techniques.

## What to Expect

The procedure takes a short time and you go home the same day. Stitches are removed after about a week or so, depending on the technique. The earlobe may be swollen and tender at first, and a small scar will remain, which usually fades to a fine line.

## Wearing Earrings Again

Healing needs time before the ear can be pierced again, commonly several weeks to a few months, and the new piercing is often placed slightly away from the scar line. We advise on timing and on choosing lighter earrings to protect the repair. See [recovering from earlobe repair and re-piercing](/blog/earlobe-repair-recovery-and-re-piercing).

## Who Needs Extra Care

People who form keloids or thick scars, which is more common in some skin types, need a careful plan, since the repair itself, like the original piercing, can trigger a scar. We discuss this in advance and plan aftercare accordingly. Those with bleeding problems, active skin infection, or other health conditions also need assessment first.

## Prevention

Lighter earrings, avoiding pulling on earrings, removing them for sports, and not wearing heavy earrings for extended periods all help. See [preventing torn and stretched earlobes](/blog/preventing-torn-and-stretched-earlobes).

## Booking

Online booking is for a consultation. The repair is planned and scheduled with the doctor after your examination.

## Realistic Expectations

Repair restores the lobe's continuity and shape, but a scar remains, and a repaired lobe is somewhat more vulnerable than an untouched one. We explain what to expect for your lobe before you decide.
