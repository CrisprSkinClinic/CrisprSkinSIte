Acne along the jawline, thick dark hair on the chin or abdomen, and a dark, velvety neck are three very different complaints. They are often treated separately, by different people, with limited success. Yet they frequently share a root cause: hormones, especially androgens, and insulin resistance, most often in the setting of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS).

That is why we treat them as one cluster. A patient who comes in about [hormonal acne](/conditions/hormonal-acne) is also asked about periods and hair. Someone with [hirsutism](/conditions/hirsutism) or [acanthosis nigricans](/conditions/acanthosis-nigricans) is asked about blood sugar, weight and family history. Treating the skin alone, without looking for the cause, is the commonest reason for relapse.

## Start With the Pattern

Our [PCOS skin and hair checklist](/blog/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs-checklist) shows how the signs fit together, and our page on [PCOS and the skin](/conditions/pcos-skin-and-hair-signs) explains what we look for.

## What We Do

We examine, order only the tests that are needed, explain the likely cause, and agree a plan. Skin and hair treatment starts at the same time as the work-up, so you do not wait. Where a wider hormonal or metabolic assessment is needed, we coordinate with your gynaecologist or physician.

## A Measured Approach

Hormonal treatments need follow-up, and results take months, because hair and skin renew slowly. We give you realistic timelines and review progress regularly.
