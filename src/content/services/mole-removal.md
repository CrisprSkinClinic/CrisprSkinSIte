Most moles are harmless, and many people live with them for life. People ask for removal because a mole catches on clothes or a razor, because of appearance, or because a doctor has advised it. A good removal starts with the right question: should this mole come off, and if so, how?

## Who Is Mole Removal For?

- **Cosmetic reasons:** a mole on the face, neck or another visible place that you would rather not have.
- **Irritation:** a mole that rubs on clothing, jewellery, a bra strap or a collar, or is cut while shaving.
- **Medical advice:** a mole that has changed, or that looks different from your other moles, should be examined and, where advised, removed and tested.

Removal is not advised for everyone. Some moles are best left alone, and some are better watched with photographs. We tell you which applies to yours.

## How Mole Removal Is Done

All methods use local anaesthetic, so you feel a small pinch at the start and no pain afterwards. The method depends on the mole.

- **Shave removal** suits raised, clearly benign moles. It is quick and leaves no stitches, but a faint mark remains, and a small number of moles can regrow from deeper cells.
- **Punch excision** removes a small round plug including the root, closed with one or two stitches. It suits small moles that are flat or deeper.
- **Elliptical excision** takes the whole mole with a margin, closed with fine stitches along the natural lines of the skin. It is the standard for larger moles and for any mole where complete removal is wanted.

We do not use lasers or burning methods to remove a mole that we have not first examined, because they destroy the tissue and prevent testing.

## Should a Removed Mole Be Tested?

We advise sending removed moles for testing wherever there is any doubt, and in every case where the mole was changing. Testing is the only way to be certain what a mole is. Read more in [do removed moles need a biopsy?](/blog/do-removed-moles-need-testing-histopathology).

## Recovery, Scarring and Aftercare

Most people return to normal activity the same day. Stitches, where used, come out after about a week on the face and two weeks on the body. Keep the wound clean, covered and dry for the first day or as advised, and protect it from the sun for several months, because new skin darkens easily. Every removal leaves some scar. Shave removals leave a flat, pale or slightly pink mark. Excisions leave a thin line that fades over six to twelve months. See [mole removal scarring and aftercare](/blog/mole-removal-scar-and-aftercare). Scars that are thick, raised or darker than expected can be treated; see [scar revision](/services/scar-revision).

## Risks

Infection, bleeding, a wider or raised scar, pigment change and regrowth of the mole are possible. Keloid-prone skin needs special care. We reduce these by choosing the right method, using clean technique, giving clear aftercare, and reviewing you after healing.

## Why Not Remove Moles at a Beauty Clinic or at Home?

Home remedies, creams and "mole removers" can burn the skin, scar and hide a dangerous mole. A removed mole that is not tested may miss a problem. See [why removing moles at home is risky](/blog/mole-removal-methods-shave-punch-excision-laser).

## What to Expect at Your Visit

We examine the mole and your other moles and skin, ask about changes, explain the options and the likely scar, and agree a plan. Many simple removals can be done at the same visit or soon after.
