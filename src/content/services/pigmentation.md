

## Not All Pigmentation Is the Same

- **Melasma:** symmetrical brown patches on the face, driven by sun, hormones and heat. See [melasma](/services/melasma).
- **Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation:** marks left after acne, eczema, bites or injury. See [post-acne marks versus scars](/blog/red-and-brown-marks-after-acne-vs-true-scars).
- **Sun spots and freckles (lentigines):** from cumulative sun exposure.
- **Dark under-eye circles and dark neck or folds:** often from genetics, friction, or insulin resistance. See [acanthosis nigricans](/conditions/acanthosis-nigricans).
- **Drug- or product-related darkening,** and, less often, conditions needing other tests.

A correct diagnosis comes first, because treatments differ.

## Treatment Foundations

1. **Daily sun and visible-light protection,** with a tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen. Without it, nothing else lasts. See [sunscreen for Indian skin](/blog/sunscreen-and-visible-light-indian-skin).
2. **Prescription brightening creams** used for a set period and then reduced.
3. **Gentle chemical peels** in a series. See [chemical peels for pigmentation on Indian skin](/blog/chemical-peels-for-pigmentation-and-melasma-on-indian-skin).
4. **Oral medicine** in selected patients, such as tranexamic acid for melasma, with a safety check.
5. **Laser** only in carefully chosen cases, since heat can worsen some pigment.

## What We Avoid

Strong over-the-counter bleaching mixtures, which can thin and irritate the skin, and aggressive treatments that cause darkening.

## What to Expect

Improvement takes months, and many types of pigmentation relapse with sun, hormones or irritation, so maintenance is part of the plan. A thirty to fifty percent lightening is a realistic aim in many people.

## When to See a Doctor

Any patch that is changing, bleeding, very dark or irregular should be examined. Pigmentation that is sudden, widespread or with other symptoms needs a medical review. See [post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation](/conditions/post-inflammatory-hyperpigmentation).
