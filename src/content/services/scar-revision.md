A scar is the skin's way of repairing a wound, and every wound that goes deeper than the top layer leaves one. Many fade to fine pale lines. Some heal thick, raised, wide or sunken, and these can be improved. No treatment erases a scar completely, but most can be made flatter, softer, smoother and less noticeable.

## Know Your Scar Type

- **Flat, pale or slightly wide scars,** the usual result of cuts and surgery.
- **Hypertrophic scars:** raised, red and firm, but staying within the original wound. They often improve by themselves over a year or two.
- **Keloids:** raised scars that grow beyond the original wound and may keep growing. They are more common in darker skin and on the chest, shoulders and earlobes.
- **Atrophic (sunken) scars,** including most acne scars. See [acne scar treatment](/services/acne-scars).
- **Stretch marks (striae):** lines that form when skin stretches fast, in pregnancy, growth spurts or weight change.

Read more in [scar types explained](/blog/scar-types-hypertrophic-keloid-and-atrophic-explained).

## What Treatment Involves

The right treatment depends on the type.

- **New raised scars:** silicone gel or sheets, pressure where possible, sun protection and, if needed, steroid injections.
- **Hypertrophic and keloid scars:** steroid injections every few weeks, sometimes combined with other medicines, laser or freezing. Surgery on keloids needs great care, because cutting a keloid can cause a larger one. See [keloid treatment: what works](/blog/keloid-treatment-what-works-and-why-keloids-return).
- **Sunken scars:** subcision, microneedling, peels and laser, and sometimes fillers. See [scar revision options compared](/blog/scar-revision-options-compared).
- **Wide or badly placed scars:** surgical revision, once the scar has matured.
- **Dark or red scars:** sun protection, gentle lightening and laser as suitable.

## When to Treat

New raised scars respond best to early treatment, in the first weeks to months. Mature flat scars are best revised after twelve months, when the scar has settled. We tell you when to wait.

## Realistic Results

Expect improvement, not disappearance. A common finding is a thirty to seventy percent improvement in appearance, depending on type, age, site and skin. Several sessions are usual, and some scars need maintenance.

## Caring for a New Scar

Keep it clean and moisturised, protect it from the sun, avoid picking, and use silicone as advised. See [care of a surgical scar in the first six months](/blog/surgical-scar-care-first-six-months).

## Risks

Treatment can cause pigment change, thinning of the skin from steroid injections, recurrence of keloids and, with surgery, a scar of the same or worse quality. We choose conservative steps first and explain the risks before starting.

## What to Expect at Your Visit

We examine the scar, ask about its history, explain the type and the options, and set a plan with time frames. We take photographs so that progress can be judged fairly.
