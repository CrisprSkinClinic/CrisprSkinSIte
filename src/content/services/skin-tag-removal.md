Skin tags are small, soft, skin-coloured or brown growths that hang from the skin on a thin stalk. They are harmless, and very common, especially in skin folds such as the neck, underarms, groin and under the breasts. They are not contagious and do not turn into cancer. People ask for removal because of rubbing, catching, bleeding or appearance.

## Why Skin Tags Appear

Friction and skin folds play a part, and tags often run in families. Weight gain, pregnancy and insulin resistance make them more likely, and a sudden crop of tags, especially with a dark, velvety neck, can be an early sign of high insulin. See [why skin tags appear and whether they grow back](/blog/why-skin-tags-appear-and-do-they-come-back) and [acanthosis nigricans](/conditions/acanthosis-nigricans).

## How Skin Tags Are Removed

Removal takes a few minutes. We choose the method by size, number and site:

- **Snip excision:** the quickest for tags with a thin stalk. A numbing cream or a tiny injection is used.
- **Cautery:** seals the base as it removes the tag, useful for small tags and those near the eyelid, where bleeding must be minimal.
- **Freezing:** a simple option for small tags; the tag darkens and falls off. It can leave a pale or dark mark, so we do not use it for every site.

Large tags, tags near the eyes, and tags in crowded areas are treated carefully, sometimes in more than one sitting.

## Recovery and Aftercare

A small scab forms and drops off in a week or so. Keep the area clean and dry, avoid picking, and apply the ointment we give you. Most people return to normal activity straight away. The skin may stay pink or slightly paler or darker for some weeks.

## Will Skin Tags Come Back?

A removed tag does not grow back, but new tags can appear nearby or elsewhere, especially if the cause persists. Keeping weight in a healthy range and treating insulin resistance lowers the chance.

## Why Not Remove Them at Home?

Tying with thread, cutting with nail clippers and home "removal" creams cause bleeding, infection, scarring and burns, and can remove a growth that was not a skin tag at all. See [skin tag removal at home: the risks](/blog/skin-tag-removal-at-home-risks).

## When to See a Doctor First

See a dermatologist before removal if a growth is dark, hard, irregular, bleeding, growing quickly or painful, or if you have dozens of tags. Every removal we do is checked, and anything unusual is sent for testing.

## What to Expect at Your Visit

We examine the tags and your skin, explain the method, the numbing and the aftercare, and in most cases remove them at the same visit.
