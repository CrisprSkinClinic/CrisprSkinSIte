Xanthelasma are soft, yellowish patches that appear on or around the eyelids, most often near the inner corner. They are collections of fat (cholesterol) deposited in the skin. They are not cancerous, do not harm the eye or vision, and are not painful, but they tend to persist and slowly grow, and many people want them treated for appearance.

## Why They Deserve a Medical Check

Xanthelasma can be a sign of raised cholesterol or another lipid disorder, though many people with them have normal blood tests. For that reason, we usually recommend a lipid profile, and we review risk factors for heart disease. Removing the patches without checking the cause misses the part that matters most for your health. See [xanthelasma and cholesterol](/blog/xanthelasma-what-it-is-and-the-cholesterol-link).

## Our Three Treatment Options

No single option suits everyone. The choice depends on the size, depth, and position of the patches, your skin type, and how much healing time you can accept.

**TCA touch.** A small amount of trichloroacetic acid is applied precisely to the surface of the patch with a fine applicator, causing the affected layer to frost, darken, and peel away over days. It suits small, flat, superficial patches. It is quick and involves no cutting, but it often needs repeating every few weeks, and the strength and technique must be controlled carefully on thin eyelid skin to avoid scarring or pigment change.

**Surgical excision.** The patch is cut out under local anaesthetic and the skin is closed with fine stitches. It suits larger or deeper patches and tends to give more lasting results, but it leaves a fine scar and needs skill near the eyelid so that the lid is not pulled.

**Radiofrequency ablation (RFA).** Radiofrequency energy is used to remove the patch layer by layer under local anaesthetic, with good control over depth. It suits small to moderate patches and offers a bloodless, stitch-free option, with a short period of redness and crusting while it heals.

We compare these in more detail in [xanthelasma treatment options compared](/blog/xanthelasma-treatment-options-compared).

## Risks to Understand

Eyelid skin is thin, so every method needs care. Possible problems include scarring, lightening or darkening of the skin, and, with larger excisions, pulling of the eyelid. Recurrence is common after any method, so we discuss this honestly. See [recurrence and aftercare](/blog/xanthelasma-recurrence-and-aftercare).

## What to Expect at Your Visit

We examine the patches, ask about your family history and general health, and arrange blood tests if you have not had them. We explain which of the three methods suits you, the likely number of sessions, healing, and the chance of return, and we tell you when it is sensible to wait or to treat the cholesterol first.

## Booking

Online booking is for a consultation. The procedure itself, whether a TCA touch, excision, or RFA, is chosen and scheduled with the doctor after your examination and any blood tests.

## Realistic Expectations

Treatment can remove the patches well, but new ones can form, especially if the underlying cause is not addressed. Treating raised cholesterol and looking after your general health may reduce the risk of recurrence, and we coordinate that with your doctor where needed.
